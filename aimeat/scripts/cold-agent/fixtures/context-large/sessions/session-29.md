> ### Chill locks and the length of the pickup window
>
> #### Summary
>
> Chill locks look like a side effect of *when* the window closes rather than *how long* members need. With every pod filled by 07:00 on Thursday, a 48-hour window closes on Saturday morning, the one part of the week when an orphan box cannot leave the pod. Shortening the window moves the close into Friday, where the existing weekday orphan run can reach it. On the figures available, a 30-hour window looks like the best balance. Details and caveats follow.
>
> #### 1. How a chill lock forms
>
> 1. A box goes into a chill cell on Thursday's dawn run.
> 2. It is not collected before the window closes and becomes an orphan box.
> 3. The food bank takes nothing at weekends and caretakers will not hold boxes, so the box stays in the cell until the orphan run reaches that pod.
> 4. Backroom keeps the cell marked as held until the box is scanned out.
> 5. If the orphan run misses the pod, or the box is removed without a scan, the cell is still held when the next Thursday's drop arrives. The chill box assigned to it has to go elsewhere, in practice into a dry cell with an ice brick.
>
> Step 5 is rare in absolute terms but expensive. Each K-16 has only 4 chill cells, so one lock removes a quarter of that pod's chill capacity for the drop. Across 9 pods there are 36 chill cells per drop against 108 dry cells; the chill cells are the scarce resource.
>
> #### 2. Why the 48-hour window makes this worse
>
> Window length affects chill locks indirectly, through the close time:
>
> | Window | Close (for a 06:00 drop) | Falls on | Same-day weekday orphan run possible? | Earliest the cell is free |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 48 h | Saturday 06:00 | Weekend | No | Monday |
> | 36 h | Friday 18:00 | Friday evening | Only with a late handover | Friday evening, else Monday |
> | 30 h | Friday 12:00 | Friday midday | Yes | Friday afternoon |
> | 24 h | Friday 06:00 | Friday morning | Yes | Friday morning |
>
> Drop times spread across the dawn run, so actual close times shift by an hour or two depending on where a pod sits in the loop. The pattern holds regardless: anything up to roughly 30 hours closes inside a normal working Friday, and anything over roughly 41 hours runs into the weekend.
>
> Under 48 hours, every orphan box waits at least two nights, plus however long the orphan run then needs to work through all pods. That stretches the period in which a missed box can survive into the next drop. It also means the food bank receives produce that left the grower four to five days earlier rather than one to two.
>
> #### 3. What a shorter window costs
>
> The 48-hour window was introduced on 28 April for a specific reason: members on shift work were missing their boxes. That reason has not gone away, and the current 3 % orphan rate is partly a result of the longer window.
>
> The key question is how many collections currently happen late in the window. I do not have the front-pick timestamps, so the following is a model, not a measurement:
>
> - Boxes per week: roughly 107 dropped, about 104 collected.
> - Current orphan rate: 3 %, so around 3 orphan boxes a week.
> - Assumed share of collections after hour 30: 2–4 %.
> - Assumed share after hour 36: 1–2 %.
>
> | Window | Modelled orphan rate | Modelled orphan boxes / week | Modelled pickup rate |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 48 h | 3 % | ~3 | ~97 % |
> | 36 h | 4–5 % | ~4–5 | ~95–96 % |
> | 30 h | 5–7 % | ~5–7 | ~93–95 % |
> | 24 h | 8–12 % | ~9–13 | ~88–92 % |
>
> Some late collectors would adjust to a shorter window, so these are probably upper bounds. What matters is the distance from the kill criteria: even the 30-hour case stays well clear of an 85 % pickup rate. The 24-hour case comes uncomfortably close in a bad week and would hit shift workers hardest, so I would not consider it.
>
> Financially, an orphan box is not lost revenue: the card on file is charged on the drop day and the grower is paid either way. The cost is member goodwill, the risk of cancellations, and the driving time of the orphan run.
>
> #### 4. Recommendation: 30 hours
>
> 1. **It closes on a weekday.** A Friday-midday close lets the orphan run clear every orphan box the same afternoon. No cell carries a box over the weekend, and the chance of a lock reaching Thursday falls close to zero.
> 2. **It still covers a full day and a night.** A member on a Thursday night shift has Thursday daytime and Friday morning. A member on a Thursday day shift has Thursday evening and Friday morning.
> 3. **The orphan rate has headroom.** At 3 %, a few extra orphan boxes a week do not threaten the kill criteria.
> 4. **Fresher food for the food bank.** Produce reaches them roughly two days earlier than now.
>
> The main argument against 30 hours is the member who collects on Friday afternoon or evening, for example someone on a run of late shifts. A 36-hour window keeps those members, but it closes after normal working hours on Friday. It therefore only fixes chill locks if the food bank can accept boxes on a Friday evening. If they can, 36 hours is a reasonable alternative and the gap between the two options narrows considerably.
>
> #### 5. If the window is shortened, alongside it
>
> 1. **State the close time, not the length.** "Friday 12:00" is easier to remember than "30 hours". Put it in the drop SMS that carries the wake code and next to the QR stub in Picker.
> 2. **Send one reminder** a few hours before close to anyone who has not collected.
> 3. **Explain the change.** The 48-hour window was a visible concession to members; reversing it without a reason will read as a cut.
> 4. **Fix the scan-out gap separately.** Some locks appear to come from boxes that were removed but never scanned out. That is a Backroom issue, and no window length will solve it.
> 5. **Review after four box weeks**, comparing orphan rate and chill lock incidents before and after the change.
>
> #### 6. What I could not check
>
> - The distribution of collection times within the window. This is the single figure most likely to change the recommendation; Backroom should be able to produce it from front-pick logs.
> - The food bank's intake hours on Fridays.
> - Whether chill demand differs by pod. A lock at a pod that rarely fills all 4 chill cells matters less than one at a pod that fills them every week.
> - Weather. In hot weeks, a box that misses its chill cell is a quality problem, not just an inconvenience. The cold chain log may show how long an ice brick keeps a dry cell acceptably cool.
