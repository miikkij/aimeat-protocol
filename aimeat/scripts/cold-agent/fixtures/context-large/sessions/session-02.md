# Larder · session 2 · 20 January 2026 · Naming and the first grower search

We met at the Garage again. Sleet all morning, the kind that comes sideways off the harbour, so we kept the side door shut and ran the little heater, which made the place smell of warm dust. Rasmus brought coffee in the big thermos and a bag of cinnamon buns that were gone by half past ten. We had a loose list of four things to get through: a name, how to find a grower, how to start talking to buildings, and how the two of us actually work week to week. We got through all four, though only one of them ended in a decision.

## The name

We have been calling this "the box thing" in messages and "the project" in front of other people, and it was starting to cause small problems. Rasmus needs a name for the repo, for the domain, and eventually for whatever goes on the front of a pod. I need something to say on the phone to a grower or an HA chair that doesn't sound like two people who haven't thought it through.

We each brought a few candidates. Mine were mostly descriptive and, read out loud in the Garage, mostly bad. "Lobbybox" says exactly what it is, but it sounds like a parcel company and puts the emphasis on the lobby rather than the food. "Veggie Locker" made Rasmus think of a gym changing room, and once he said it I couldn't unhear it. "Doorstep" is simply wrong, because the whole point is that nobody comes to your door. Rasmus's list was shorter and more abstract: a couple of made-up words that would be easy to get domains for and impossible to remember. He crossed them out himself before I said anything.

"Larder" came from me, almost as an aside. My grandmother had one, a narrow cool room off the kitchen with a stone shelf and a little vent to the outside, where the potatoes and the jam and the Sunday leftovers lived. I said a pod is more or less that for a building: a cool place downstairs where your food waits until you want it. Rasmus went quiet for a second and then said "Larder, Ledger, Picker, Field," and laughed because it sat so comfortably next to the names he has already given his own pieces.

The reasons we talked through, roughly in the order they came up:

- It's a real word, not an invented brand. People already know what a larder is, or can guess, and the guess is right: somewhere you keep food.
- It describes what happens in the pod, not the technology. Nobody wants to tell a neighbour "my vegetables are in the locker system."
- It works in a sentence. "It's in the Larder" and "did you get your Larder box" both sound like things a person would actually say in a lift.
- It's short. It will fit on a pod door and on a phone screen without being shrunk.
- It's a little old-fashioned, which we both think is right for the bet. We're asking people to trust a new habit; a homely word helps.

Rasmus, being Rasmus, looked it up on his phone to make sure there wasn't anything embarrassing in it. There are a few other meanings. In a big restaurant kitchen the larder is the cold section, where things get prepped and kept chilled, which we decided was fine and even quite fitting. There is also the bird sense: a shrike keeps a "larder" of insects and small animals spiked on thorns. Rasmus read that one out with some relish. We agreed that nobody in a lobby is going to think of shrikes, and that if they do it will at least be memorable.

**Decision: the product is called Larder.** Rasmus will check what's free for domains and handles before we say it to anyone outside the two of us, but that's a check, not a reopening. If the obvious domain is taken we use a longer one; we don't change the name.

We did not talk about a logo, colours, or a tagline, or rather we started to and then stopped, which is where the next word came from.

## The parking lot

About ten minutes after we'd settled on Larder, I was sketching a little drawing of a jar on the whiteboard and Rasmus was suggesting a green that I thought looked like hospital scrubs. He said, "Put it on the parking lot," which is apparently what they said at his last job when a meeting drifted. It stuck immediately, partly because we are sitting in a garage with an actual parking area outside the roller door, and the joke made itself. So the parking lot is now a section at the bottom of our shared doc: ideas we like, or half like, that we are deliberately not dealing with yet.

What went on it today:

- Logo and colours (not the green).
- Recipe cards in boxes. I like it; Rasmus thinks nobody reads them. Parked.
- A line on the pod front explaining what Larder is, for people walking past who aren't members. We'll need words for that at some point, but not this week.
- Rasmus's idea of a tiny "pod diary" where he'd note odd things he sees in the data. Interesting, but there's no data yet.

The rule we agreed on, informally, is that putting something on the parking lot isn't saying no. It's saying not now. We'll glance at it at the end of each session and see if anything has become urgent.

## Box week

This one came out of Rasmus's half of the morning. He has started the bones of the Ledger and wanted to know what a box "belongs to." Not which member and which grower, that part's obvious, but which week. We haven't chosen a delivery day, and he didn't want the data to depend on one. If the drop day changes later, or if one grower delivers on a different day from another, he doesn't want to rewrite everything.

So he proposed that every box belongs to a box week, Monday to Sunday, whatever day it actually turns up in a compartment. A box is "the box for that box week." I asked a few questions to make sure I understood: if a box gets dropped on a Thursday and collected on a Saturday, it's still the same box week? Yes. If something goes wrong and a box doesn't happen, the gap belongs to that box week too? Yes, that's the point; the week exists whether or not the box does. I liked that it gives me a clean way to talk to growers as well: "how many boxes can you do per box week" is a simpler question than one tied to particular days.

Rasmus was a bit sheepish that it sounds obvious. It is obvious, but writing it down means we'll both use it the same way, and I can already hear myself saying it.

## Red list

My coinage, and a very practical one. When we sat down I'd written three things at the very top of the doc, in red, because they were the things stopping us from moving this week. Rasmus started calling it "the red list" within about a minute and the colour has now become the name. The idea is that it's short, it's only about this week, and anything on it is blocking actual work, not just undone. If something isn't blocking, it belongs on a normal to-do list, or on the parking lot.

This week's red list, as of the end of the session:

- **No grower.** Nothing about boxes is real until someone grows the vegetables. Stays red.
- **No way into the buildings yet.** We know the buildings, we don't know the people. Stays red until the first lobby walk is booked.
- ~~Ledger needs a unit of time for boxes~~ — cleared today by box week.

It felt good to strike one out before lunch.

## The first grower search

This was the longest part of the morning and the least conclusive, which I expected. January is the worst possible time to go looking for vegetables. Nothing is growing, the market stalls in town are down to a handful of hardy regulars selling stored roots and apples, and most small farms are either resting or doing their repairs and paperwork. On the other hand, this is exactly when growers have time to talk, and when they're thinking about the season ahead. I'd rather call someone now, when they can sit down with a coffee, than in June when they're in the field from five in the morning.

I went through the ways I can think of to find people:

- The few stalls still at the winter market. I'll go on Saturday and just talk to whoever is there. Even if they're not right for us, they'll know who is.
- The farm shops and roadside stands on the routes out of Lakeport. Most are shut in winter, but they have phone numbers on their signs and some have websites.
- The regional growers' listings online, which are patchy and out of date but better than nothing.
- Asking around: friends, the people I used to know from the community garden, anyone who's ever mentioned "a farm my cousin runs."

Rasmus asked what we're actually looking for, and we talked about it for a while without pinning anything down, because I don't want to write a job description for a grower before I've spoken to one. The things that came up: somewhere close enough to Lakeport that getting the boxes to the last metre isn't a whole day's driving; someone who can be steady from box week to box week rather than having a glut one week and nothing the next; and, honestly, someone who finds the idea interesting rather than strange. A grower who thinks the bet is silly isn't going to put up with our early mistakes.

We also talked about whether the first grower needs to be the only grower, or whether we'd start with more than one. We didn't settle it. Rasmus's instinct is that one is simpler for the software; mine is that one is fragile. Both are true. We left it alone.

The question for the notes, and for next week: **who will be the first grower?** I don't know yet. I have no names, only ways of finding names. That's the job for this week.

## Lobby walks

The other red item. We have the rough count from last week, about 40 apartment buildings within 3 km of the Garage, but a count on a map isn't a relationship with anyone. At some point someone has to go into a building, find whoever on the HA board is willing to talk, and stand in the lobby looking at the floor.

I said something like "I'll just go and walk the lobbies," and by the end of the sentence Rasmus had turned it into a noun: a lobby walk. It means going to one building, meeting its HA (or whoever they send), and looking for a possible lobby slot. It's not a sales visit and it's not a survey, it's a walk. I like that the word keeps it modest. We don't have anything to sell yet, really, apart from the bet and a name we picked this morning.

What we talked about for the first few:

- Start close to the Garage. If anything ever comes of it, closer is easier, and it's less awkward to walk in when I can honestly say I live nearby.
- Bring a tape measure and take photos of any corner that might work as a lobby slot, with permission. Rasmus wants rough dimensions so he can stop guessing.
- Bring something to leave behind: one printed page saying what Larder is, with my number. Rasmus will make a plain version; the nice version is on the parking lot.
- Don't promise anything. No dates, no money, no "we'll definitely be here." We're asking questions, not making deals.
- Ask how their lobby actually gets used: who comes through, when it's busy, whether things get left there, who has keys. I'm curious and slightly worried about what we'll hear.

Rasmus said he'd come on the first one or two, mostly so he can see a lobby with his own eyes instead of only through my photos. After that I expect it'll mostly be me.

A side note I wrote while we were talking: I've been saying "customers" in my head and in my own notes, and Rasmus pointed out, gently, that he's been writing "member" everywhere in the Ledger. When I'm talking to an HA I'll try to say members. I'll probably slip.

## How we work

The last half hour was about rhythm, which sounds dull but was useful. Two sessions in, we're both already noticing that the work splits naturally. Rasmus disappears into code for days at a time, I'm on the phone or out walking, and if we don't stop to compare notes we'll end up building two slightly different Larders.

When Rasmus put a recurring slot in our calendars after the first session he called it "sit-down," and I've been calling it "the Tuesday thing." Somewhere today the two merged into the Tuesday sit-down, and that's what it is now: our weekly working session, the two of us, here at the Garage unless one of us is ill or away. These notes are from it, and I'll keep writing them.

The shape we're going to try, without making a ceremony of it:

- Start with the red list: what's blocking us, what got unblocked since last time.
- Then whatever's most alive that week; today that was the name and the grower search.
- End with a glance at the parking lot, to see if anything there has become urgent.
- I write the notes the same day and put them in the shared folder so Rasmus can correct anything I got wrong about the software.

Rasmus asked half-jokingly whether we need a rule about what happens if a Tuesday doesn't work. We agreed we'd rather not have rules until we've broken one, and put it on the parking lot, mostly to make the point.

The sleet had turned to rain by the time we finished. Rasmus went off to look at domains; I stayed and made a list of phone numbers.
