# Tidewater (board game) · playtest 2 · 19 April 2026 · Waves and pilots

Pauli came over around one, Venla a bit later because the bus was running on a Sunday timetable. Grey, windy day, which felt fitting. We had coffee and the last of the pulla Venla brought, and I laid out the new board before anyone could start talking about something else. The aim today was to try the event system properly for the first time, and the fog bank tiles, which in the last playtest mostly sat there being annoying.

We played three games in the afternoon: two with all three of us, and one short two-player game between Pauli and me while Venla sketched. The two-player game was mainly to check that the events don't fall apart at the low end of 2 to 4 players. They didn't, though it was close in one place (below).

## The timer gets a name

The first thing that happened was a naming problem. I've been calling the strip along the board edge the "tide track," which is accurate but dull. It advances one step at the end of every loop, and when it's full the game ends. About twenty minutes into the first game, Venla moved the marker forward and said, "there goes the heartbeat again." She meant that it ticks along steadily underneath everything else. Pauli picked it up straight away, and by the second game all three of us were saying "heartbeat." I'm keeping "tide track" in the rules text for now, because it says what the thing is. At the table, though, it's the heartbeat, and the two mean the same thing. Whichever name we use, it's also how we hit the 45 minute target. If games run long, the heartbeat is the lever, not the number of loops people feel like playing.

## Events: waves and the storm deck

The event cards were the main thing to test. Before today I hadn't settled how many to reveal at once. In the first game I flipped one card at a time, whenever the heartbeat moved. It was flat. One card a loop is easy to plan around, and nobody ever felt pushed. Pauli said it was like weather in a game where you can always see out the window.

In the second game we tried revealing a handful together. Pauli kept saying "draw from the storm deck" instead of "the event deck," and the name stuck because it's shorter and sounds better. Then, when three cards came up at once and two of them hit the same corner of the board, Venla said "that's a whole wave." So "wave" now means the set of event cards that come up together.

**Decision: a wave is three event cards from the storm deck.**

Why three:
- Two felt too much like one card a loop. Players could still plan around them without effort.
- With three, the cards start to interact. One closes a lane, another pushes a pod into the next lane, and suddenly a drop someone planned two turns ago is in trouble. That's the feeling I want.
- We tried four once, out of curiosity. It was too much to read at once, and Pauli spent a full minute working out which cards cancelled each other. Three can be read at a glance.
- Three also matches the pod and the three-card hand limit I already use elsewhere, so it's one less odd number to remember. Pauli joked that the whole game is secretly about the number three. Probably true.

When a wave lands it's easy to forget which card hit where, especially when two cards hit nearby harbour tiles. Venla had a bag of small grey plastic markers from some other game. She put one down on each spot that got hit and drew a little gull on the back of an old cargo card to label the pile. Pauli started calling them "gull tokens" because "the gulls show where the storm went." So a gull token marks where an event card struck. They clear when the next wave comes. We didn't formally decide that, it's just what we did today. Venla wants to draw proper gulls for them. I said yes before she'd finished the sentence.

## Pilots

The fog bank problem from last time: nobody ever goes near them, so they work as walls and nothing more. I brought a new piece to try, a small white ship that can move through a fog bank when ordinary ships can't. I'd been calling it "the fog ship" in my notebook. Pauli said that sounded like a ghost story and asked if it was more like a harbour pilot, the boat that guides big ships through tricky water. Yes, exactly that. So it's a "pilot" now.

We tried two ways of handing them out. In the first game there were two pilots on the board, open to whoever reached them first. That turned into a race. Venla got one on the second loop, and then she more or less owned the fog banks for the rest of the game. Not fun for Pauli or me. In the second game everyone had their own.

**Decision: each player has one pilot.**

The reasons:
- A shared pilot made one player strong and the rest resentful, as above.
- With one each, the fog banks become a real choice for everybody, not just a privilege for one person.
- It's easy to explain. One ship, one pilot, both on your captain sheet.
- It scales. With 2 to 4 players there's no need to change the count of shared pieces per player count, which I'd rather avoid.

What the pilot can actually do beyond fog banks is not settled. Can it carry cargo? Can it escort your main ship? We played it today as fog-movement only, no cargo, and it felt a bit thin. I've parked that. Venla half-suggested the pilot could clear a gull token. That's interesting, but it ties two new systems together before either is stable, so it's on the parking lot too.

## The reef

The last new thing was the ring of tiles around the hub. In the first game they were just ordinary sea. Everyone sailed straight through to the hub, and the hub was rammed every loop. Pauli, looking at the jagged edge I'd drawn on the paper version, said "so that's the reef, then." Fair enough: the reef is that ring around the hub, and passing it damages ships.

The question was what "damage" means. Options we talked about:
- Lose a turn. Too swingy, and it made the two-player game miserable.
- Lose one cargo card. We tried it. People shrugged and went through anyway, so it barely changed anything.
- Lose two cargo cards.
- Damage marks on the captain sheet. Interesting, but it's a whole new system, and I don't want more systems this week.

**Decision: passing the reef costs two cargo cards.**

The reasoning: with one card nobody cared, and the hub stayed as crowded as before. With two, people actually stopped and thought. Do I go around via the outer harbour tiles, or take the hit and make my drop at the hub? In the second three-player game Pauli took the long way twice and Venla went through once. That's the kind of split I was hoping for. The pilot also matters more if the reef bites, because routes through the fog bank become a way to avoid the crowded centre.

That said, I'm not sure two is right in the long run. In the two-player game, Pauli went through the reef early with a nearly empty hold, lost both cards, and basically had nothing to do for two loops. He said it felt like a punishment for being curious. Venla thought it was fine and that you just learn not to do that. I can see both sides.

**Open: is the reef too punishing?** Not deciding today. I need more games, ideally with someone who hasn't played before.

## Other bits

- The heartbeat felt a little long in the first game. It came in well over 45 minutes, partly because we kept stopping to talk, so I'm not drawing conclusions yet.
- Pauli found a typo on one of the captain sheets (“hrabour”). Embarrassing.
- Venla wants the storm deck cards to have a clear "where it hits" icon so gull tokens are quicker to place.
- The dawn run worked as usual. Nobody had anything to say about it today.
- Parking lot: pilot carrying cargo; pilot interacting with gull tokens; reef damage marks on the captain sheet instead of cards.

Pauli left around six. Venla stayed and drew three versions of a gull while I packed up.
