# Larder · session 40 · 13 October 2026 · Planning Season 2

Rain all morning, the sideways kind that comes off the harbour, and the Garage door leaked along the bottom again, so we sat with our feet up on empty crates. The coffee machine gave up halfway through the first pot and we finished on Rasmus's thermos. Maija was with us for the whole session, not just the grower part, and that suited the agenda. This was the first sit-down that was entirely about Season 2 rather than the pilot.

The pilot is done. The extension ran out on 30 September, and Season 2 starts on 2 November with the same 9 pods. There is no third wave this year. I said that out loud at the start so that nobody (me) drifts into planning one by accident.

Before anything else I read out the end-of-pilot figures, so we stop quoting them slightly differently to each other:

- best week: 152 boxes picked up
- pickup rate in September: 96 %
- members at the end: 214

Rasmus still winces at 152 against the 160 we were aiming for. I think 152 is fine given where we were in March. Maija, who wasn't around in March, said it looks fine from the outside too. We left it there.

## Cold start

Rasmus had a page of worries on the back of an old loop sheet with "COLD START" written across the top. The name stuck before the first coffee was finished.

Rasmus's point is that all through the pilot, people joined in a trickle. Wave 1 came in pod by pod, wave 2 came in building by building over the summer, and there were a handful of signups in any given week. Every problem arrived on its own, and we had time to look at it.

The first week of Season 2 is different, because every member starts at once:

- every card on file is charged on the same drop days
- every grower sends their first Season 2 box sheet in the same few days
- every member opens a compartment from Picker in the same week
- whatever is quietly wrong with nine pods shows up on the Pulse at the same time

Rasmus compared it to switching a server back on after it has been off for a while. The first minutes are when it falls over, because everything asks for something at the same moment. Maija said, "so cold start week is the week we find out what we forgot." After that all three of us were saying "cold start" as if we'd always had the word.

We went through Rasmus's page item by item:

- **Cards.** Rasmus wants Backroom to flag any card on file that runs out before the first drop day, so we can ask those members now and not on the morning itself. A failed charge at six in the morning means a box in a compartment that nobody has paid for. I don't want to be deciding what to do about those from the driver's seat.
- **Front-pick.** For a lot of members, cold start week will be their first front-pick with the QR stub rather than the wake code. Some will be on the Android build for the first time as well. Rasmus is fairly confident the QR stub holds up. I'm less sure about people standing in a lobby with one bar of signal. The wake code is still there as the fallback, but if both feel slow, people walk away and come back later, and later can be after the window closes. I'll keep my phone on and free for the whole first drop morning. Maija will cover the second drop day the same way.
- **Growers.** Maija, from the grower desk, will call all eight growers in the week before to go through their first box sheet with them. Aino goes first, because the others tend to copy the way Ridgeback's sheets are laid out. Maija mentioned that on the last call Juho said the cabinet "will be hungry after the break." I wrote it down because it's nice, not because it means anything.
- **Pack sheet.** The family boxes are still hub-packed here, and the cold start pack sheet will be the longest we've had, since everyone is on it at once. Rasmus will generate it a day early. That way I can check it against the box sheets the evening before, not at the pack line with the van idling outside.
- **Photo proofs.** Rasmus will go through the photo proofs from both cold start drop days on the same mornings, pod by pod. The aim is to catch any ghost drop or double drop before a member finds it for us.
- **The sweep.** 2 November is a Monday, so the sweep lands the day before the first drop. That's luck rather than planning, but we'll take it: every pod checked and wiped the day before cold start.

Rasmus also wondered aloud whether we should stagger Season 2 starts by building, to take the edge off. We spent five minutes on it and let it go. Season 2 starts on 2 November, and starting it in pieces would just give us several small cold starts instead of one big one. I'd rather have one bad week we're ready for than four mediocre ones we aren't.

## Two runs or one

Delivery day was meant to be a quick check, and it wasn't.

The twin run stays on Tuesdays and Fridays as it stands. It's those two days and not Monday because Monday belongs to the sweep, and nobody wanted to reopen that. What Rasmus did reopen was the bigger question: **does Season 2 keep two dawn runs a week at all?**

Rasmus's case is cost. The twin run means:

- two van days a week
- the cargo bike leg twice a week
- two early mornings for whoever is driving

The CPD of a run that goes out half full doesn't get better just because the pods like being filled twice. Rasmus also pointed out that Season 2 is when the money we spend stops being "pilot money" in our heads. That's fair, even if that question about Hanna's investment is still sitting open and isn't one for today.

My case for two runs:

- Fewer boxes per run makes the seven o'clock line much easier to hold, especially on the outer loop when the weather turns.
- It spreads the chill cells across the week, so we're not back to the chill locks we had in the summer.

I also don't fully trust a cost argument made before we've seen a single Season 2 week.

Maija gave the grower desk view. The growers have planned their picking around Tuesday and Friday for weeks now. Changing to a single run would mean a full round of calls, probably a growers' circle, and some unhappiness from whoever ends up on the "wrong" day. Maija would rather not do any of that in the same month as cold start.

We didn't settle it. It stays open. What nobody wants is to change anything about delivery before cold start, so cold start week runs on the twin run exactly as it is now. Rasmus will keep per-run figures in Backroom through the first weeks of Season 2, so that next time we argue with numbers instead of instincts. Rasmus said "the Ledger has all that already" and then corrected it to Backroom before I could say anything. Maija laughed. Maija has only ever known it as Backroom.

## Waitlist

Maija pulled the waitlist from Backroom before the session: **63 households**. That's more than I'd have guessed if anyone had asked me last week. It's a good number to have and an awkward one, because Season 2 keeps the 9 pods and there is no third wave this year. None of the 63 is getting a pod in their lobby this year, however keen their building is.

We went round a few ideas without deciding any of them.

**Pod clusters.** I wondered whether some of the 63 live close enough to an existing pod building for a pod cluster to make sense. Backroom has the street addresses, but nobody has put them on a map. Rasmus will do that before we talk about it again. Maija pointed out the obvious problem straight away: a household in the next building over doesn't have a fob for our lobby. The pod might as well be across the bay. So even if some of the addresses are close, it isn't a solution on its own, just something worth knowing.

**Surplus.** Rasmus, half joking, said we could at least sell the waitlist some surplus somehow. I said that if the words "honesty shelf" came next I was going home. They didn't. It went on the parking lot as "something for the waitlist that isn't a box", with a note next to it that we all remember how the honesty shelf went.

**Lobby hosts.** Maija thinks the lobby hosts get asked about this far more often than we hear. People in neighbouring buildings ask the person they know rather than writing to us. That includes Oskar at Harbour Row (the lobby host, not Oskar at Kestrel). Maija will ask the lobby hosts what they've been telling people, so we can at least make sure we're all saying the same thing.

**The message.** The one thing that does need doing is a straight message to the 63 households. I'll draft it: Season 2 is the same 9 pods, we'll keep them on the list, and we're not giving any dates or promises. We read the draft together before anything goes out. It's material that leaves Larder, so it stays plain: no talk of orphan runs, no talk of where boxes go. Not that there's any reason it would come up, but Rasmus said it anyway, which was right.

Rasmus sat back at this point and said the 63 is the most encouraging thing anyone has said in this room since the first drop. Maija said it's also 63 people we're about to disappoint politely. Both are true.

## Pod passport

This came up from Maija, nearly at the end, and it's the thing I'm happiest about from today.

Maija's point is that whoever drives the loop and does the sweep has to carry everything in their head:

- who the caretaker is in each building
- which lobby host to text
- where the fob for that lobby lives
- which door on which pod sticks when the lobby is cold

Rasmus and I carry most of that. Nobody else does. Linden Yard is the worst case, because it's the newest stop since the move and even I still have to think about it there.

"Each pod should carry its own passport," Maija said: one page, kept in the hatch, with the building contacts, where the fob is, and the pod's faults. Rasmus tried "pod sheet". I tried "hatch card". Then Rasmus said "pod passport" back to Maija in the very next sentence without noticing, and that was that.

Rasmus's first reaction was that the Pulse already has the faults. True, but the Pulse knows that a cell didn't open on the first try. It doesn't know that the caretaker leaves the fob with a neighbour on Mondays, or that one particular door needs a shoulder when it's cold. And a sheet of paper in the hatch still works when your phone is dead at quarter to seven.

So the faults part will come from the Pulse, printed per pod by Rasmus, so that the paper and the screen don't disagree. Maija writes the contacts and fob part by hand from what we know.

Rasmus added one caution that I agree with. More people than us open the hatch, including Kestrel's crew whenever they service or move a pod. So a pod passport carries contacts, fob and faults and nothing else. Nothing from the do-not-tell list goes anywhere near it, and nobody scribbles notes in the margins. Kestrel's own service number will be on it, which is fine. Oskar at Kestrel knows perfectly well where the pods stand.

Maija drafts the Linden Yard one first. We'll look at it together before doing the other eight. If it works, ideally all nine are in their hatches before cold start, but none of us is going to kill ourselves over it.
