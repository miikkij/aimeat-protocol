# Larder · session 17 · 5 May 2026 · The growers' circle

We moved the Tuesday sit-down to the Garage for today, because my kitchen table seats four and we were seven: Ilona and me, Aino from Ridgeback Farm, Pekka from Saltmeadow Gardens, Liisa from Three Oaks Market Garden, and our two newest, Juho from Fernbrook and Kaisa from Mossy Gate Farm. It was the first time every grower had been in one room. It was grey and gusty on Pier Road, so we kept the door half open for air and half shut for the wind. The big thermos was empty before ten. Aino brought a bag of radishes nobody had asked for and everybody ate.

About an hour in, Aino said this wasn't a sit-down any more, it was a growers' circle. She said she'd like one of these whenever the money changes, instead of an email afterwards. Ilona wrote "growers' circle" on the whiteboard and it stayed there all morning, so that's the name now: the meeting of all of Larder's growers together, as opposed to Ilona's one-to-one calls with each farm. Aino had a point. Both earlier times we touched the house cut, the growers heard about it after we'd decided.

## Five growers

Housekeeping first. Fernbrook and Mossy Gate Farm have both signed their GSAs with Ilona over the past week, which takes us to 5 growers. Ilona did both onboardings herself. Each got the onboarding pack, and she sat with each of them for an hour going through Field on her laptop.

Juho's questions were mostly about the cabinets: how cold the chill cells really stay in a warm lobby, and what happens to a box nobody collects. Pekka answered the second one before I could get a word in, which I took as a good sign. The older growers explain the system to the new ones now. Kaisa asked whether a drop scan works with gloves on. Mostly, if you take the right one off. She also asked whether Picker members can see which farm their box came from. They can see the grower on the box page, and she seemed pleased about that.

Pekka and Kaisa already knew each other from market days. Liisa and Juho had never met but turned out to buy seed from the same place, which kept them busy through the first coffee.

## The house cut, again

This was the main business, and the reason Ilona wanted everyone there rather than doing it farm by farm. The growers had talked among themselves beforehand, and Liisa spoke for all five: they want a bigger grower share. Their reasons were plain:

- Fuel to the pods.
- Time on box sheets and drop scans that a market stall doesn't ask for.
- The fact that a Larder box takes their best produce on a Tuesday morning, when they could be selling it elsewhere.

Pekka still says "commission" and probably always will. Nobody corrected him.

Ilona reminded the room, and me, what we did last time. On 3 March we started at 20 % and changed it to 15 % of the box price within minutes. At 20 %, the grower share on what was then a 24 € box left Aino less than her market stall pays her. I remember saying that day that 15 % was about as low as Larder could go. So today means going back on something I'd called a floor of our own, two months later.

My worry wasn't the money as such. It was that changing the house cut twice in two months makes us look like we don't know our numbers, and that's partly true. I said I couldn't honestly tell them whether 12 % works, because I don't know what one drop costs us. The Ledger has the stops, the boxes and the van-day receipts. It doesn't have the hours, or how the van and fuel split across the stops on a loop.

Ilona said, "Cost per drop, then. Call it CPD and let's actually work it out." It stuck, and by the end of the morning even Juho was saying it. CPD is what one drop costs Larder in driving, fuel and time. Juho asked whether "time" meant our time too. It does, and our time is the part we keep leaving out.

We don't have a reliable CPD yet, and I'm not putting a guess in these notes. What we do know is everything the house cut has to carry: the lease on each pod, the lobby fee to each HA, now the insurance (below), and whatever CPD turns out to be.

Ilona's argument for going lower anyway was that growers are the part of this we can't replace quickly. She'd rather run Larder tight for a while than spend June looking for a sixth farm because one of the five walked off. On the 27 € standard box, 3 points is 81 cents a box: the grower share goes from 22.95 € to 23.76 €. "81 cents is cheaper than an onboarding," she said. I couldn't argue with that.

**Decision: the house cut goes down to 12 % of the box price, because the growers' circle asked for a bigger grower share. This replaces the 15 % we set on 3 March.**

I'll change the split in the Ledger this week. Payouts already go out on the new figure from now on, and I'm not touching earlier box weeks.

## Floor price

Pekka wasn't finished. "Fine," he said, "but you've changed it twice since March. What stops it going back up next time you two get nervous about the numbers?" That was fair, and it's the same question Ilona and I wrote down at the sit-down on 28 April: would a guaranteed minimum per box keep growers in?

Kaisa gave it its name. She sells to a couple of restaurants, and she said the only buyers she trusts are the ones with a floor price, the least they'll pay whatever happens. Within five minutes everyone was saying "floor price" for the least a grower is paid for one standard box.

The answer to our 28 April question is yes. All five said a written floor would matter more to them than another point or two off the house cut. Aino said it outright: she can plan a season around a number she knows, but she can't plan around a percentage that moves.

**Decision: every GSA gets a floor price. A grower is paid at least 18 € for each standard box, whatever the house cut.**

At 12 % on a 27 € box the grower share sits well above 18 €, so the floor doesn't bite today. It's a promise about the future, and it limits how far we can ever move the house cut against the growers without reopening the GSAs. I'm fine with that limit. It's honest.

Liisa liked that it goes into the GSA itself, signed by the grower and by Ilona, rather than living on as Ilona's word at a meeting. Liisa also asked whether the half gets a floor of its own. We talked about it for a few minutes and left it there. Today's floor is for the standard box only.

Pekka joked that if building champions get a champion perk, growers should get one too. Ilona said the floor price *is* the growers' champion perk, and that he'd just been handed it. He accepted this with very bad grace and a second radish.

So the guaranteed-minimum question from 28 April is closed: the growers' circle agreed to the floor price.

## The sweep

Liisa brought this up. On her last drop at Mill Gate, the pod front was sticky with something, one of the chill cells smelled strongly of last week's leeks, and the hatch seal on one compartment had come loose at a corner. She fixed what she could and drop-scanned her boxes in anyway. She said she wouldn't want to open that door as a member.

Pekka and Aino both said the same about other stops. Aino said Raimo has been wiping pod zero at Alder Court every so often without being asked, which is kind of him. It's also the only reason Alder Court looks better than the others, and it's not a system.

Ilona said someone needs to give every pod a proper sweep once a week. I wrote "the sweep" on the board under "growers' circle", and that was that. The sweep is the weekly cleaning and check of every pod. What goes on the list:

- Wipe every compartment, inside and out, and the front panels and shroud.
- Check the hatch seals.
- Check the chill cells are cold and the cold chain log has no gaps.
- Check the spine's screen is on and the heartbeat is reaching the Ledger.
- Tidy the honesty shelf at Alder Court.
- Look for any OB that shouldn't still be there.

That last point matters. With the window and the weekday orphan run, nothing should be in a compartment by Monday. If the sweep finds an orphan box, something went wrong earlier in the week and we want to know about it.

We argued a bit over who does it. The growers offered to do it on their own drops, but a grower only sees the cells they fill. Ilona pointed out that whoever drives the loop already knows every stop, has the fobs and has the loop sheet in hand. Monday is the day before the dawn run, so the cells would be clean and checked going into Tuesday.

**Decision: every pod gets the sweep on Mondays, by whoever drives the loop that week.**

I'll add a sweep checklist to the back of the loop sheet, so it gets ticked off in the same place. This doesn't settle the caretaker question. Whether Raimo and the other caretakers should get something for helping with the pods is still open, and today didn't change that.

## Insurance

"Who insures a pod standing in a lobby?" has been on our list since 24 March. Two things finally moved it.

First, Oskar at Kestrel sent through an offer for their lobby insurance last week, after I'd asked him about it on the lease call. This is Oskar the supplier contact, not Oskar our champion at Harbour Row. Lobby insurance is Kestrel's own cover for a pod standing in a lobby, against damage and theft.

Second, we never got a straight answer from anyone on whether an HA's building cover would stretch to a pod it doesn't own. I didn't want to find out after a break-in. Kestrel owns the pods under the lease anyway, so their cover fits more neatly than us shopping around for a policy of our own.

Juho asked whether anyone has ever actually stolen a pod. Aino said she'd like to see them try, plinth and all. But damage is the real worry: a pushchair into the front, a kicked hatch, someone working at a door with a screwdriver.

It also helps Ilona on lobby walks. An HA is a lot more relaxed about giving up a lobby slot when we can say the pod in it is insured. She thinks Sirpa will be glad to hear it.

**Decision: Larder takes Kestrel's lobby insurance for 6 € a month per pod.**

With 4 pods that's 24 € a month, and it goes on the list of things the house cut has to carry, next to the lease and the lobby fee. The 24 March question is settled: Larder insures the pods through Kestrel's lobby insurance.

## Odds and ends

- Juho wondered about a printed map in each lobby showing which growers supply that building. Also on the parking lot. Ilona reminded us that nothing that leaves Larder names where the orphan boxes go, so any map would have to be careful about that.
- Pekka wants the payout breakdown in Field to show the grower share and the house cut per box, not only the total. That's a small job and I'll do it with the Ledger change.
- Nothing went on the red list today, which is a first in a while.

Everyone was gone by half twelve. Liisa took the last of the radishes, and Ilona and I stood in the doorway for a bit agreeing that it had gone better than either of us expected. A month ago I'd have said we couldn't afford 12 %. I still don't know that we can, which is why CPD is the first thing on my list.
