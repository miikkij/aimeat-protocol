# Larder · session 1 · 13 January 2026 · Kick-off: the locker idea

We did the kick-off at Ilona's kitchen table rather than the garage, because the garage is still unheated and it was the kind of January morning where the sleet comes in sideways off the harbour. Two pots of coffee, one notebook each, and a whiteboard that turned out to be mostly decorative because neither of us had a marker that worked. These are my notes, written up the same evening while it's fresh.

## The idea, as we talked it through

The starting point is something we've both been circling for months without saying it out loud properly. People in Lakeport say they want local vegetables. There are small farms within reach of the city that would like to sell more directly. And yet the existing ways of connecting the two are awkward for almost everyone. Farm-gate shops mean a drive. The Saturday market means being free on a Saturday morning and carrying bags home on the tram. Home delivery means someone has to be in, or the box sits on a doorstep in the rain, or it goes back to a depot and the whole thing collapses into missed-delivery cards.

What we kept coming back to is that the problem isn't really the vegetables or the farms. It's the handover. Ilona put it well: "Everybody's solved the farm to the van. Nobody's solved the van to the kitchen." We spent a good while on that phrase and eventually I wrote "last metre" in the margin, partly as a joke on "last mile". The point is that in a city like this the van can get to the building fine. What fails is the short step from the delivery vehicle to wherever the food waits safely until the person comes for it. The last metre is the bit we want to own. Ilona liked it enough that they underlined it twice, so I think it's staying.

The obvious answer, which we'd both half-arrived at separately, is lockers. Parcel lockers are already everywhere in Lakeport. People are used to the idea of a code and a door that pops open. But parcel lockers sit outside supermarkets and petrol stations, which puts us back at "you have to go somewhere". The version we actually want is a locker inside the building where people live. You come down in your slippers, you open a door, you take your vegetables upstairs.

## Pods in lobbies

We needed a word for the locker unit itself, because "locker" kept getting confused between the whole cabinet and a single door. I started calling the cabinet a "pod" somewhere in the middle of the second coffee, mostly because it's short and sounds less like a gym changing room. Ilona objected at first ("it sounds like a coffee capsule") but then used it three times in the next five minutes without noticing, so that settled it. So: a pod is the locker unit that stands in the lobby of an apartment building, and members collect their boxes from it.

The single lockable door on a pod we're calling a compartment, which is just the word the parcel-locker people use anyway, and there was no reason to be clever about it. One compartment holds one member's box for one drop. That one-box-per-compartment-per-drop thing matters because it means the number of compartments in a pod is effectively the ceiling on how many people in that building we can serve on a given drop. We didn't try to put a number on it today. That depends on the pod model, which we haven't even started looking at, and I'd rather not guess.

The other thing that needed a name was the floor space. A pod has to physically stand somewhere, and in practice that somewhere belongs to the HA. HA is just our shorthand for housing association, the residents' association that runs the building's common spaces: the lobby, the stairwells, the bike room, the noticeboard. Every building we'd want to be in has one, and none of them owe us anything. Ilona started saying "lobby slot" for the patch of floor an HA agrees to let a pod stand on. I like it because it makes it sound like something you ask for and get granted, which is exactly right. A lobby slot is permission as much as it is square metres.

We talked for a long time about what an HA would actually want in exchange, and I want to record that we didn't settle it. Possibilities that came up and were left alone:

- Nothing at all, if the residents themselves want it and vote for it.
- Some kind of fee for the space. Ilona thinks some HAs will ask for one on principle.
- Something for the residents rather than the HA.
- Help with power, if the pod needs to be plugged in.

That last one opened the chilling question, which I'll come back to under worries. The short version is that a pod holding vegetables isn't a parcel locker. A cardboard box of books doesn't care if the lobby is warm. A bag of salad leaves does.

Ilona raised caretakers too. Most of these buildings have a caretaker or a maintenance company who would be the person actually walking past the pod every day. Do we involve them? Do they become a problem if we don't? Parked for now. Neither of us knows enough about how caretaking is arranged in Lakeport buildings to have an opinion worth writing down.

## Growers and boxes

We spent less time on the supply side because Ilona knows it much better than I do and is already confident it's solvable. The farms we'd be working with are what everyone in this world just calls growers: small local farms that would supply the boxes. Not wholesalers, not a co-op warehouse, just growers. I asked whether we'd ever source from anyone bigger, and Ilona said it's not the kind of thing worth deciding in week one, which is fair.

A box is one member's weekly delivery of vegetables from one grower. That "from one grower" part was a deliberate choice of definition rather than a business decision. It just makes the word mean one thing. Whether a member could ever get stuff from several growers in one go is a different question for another day. For now, when we say "a box", we mean one person, one week, one grower.

I asked the obvious questions about what goes in a box and how big it is and what it costs. Ilona's answer was that we need to talk to growers before we have opinions, and that anything we said today would just be a number we'd then get attached to. I agree. So the notebook has a page headed "prices" that is deliberately empty, with a note to myself not to fill it in until we've actually talked to people.

## Members (or customers)

Here we had a small, friendly argument about words. Ilona kept saying "customers", which is the normal word and what anyone would say. I kept saying "members", and when Ilona asked why, I had to work out my own reason. It's this: the whole thing only works if the people in a building feel like it's a bit theirs. A pod in a lobby is a shared object in a shared space. If it feels like a vending machine some company put there, people will resent it, the HA will get complaints, and the lobby slot goes away. If it feels like something the residents signed up to together, they'll look after it.

So a member, in our notes, is a resident who subscribes to Larder boxes. Ilona said "fine, but I'm going to keep saying customer", and I said that's fine too, they're the same thing. I'm writing that down so that future-me doesn't get confused reading Ilona's notes: customer and member mean the same person. My guess is that "member" wins over time, but I'm not going to police it.

We did talk about what a member actually experiences, step by step, mostly as a way of finding holes:

1. They live in a building that has a pod.
2. They sign up somehow (left completely open, I just wrote "?? signup").
3. A grower packs their box.
4. Someone brings it to the building and puts it in a compartment. That's the last metre.
5. The member is told it's there and how to open it.
6. They come down whenever they like and take it.

Step 5 is the one I'm most interested in from the software side, obviously, but I deliberately didn't start designing anything. I don't know yet whether it's an app, a text message, a code on paper, or something else, and I'd rather find out what people actually want than build the thing I'd enjoy building.

## The bet

Towards the end of the morning Ilona asked a good question: "If this doesn't work, what's the one thing that will have been wrong?" We went round it a few times. Is it that growers won't supply? Probably not, Ilona thinks they will. Is it that HAs won't give us space? Possibly, but that's an obstacle, not the core idea. Is it that people don't want local vegetables? No, they say they do, and we're not trying to convince anyone of that.

What it actually comes down to is the pickup. The whole idea stands or falls on whether residents will pick up vegetable boxes in their own lobby if pickup is open around the clock. Not at a shop, not at a market, not in a two-hour delivery window. Their own lobby, whenever suits them. If that's true, everything else is logistics. If it's not true, if people still forget, still leave boxes to wilt, still prefer the supermarket on the way home, then pods in lobbies are just expensive furniture.

I said "that's the bet, then", and Ilona wrote THE BET in capitals across the top of the next page. So that's our name for it. The bet is the hypothesis we're actually testing. Everything we build or arrange in the next while should be in service of finding out whether the bet holds, as cheaply and quickly as we can.

I want to be careful here, because I can feel us both wanting to get ahead of ourselves. "Around the clock" is the hypothesis, not a promise we've made to anyone. How the pickup window actually works in practice, whether it really is all hours, what happens to a box nobody collects, all of that is open. The bet just says that open-all-hours pickup in your own lobby is what makes the difference.

## How many buildings, roughly

Ilona has been walking the neighbourhood around the garage with this vaguely in mind for a few weeks and reckons there are about 40 apartment buildings within 3 km of the garage. That's not a target or a plan, it's just the size of the pond. Within 3 km matters because the garage is where Ilona expects the operational side to live, so anything further out starts to feel like a different problem. We didn't pick a building, didn't pick how many pods, didn't pick anything. But 40 buildings within easy reach is enough to make the idea feel real rather than theoretical, and it's a useful number to hold in our heads when we talk about HAs.

## Worries and parking lot

Things that came up, that I don't want to lose, but that we explicitly didn't decide:

- **Cold.** A pod with fresh vegetables in a heated lobby is a problem. Does it need chilling? If so, it needs power, and that's an HA conversation. I don't know enough about this yet to say anything sensible.
- **Uncollected boxes.** What happens when someone doesn't come down? Ilona's instinct is that this will happen more than we think. No answer today.
- **Weather and the van.** Lakeport in January is exactly like today. The last metre is short, but it's not nothing in sleet.
- **Who does the last metre?** Me, Ilona, a grower, someone else? With what vehicle? We didn't go there.
- **Trust in the lobby.** Will people be weird about a locker in their shared space? Will someone vandalise it? Ilona thinks most HAs will ask about this first.
- **Money.** Not just prices, but who we'd even need to pay and for what. Pod, space, delivery, growers. The empty "prices" page stays empty.
- **The name.** "Larder" is what I've called the folder on my laptop and what we've both been saying. It's fine as a working name. Whether it's what members ever see is a separate conversation.
- **Half-idea from Ilona:** a shelf next to the pod where people leave surplus veg for neighbours. Lovely, probably a distraction, parked.
- **Half-idea from me:** some kind of per-building person who's the friendly face of the pod for their neighbours. Also parked. Ilona said "that's just a caretaker with extra steps", which made me laugh but isn't quite what I meant.

Ilona's phone went twice during the session. Once was their sister, once was a grower Ilona already knows from the market, which Ilona took as a sign. I'm taking it as a sign that Ilona's phone rings a lot.

## Where we ended up

No big decisions, which feels right for a first session. What we do have is a shared vocabulary (pod, compartment, lobby slot, HA, grower, box, member, last metre, the bet) and a clear sense that the bet is the thing to test before anything else. We both left a bit giddy, which I want to note so we can look back later and see whether it was justified.
