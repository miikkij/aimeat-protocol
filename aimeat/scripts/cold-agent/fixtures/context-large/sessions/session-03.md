# Larder · session 3 · 27 January 2026 · The first grower signs

Aino came to the Garage this morning, which already says something. She drove in from Ridgeback Farm through sleet that couldn't decide whether it wanted to be snow, with a crate of leeks and celeriac on the passenger seat "so you know what you're talking about." Ilona had the coffee on before either of us had our coats off. It was a long sit-down, longer than our usual Tuesday ones, and I'm writing this up the same evening while it's fresh.

## Ridgeback Farm is in

The short version first: the question we left hanging on 20 January, who will be the first grower, is settled. Aino's Ridgeback Farm is our first grower, and she signed the first GSA today with Ilona signing for Larder. Growers signed: 1 (Ridgeback Farm).

It didn't feel like a foregone conclusion when she walked in. Last week we'd talked through what we wanted from the first grower: someone close enough that the drive to town isn't a day's work, someone who already sells direct and knows what a person actually does with a kilo of beetroot, and someone patient enough to put up with us figuring out the last metre while we go. Aino ticks all of that, but she was also the person most likely to tell us the whole thing was daft, and for the first half hour I thought she might.

What turned it was the pod. Ilona had the photos from the lobby walks up on the laptop, the lobby slots we've been shown, the narrow entrance halls, the one with the radiator that would cook anything left near it. Aino looked at them for a long time and then said that her biggest problem at the market stall isn't selling, it's the people who mean to come and don't, because the stall closes before they get off work. That's the bet in her own words, without us having said it. After that the conversation was about how, not whether.

She had real worries and we didn't paper over them:

- What happens to a box nobody collects. We don't have an answer yet and said so. It went on the red list rather than the parking lot, because she'll want to know before her first harvest goes in.
- Whether she'll be stuck dealing with members directly when a carrot is bent. Ilona said no: members talk to Larder, Larder talks to growers. Aino visibly relaxed at that.
- How much she has to commit in a bad week. This is where the GSA came in.

## The GSA

Ilona had a draft contract with her, and for most of last week we'd been calling it "the grower agreement" or "the paper" or, from me, "the contract thing." Aino read the title out loud: "grower share agreement." Ilona said, "GSA, then," and that's what everyone called it from then on. It's short, which I think is the main reason Aino was willing to sign it on the day rather than taking it home to sit on. It sets the grower share and it sets out what the grower is responsible for: what goes in the box, how it's packed, when it's ready, and what she tells us when something fails.

The term "grower share" is also new as of today, and I think it came out of me trying to explain the money without saying "your cut," which sounded wrong when said to the person doing the actual growing. The grower share is the part of the box price that goes to the grower. Aino asked what we'd call the rest, and Ilona said, "the house cut," half as a joke, like a casino. Aino laughed and said at least it was honest. It stuck. So: grower share for her, house cut for Larder, and the two together make the box price.

We did not put a figure on the house cut today as a thing of its own, and I want to be clear in these notes that we haven't decided what it has to cover or how big it should be across the board. The grower share for Ridgeback is written in the GSA Ilona and Aino signed; the house cut for now is simply whatever is left of the box price after it. We'll have to come back to that properly once we know what the pods, the driving and the software actually cost us. I put a line on the parking lot: "what does the house cut have to pay for — list it before we argue about the size."

Ilona keeps the signed original. I asked for a scan for the shared folder, and she gave me a look that meant "obviously," and then wrote it on her hand anyway.

## What a box is, and what it costs

We'd been saying "a box" for weeks without ever pinning down how big it was. Aino forced the issue by asking how many people she's feeding. We settled on talking about a standard box: about 6 kg of vegetables, meant for two to three people for the week. Aino says that's roughly what her regulars at the stall walk away with when they're buying for a couple with a kid, and it fits in a compartment with room to spare, which I checked against the measurements from the lobby walks while they argued about swedes.

"Standard box" is a phrase Aino already used. To her it's the ordinary bag, as opposed to whatever someone special-orders. It made sense to borrow it, because it leaves room for other sizes later without us committing to any of them today. Ilona did ask Aino whether a smaller box would make sense for people living alone. We didn't go any further than that and it isn't decided.

Then the price. Decision: a standard box costs a member 24 €.

How we got there, roughly:

- Ilona had been round the shops on the weekend looking at what a comparable bag of local vegetables costs when you buy it loose, and the honest answer is that we are not going to be the cheapest. We don't want to be. The pitch is that it's in your own lobby whenever you want it, and that a real farm grew it.
- Aino needs a grower share that makes it worth her while to grow for us rather than just doing more market days. At 24 € the GSA works for her. She said that plainly, and I believe her; she isn't someone who signs things to be polite.
- A round number matters. I'd been modelling odd figures in the Ledger, and Ilona pointed out that nobody standing in a lobby reading a flyer is going to remember a price with cents on it. 24 € is easy to say, easy to put on a poster in a stairwell, and easy for an HA chair to repeat at a meeting.
- We talked about whether it should be lower to start, to get people in. We decided against it for now, because a low launch price only teaches members that the real price is a rise. If the bet is right, people will pay for the convenience. If they won't pay 24 € for it, that tells us something we need to know early.

Ilona used "customer" once or twice while we were on price, which she does when she's thinking about the shop comparison. Aino asked whether members and customers were different people, and Ilona said, "No, same person, I'm just old-fashioned."

For the record, I'll put the 24 € into the Ledger as the standard box price. Until today it's been sitting there as a placeholder with a question mark next to it.

## Box sheets

This was Aino's idea, more or less, and it's going to save us grief. She asked how we'd know what's in each week's box. For the stall, she writes a list on the back of an envelope every week of what's coming out of the field and roughly how much, and she said she could just send us that. Ilona said, "So, a box sheet," and Aino said fine, call it that.

So the box sheet is the list a grower sends every week of what goes into that week's boxes. We want it for a few reasons: so members know what's coming (Ilona's main point, since people plan meals), so I have something to check a box against when someone says it's short, and so that when there's eventually more than one grower we're not all trying to remember who said what on the phone.

I'd like the box sheet to land in the software rather than in someone's email inbox or a photo of an envelope. I didn't promise anything, and I'm not sure yet whether that belongs in Field or somewhere else. For the first weeks a photo of an envelope will do. Aino will send a trial one from what she has in store now, so I can see the format she naturally writes in before I go building anything around it.

We didn't decide what day of the week the box sheet arrives, or how far ahead. That depends on things we haven't settled yet.

## Who looks after growers

Decision: Ilona onboards new growers.

This was quick, but I want the reasons written down because it'll come up again. Ilona is the one who's been out to the farms, has the phone numbers, and spent last week drafting the GSA. Growers should have one person at Larder they know by name, and it shouldn't be the one who disappears into code for three days at a time. I'll help with anything technical a grower needs to do, but the relationship is Ilona's. Aino said this was the right call, mostly because she'd already decided Ilona was the sensible one. I'm choosing not to take that personally.

Onboarding means signing the GSA and walking the grower through what we expect. It also means making sure they know how to send a box sheet, what the packing needs are, and who to ring when things go wrong. Right now all of that lives in Ilona's head and in a few loose documents. She's calling the collection the onboarding pack: the documents and instructions a new grower gets when they join. Aino is effectively getting a first, rough version of it, and has agreed to tell us everything in it that's confusing or wrong, which in practice means she's helping write it.

What should go in the onboarding pack, from today's conversation (not final, just what came up):

- a copy of the GSA and a plain-language page explaining it
- what a standard box is and what we expect it to look like when it arrives
- how and when to send the box sheet
- who to contact at Larder, and for what
- photos of a pod and a compartment, so growers can picture where their vegetables actually end up

The last one was Aino's request. She wants to see the place before she believes in it, and frankly so would I.
