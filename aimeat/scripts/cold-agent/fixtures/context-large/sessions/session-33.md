> ## Churn after the standard box price rise: analysis and options
>
> ### 1. Summary
>
> Monthly churn is now 9 %. The rise appears to coincide with the standard box going from its earlier price to 29 € on 2 June. Pickup behaviour is healthy (the pickup rate is well clear of the 85 % kill threshold, and the orphan rate is around 3 %), so the problem is not the pod experience. It is the decision to keep subscribing. My recommendation, set out in section 6, is a standard box at 27 € combined with a loyalty discount for long-standing members. The reasoning and the alternatives are below.
>
> ### 2. What the data shows
>
> 1. **Churn is concentrated in standard box members.** The cancellation records skew towards the standard tier rather than Sprout or Harvest. This is what you would expect if price is the driver, since the standard box took the rise.
> 2. **Cancellation reasons point at price.** The free-text reasons left in Picker are dominated by price and value comparisons ("too expensive", "the market is cheaper"). A smaller group mentions moving or summer travel; these are normal background churn and would happen at any price.
> 3. **Pickup is not the issue.** The 36-hour pickup window has not raised orphan boxes; the orphan rate remains about 3 %. Members who stay are collecting their boxes.
> 4. **The north number is growing, but slowly.** Last week 131 boxes were picked up. The target for the end of the extended pilot (30 September) is 160. With 9 pods across wave 1 and wave 2, the pilot is adding members, but a 9 % monthly churn means a large share of new sign-ups is spent replacing members who leave.
>
> ### 3. Why churn matters more than it looks
>
> A monthly churn of 9 % compounds. Holding sign-ups constant, it means roughly a third of the membership turns over in four months. For a pilot that has to reach 160 boxes a week by 30 September, every lost member has to be replaced before any growth counts. My estimate is that pre-rise churn was in the region of 4–5 % a month (this is modelled from the cancellation dates and is less certain than the current figure), so the rise roughly doubled it.
>
> It is also worth noting that the kill criteria are written around the pickup rate, not churn. The pilot could therefore meet its kill criteria comfortably while shrinking. You may want to watch churn alongside the pickup rate even though it is not formally a stop condition.
>
> ### 4. Did the original reason for 29 € still apply?
>
> The rise to 29 € was made to pay for packing in the Garage. Since then, packing has moved back: standard and small boxes are grower-packed again, and the Garage packs only the family boxes. The cost the rise was meant to cover has therefore largely gone away for the standard tier, while the price has stayed. That is a strong argument that the standard price can come down without undoing the reason it went up.
>
> ### 5. Unit economics at different standard prices
>
> Assumptions: house cut 14 %, grower share 86 %, sales share to the HA 2 % of box sales (assumed here to be paid from Larder's side), floor price 18 € for the grower per standard box.
>
> | Standard price | Grower share (86 %) | House cut (14 %) | Sales share (2 %) | Larder keeps after sales share | Floor price met? |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 29 € | 24.94 € | 4.06 € | 0.58 € | 3.48 € | yes |
> | 28 € | 24.08 € | 3.92 € | 0.56 € | 3.36 € | yes |
> | 27 € | 23.22 € | 3.78 € | 0.54 € | 3.24 € | yes |
> | 26 € | 22.36 € | 3.64 € | 0.52 € | 3.12 € | yes |
>
> Points from the table:
>
> 1. **The floor price is not at risk** at any of these prices. Even at 26 € the grower receives 22.36 €, well above 18 €.
> 2. **Larder's margin per box falls by about 0.12 € per euro of price cut.** Going from 29 € to 27 € costs Larder roughly 0.24 € per box after the sales share.
> 3. **Fixed pod costs do not move.** The lease (85 € a month per pod) and lobby insurance (6 € a month per pod) come to 91 € per pod per month, about 819 € a month for 9 pods. These are covered by volume, not price, which is why retention matters more than margin per box at this stage.
> 4. **Break-even on retention is favourable.** If a lower price reduces churn even modestly, the members kept are worth more than the margin given up. A member kept for an extra month at 27 € brings in more house cut than the 0.24 € per box lost across the other members, for any realistic membership size.
>
> ### 6. Options considered
>
> 1. **Hold at 29 €.** Simplest, protects margin per box, but leaves churn at 9 % and makes the 160 target hard to reach. Not recommended.
> 2. **Cut to 28 €.** A small move; members may not notice it as a real change. Probably too timid to affect churn.
> 3. **Cut to 27 € with a loyalty discount.** A meaningful price change, plus a reward for staying. For example, members who have taken a box for eight consecutive box weeks could receive 1 € off each box from then on. This targets the members most likely to stay and gives them a reason not to cancel.
> 4. **Cut to 26 €.** The largest reduction considered. Clear and simple to communicate, and the floor price still holds. It gives up the most margin per box, and leaves no room to reward loyalty without going lower still.
> 5. **Introductory discount for new members only.** Helps acquisition but does nothing for the members who are leaving, and could annoy existing members.
>
> ### 7. Recommendation
>
> I recommend **option 3: a standard box at 27 € with a loyalty discount.**
>
> Reasons:
>
> 1. It removes most of the gap that seems to have triggered cancellations, while keeping Larder's margin per box higher than at 26 €.
> 2. The loyalty discount speaks directly to churn. It makes leaving costlier for exactly the members who have built the habit of the weekly pickup.
> 3. It keeps a lever in reserve: if churn does not fall, a move to 26 € remains available.
>
> ### 8. Knock-on effects to check
>
> 1. **Sprout (small box, 17 €).** At 27 €, the gap between the small and standard box narrows from 12 € to 10 €. Members who are price-sensitive may trade down to Sprout. You may want to review whether 17 € is still the right small box price relative to the new standard.
> 2. **Harvest (family box, 39 €).** At 29 € + 17 €, a standard plus small box cost 46 €, and Harvest was a clear saving. At 27 € + 17 € the combination is 44 €, so Harvest remains a clear saving of 5 €.
> 3. **Make-goods.** Under the rainy-day rule, the make-good is half the box price, so it falls with any cut. The short box make-good is a fixed 5 € and is unaffected.
> 4. **Growers.** The grower share per standard box falls (from 24.94 € to 23.22 € at 27 €). This should be explained to growers before the change, ideally with the floor price as reassurance.
> 5. **Backroom and Picker.** A loyalty discount needs a consecutive-weeks counter, handling of skip weeks, and a display in Picker. This is the most complex part of the recommendation and should be scoped before committing to it.
>
> ### 9. What to watch afterwards
>
> 1. Monthly churn, per tier and per building.
> 2. Trade-down from the standard box to Sprout.
> 3. Weekly boxes picked up against the 160 target.
> 4. Cancellation reasons in Picker, to see whether price drops out of them.
>
> Churn figures respond with a lag, so a reliable read on any price change will need at least one full month of data.
