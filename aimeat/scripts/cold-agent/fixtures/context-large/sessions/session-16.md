> ### Pickup window: keep 24 hours or extend?
>
> **Summary.** On the data so far, I'd keep the pickup window at 24 hours and add a reminder SMS 18 hours after each drop. The current window is not what puts the pilot at risk: the pickup rate sits comfortably above the kill line. A reminder targets the most likely cause of misses, forgetting, at very low cost, while a longer window carries costs that are easy to underestimate. There is one caveat (section 5) that could change this.
>
> ### 1. What the figures say
>
> 1. **Orphan rate so far: 6 %.** That puts the running PUR at about 94 %, nine points above the 85 % line in the kill criteria. To trip them, the PUR would have to fall by more than that and stay there four weeks in a row.
> 2. **Boxes picked up last week: 41.** If last week's orphan share was near the running 6 %, roughly 43–44 boxes went into the pods and two or three became orphan boxes.
> 3. **Members: 38.** 41 picked-up boxes is more than one per member. Either the member count predates recent sign-ups or some households take more than one box. Worth checking in the Ledger before reading anything per member.
> 4. **First drop, 10 March: 11 boxes** into the Alder Court pod. Growth from 11 to the low 40s in about seven weeks is the strongest signal in the data. The window is a second-order question next to it.
> 5. **North number: 41 against a target of 100.** At a 94 % PUR, 100 picked-up boxes needs about 106 dropped per week. Even a perfect pickup rate would add only two or three boxes a week to the NN at current volumes.
> 6. **The figures are pilot-wide.** Alder Court has had boxes since the first drop; the other wave 1 buildings have had them for less time, so their members are newer and may still be learning the routine. A per-building split would show whether misses cluster in one lobby.
>
> ### 2. What a longer window could gain
>
> The most any window change can recover is the orphan boxes themselves, currently two or three a week. Not all would be saved by more time: some members will have been away, some will have forgotten entirely.
>
> | Option | Modelled PUR | Orphan boxes/week (at ~44 dropped) | Note |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | 24 h, as now | ~94 % | 2–3 | Baseline |
> | 24 h + reminder at 18 h | ~96–97 % (estimate) | 1–2 | Assumes reminders recover about half of "forgot" misses |
> | 48 h | ~97–98 % (estimate) | ~1 | Assumes most misses are timing, not absence |
>
> These are estimates, not measurements. The reminder figure leans on typical results for reminders in pickup and appointment services; the 48-hour figure assumes most misses are about timing. Neither has been tested on Cropbox members.
>
> ### 3. What a longer window would cost
>
> 1. **Freshness in the compartment.** With a Tuesday dawn run, a 48-hour window means some boxes sit in the pod until Thursday morning. Boxes in dry cells sit at lobby temperature, and leafy items lose quality noticeably over a second day. Members who collect late will judge the box, not the window.
> 2. **Freshness at the food bank.** Orphan boxes would reach the Harbour Pantry a day older. A two-day-old box is still usable, but the older it is, the more of it becomes waste on their side, and the relationship with Hilda depends on the boxes being useful.
> 3. **Habit.** A short window gives members a clear rhythm: box in on Tuesday, collect by Wednesday morning. Longer windows often shift pickups later rather than catching more of them.
> 4. **Cold chain log.** A longer window lengthens the period a chill cell box could be questioned. Not a problem in itself, but more to check if a member complains about quality.
> 5. **Capacity is not a constraint yet.** Four K-12 pods give 48 compartments, 12 of them chill cells. With one drop day a week, a compartment isn't needed again until the next Tuesday, so 48 hours doesn't block the next drop. At roughly 44 boxes across 48 compartments the pods are close to full, though, and if more drop days are ever added, window length will start to matter.
>
> ### 4. The reminder option
>
> - Send an SMS 18 hours after the drop to each member whose compartment hasn't been opened: "Your box closes in 6 hours", with the wake code repeated.
> - Members already get the wake code by SMS, so numbers, consent and the sending route already exist.
> - Cost: one extra text per uncollected box, a handful a week at present. Negligible next to a 27 € standard box or a 17 € small box.
> - It can be switched off without changing anything members have been told about the window.
> - It produces evidence. If misses fall after reminders, the problem was forgetting. If they don't, it's timing, and that is the case for a longer window.
>
> ### 5. The caveat
>
> The data shows how many boxes became orphan boxes, not why. If misses concentrate among a few members whose hours never overlap a 24-hour span starting at the dawn run (for example, someone leaving for a night shift on Tuesday evening and returning after the window closes on Wednesday morning), a reminder will not help them and a longer window would. Before deciding, it would be worth checking which members have more than one orphan box, and asking the building champions whether they have heard of schedules not fitting.
>
> ### 6. Food bank timing
>
> With Tuesday drops, a 24-hour window releases orphan boxes on Wednesday morning and a 48-hour window on Thursday morning. Both are weekdays, so neither depends on the still-open question of whether the Harbour Pantry takes boxes at weekends.
>
> ### Recommendation
>
> 1. Keep the pickup window at 24 hours from the drop.
> 2. Add a reminder SMS at 18 hours for uncollected boxes.
> 3. Track the PUR for four weeks with reminders on and compare it with the 6 % orphan rate so far.
> 4. Check whether misses repeat for the same members. If they do, look at those cases rather than changing the window for everyone.
