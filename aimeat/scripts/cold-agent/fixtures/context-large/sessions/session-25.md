> ## Midsummer review: pilot numbers to date
>
> ### Summary
>
> The pilot has largely answered its central question. The pickup rate has stayed well above the kill threshold, weekly pickups have roughly doubled since the spring, and the fixed cost per pod is now known. My recommendation is to end the pilot on schedule on 30 June, use July to consolidate, and begin the next phase in August. The reasoning and caveats are below.
>
> ### 1. Where the numbers stand
>
> | Measure | Latest figure | Reference point |
> |---|---|---|
> | Boxes picked up per week | 88 | North number target: 100 |
> | Pickup rate (latest week) | 91 % | Kill criteria: under 85 % for four weeks |
> | Orphan rate (cumulative) | 6 % | — |
> | Active pods | 6 (K-16) | 4 in wave 1, 2 in wave 2 so far |
> | Growers under GSA | 5 | — |
> | Grant awarded | 12 000 € | — |
>
> Points to note:
>
> 1. **The north number is at 88 % of target.** An earlier export showed 41 boxes picked up in a week. The rise to 88 is the strongest signal in the data. If the recent trend holds, 100 is plausibly within reach in a few weeks, though the data does not let me say exactly when.
> 2. **The pickup rate is comfortably clear of the kill criteria.** 91 % leaves six points of margin, and nothing in the export suggests a four-week run below 85 %.
> 3. **The orphan rate deserves a second look.** A cumulative 6 % and a latest-week pickup rate of 91 % do not fully agree. If 91 % of boxes were collected inside the window, roughly 9 % became orphan boxes that week. Either the latest week was worse than average, or the two figures are calculated on different bases (for example, whether skipped boxes are excluded). This is worth checking in Backroom before either figure is quoted outside the team.
>
> ### 2. Unit economics (estimates)
>
> To simplify, I assume every box is a standard box at 29 € and count revenue on boxes picked up only. This is conservative, because cards are charged on the drop day whether or not the box is collected.
>
> | Per week | At 88 boxes | At 100 boxes |
> |---|---|---|
> | Box sales | 2 552 € | 2 900 € |
> | House cut (12 %) | 306 € | 348 € |
> | Less sales share to HAs (2 % of sales) | −51 € | −58 € |
> | Less pod lease and lobby insurance (6 pods × 91 € a month ≈ 126 € a week) | −126 € | −126 € |
> | **Left for driving, packing, coordinator and software** | **≈ 129 €** | **≈ 164 €** |
>
> Observations:
>
> 1. **The floor price is not binding at current prices.** With a 12 % house cut, the grower share on a standard box is about 25.50 €, well above the 18 € floor. The floor would matter only if prices or the house cut moved a long way.
> 2. **The remainder is thin.** Roughly 130–165 € a week has to cover van hire on van day, fuel, the cargo bike leg, the coordinator's hours at 22 € an hour, and the pack line. Six coordinator hours alone would use most of it. The pilot is not self-funding week to week, which is normal at this scale. The grant gives a cushion but should not be read as a business model.
> 3. **Product mix changes the picture.** Small boxes at 17 € contribute less per compartment and family boxes at 39 € contribute more. The export does not give a reliable mix breakdown, so the table above is a rough guide only.
> 4. **Density beats pod count.** Pod costs are fixed per month, so each extra box picked up in an existing pod is close to pure contribution. At this stage, filling existing pods matters more than adding new ones.
>
> ### 3. What the pilot has answered, and what it has not
>
> **Answered with reasonable confidence:**
>
> 1. *The bet.* Residents do collect boxes from their own lobby when pickup is open around the clock. A pickup rate above 90 % across several buildings is strong evidence.
> 2. *Grower supply.* Five growers have signed GSAs on the current house cut and floor price.
> 3. *Orphan handling.* Orphan boxes leave the pods on the orphan run without putting any load on caretakers.
>
> **Not yet answered:**
>
> 1. *Delivery cost under the current loop design.* The Thursday dawn run and the cargo bike leg have only a few weeks of history, so cost per drop has not settled.
> 2. *Behaviour outside summer.* Weather skips, darker mornings and cold lobbies have not been tested at the current scale.
> 3. *Wave 2 performance.* Two buildings with a short history are not enough to say whether wave 2 behaves like wave 1.
> 4. *Several open questions*, such as payment at the pod, arrangements with caretakers, the name trademark and pod size for smaller buildings. The numbers do not settle any of these.
>
> ### 4. Why end on schedule
>
> 1. **A pilot exists to test the core hypothesis, and this one has.** Extending risks an open-ended holding pattern in which every decision stays provisional.
> 2. **The remaining questions are operational, not existential.** Delivery cost and seasonal behaviour are things to tune in a running service. They do not decide whether the service should exist.
> 3. **A clear boundary helps with partners.** HAs and growers respond well to a defined next phase, and it gives a natural moment to revisit terms.
> 4. **Summer data flatters.** July and August pickups will not tell you much about autumn unless the pilot runs well into autumn, which makes a short extension the weakest option.
>
> ### 5. Suggested timeline
>
> 1. **30 June:** close the pilot formally and freeze a final set of pilot figures.
> 2. **July:** consolidate. Reconcile the orphan rate with the pickup rate, measure cost per drop for each loop, document grower onboarding, and prepare the remaining wave 2 buildings. Drops continue on current terms.
> 3. **August:** begin the next phase, with new targets set from the frozen pilot figures.
>
> ### 6. Caveats
>
> - This analysis uses the export up to last week. One more week of data could change the picture, especially this close to 100.
> - All money figures are estimates based on a simplified box mix.
> - I cannot see the qualitative side: relationships with HAs, how engaged the building champions are, or how much appetite the growers have.
> - If there is one specific question you want answered before closing, extend only for that question, with a fixed end date and a single measure tied to it.
