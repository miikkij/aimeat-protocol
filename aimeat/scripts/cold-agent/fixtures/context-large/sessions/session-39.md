## Larder pilot retrospective: analysis and options for the next phase

*Based on the Backroom figures shared with me up to late September. I have not seen the final days of the pilot, so treat the counts below as a snapshot rather than final results.*

### 1. Overall assessment

The pilot has done what it needed to do. The core hypothesis held up across nine buildings and two waves, not only in the first building, and the operating model has been adjusted several times without losing members' trust. The main shortfall is scale: the north number is still below its target of 160. The question for the next phase is less "does this work?" than "where does the next 20–30 % of volume come from?"

### 2. What worked

1. **Collection behaviour.** An orphan rate of around 3 % is low for an unattended collection model. It also means the pickup rate is far above the kill criteria threshold (under 85 % for four consecutive weeks), so there is no realistic stop signal in the current data.
2. **The price correction.** Churn rose to 9 % after the standard box went to 29 €, then fell to roughly 5 % after the cut to 26 €. That is close to a natural experiment: members in these buildings are price-sensitive in the high twenties, and the 16 € / 26 € / 39 € ladder appears to sit in an acceptable range.
3. **Delivery changes.** The twin run on Tuesdays and Fridays, the cargo bike leg in the centre and the 36-hour pickup window together addressed the chill lock problem and kept Mondays clear for the sweep.
4. **Grower terms.** With eight growers on GSAs that include an 18 € floor price, the supply side looks stable. At the current standard box price the floor is not binding (see section 5), which makes it a low-cost guarantee.

### 3. What did not work, or not yet

1. **Volume.** The most recent weekly figure I have is 131 boxes picked up, against a target of 160. Closing that gap needs about 29 more boxes a week, an increase of roughly 22 %.
2. **Building dependency.** The Alder Court vote (14 to 9) shows that a lobby slot can be withdrawn by a fairly narrow majority, even where the pod was performing. The move to Linden Yard solved the immediate problem, but the exposure applies to every building.
3. **Abandoned experiments.** The honesty shelf (around 40 % of its surplus never paid for) and the neighbour credit (abused through second phone numbers) both failed on trust rather than demand. Neither points to a problem with the core service.
4. **Cost pressure.** The house cut had to rise to 14 % because the sales share and the cargo bike leg cost more than planned. Margins per box are thinner than originally assumed.

### 4. Where the remaining growth could come from

Nine K-16 pods with two drops a week give 288 compartment-uses per week. At 131 boxes, that capacity is used at about 45 %, so physical space is not the constraint. The constraint is demand per building.

| Option | Mechanism | Modelled extra boxes/week | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Deepen the nine pods | More members per existing building | +10 to +20 over a quarter | Slow; per-building growth has flattened since wave 2 completed |
| B. Third wave of three buildings | New pods in waitlist buildings | +25 to +35 by month two | HA approval, winter start, coordinator load |
| C. A and B together | Both | +35 to +55 | Spreads attention thin |

Option B assumes new pods ramp to roughly 60–80 % of the current average of about 14.6 boxes per pod per week (131 ÷ 9) within two months, broadly in line with how wave 2 buildings behaved. Option A assumes modest gains from lobby hosts and member notes in buildings that have already been canvassed heavily. On these assumptions, only B and C plausibly reach 160 within a quarter.

### 5. Modelled economics of one additional pod

Per box, at current prices:

| Box | Price | House cut (14 %) | Sales share (2 %) | Contribution before delivery |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 16 € | 2.24 € | 0.32 € | 1.92 € |
| Standard | 26 € | 3.64 € | 0.52 € | 3.12 € |
| Family | 39 € | 5.46 € | 0.78 € | 4.68 € |

For the standard box the grower share is 22.36 €, comfortably above the 18 € floor.

Fixed monthly cost per pod, as I understand it:

- Lease: 85 €
- Lobby insurance: 6 €
- Caretaker box: one standard box (26 € at retail)
- Champion box for the lobby host: one small box (16 € at retail)

That is about 133 € a month with the free boxes valued at retail, somewhat less if only the grower share is counted. At about 3.12 € per standard box, a new pod covers its fixed costs at roughly 43 standard boxes a month, or about 10 a week, before driving costs and coordinator time. Coordinator time at 22 € an hour is modest, but lobby walks, HA meetings and setup could plausibly take 8–12 hours per building, i.e. around 175–265 € one-off.

For three new pods, running costs would be on the order of 400 € a month. That is small relative to the 40 000 € angel round, although I have no figures for one-off installation costs from Kestrel. I am not trying to settle how the angel money should be allocated, which I understand is still under discussion; these figures only show scale.

### 6. Risks specific to a third wave

1. **HA risk.** Each new building repeats the Alder Court exposure. Choosing buildings with a clear vote margin and an engaged prospective lobby host reduces it.
2. **Pod size.** Whether a 16-compartment pod suits smaller buildings is still open. If the waitlist buildings are small, this needs an answer before committing.
3. **Winter conditions.** A January start means cold lobbies, more reliance on frost guard and a higher chance of weather skips, each costing half a box price in make-goods. The first weeks in a new building are when members are least forgiving.
4. **Grower supply.** Winter volume from eight growers is less certain than summer volume. The grower desk should check box sheet capacity before any building is promised.
5. **Delivery cost.** Each new stop adds to the CPD. Buildings near existing stops, ideally forming a pod cluster, keep this marginal.

### 7. Recommendation

1. **Plan a third wave of three buildings, opening in January.** It is the only option in the model that reaches the north number target within a reasonable time, and the running cost is small relative to the funds available.
2. **Use October to December for lobby walks** in waitlist buildings, aiming for three HA agreements by mid-December so pods can go in during the first weeks of January.
3. **Select for clustering and vote margin**, not only waitlist size.
4. **Keep working the existing nine pods in parallel**, especially Linden Yard, which has not yet had a full month of operation.
5. **Set a review point at the end of February.** If the new pods sit below about 10 boxes a week each, pause expansion and concentrate on depth.
