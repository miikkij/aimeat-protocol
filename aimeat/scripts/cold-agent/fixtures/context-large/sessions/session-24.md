# Larder · session 24 · 23 June 2026 · Maija joins

It was Maija's first Tuesday sit-down, so we held it at the Garage instead of Rasmus's kitchen. That way she could see the pack line tables and the add-on shelf before we buried her in Backroom. It was warm enough to keep the big door half open. Rasmus brought the good coffee, a sign he was nervous about it too. We started a bit after ten and ran long, which I expected.

## What to call the job

Before anything else we had to decide what Maija actually is, at least on paper. Rasmus had been writing "ops help" in his to-do lists, and I had been saying "our part-timer," which sounds like a shift at a café. Maija said that in her last job, the person who kept the volunteers, the van bookings and the suppliers in line was simply called the coordinator. Nobody there thought twice about it. That settled it. It's the ordinary word for the role: part-time operations work for Larder, a bit of everything that isn't writing code and isn't being me. Rasmus made "coordinator" a role in Backroom while we were still talking, so it's now official in the most binding way we have.

## The walkthrough

Rasmus gave Maija the tour of Backroom on the big screen. He called it "the Ledger" exactly once, caught himself, and said that's the one slip he gets for the month. Maija said she was glad of the rename, because she'd have been scared to click anything in something called a ledger. He showed her the members, the drops, payments, pod health and the orphan rate view. Then he showed her the Field side, so she can see what the growers see when they do their drop scans.

I took her through the numbers we steer by. NN last week was 88 boxes picked up, and pickup rate was 91 %. I told her about the kill criteria line at 85 %, mostly so she knows why Rasmus checks the PUR graph before he has breakfast. She asked whether 88 was good. Rasmus said it's good and it's not 100, which is about as positive as he gets.

We went through the building champions one by one so she has faces for names. That included Oskar at Harbour Row, who is not Oskar at Kestrel; I've written that on the whiteboard for her because it will happen. Maija asked how the champion perk works in practice. I showed her where it sits in Backroom; there's nothing for her to do there. She also asked about Raimo at Alder Court and whether caretakers get anything for what they do around the pods. I said honestly that it's still open and we haven't settled it, and we left it there.

## The Mill Gate double drop, told properly this time

Maija had heard me mention "the Mill Gate thing" on the phone last week and asked what it was. I've told this story a few times now and got bits of it wrong, so here is the version I'm standing by.

It was a double drop at Mill Gate in compartment 9. I had been saying compartment 7, and that was wrong; Rasmus checked the drop scans and it's 9. Two boxes went into the one compartment. The member who opened it found both, took theirs home, and brought the second box back the next day. They didn't leave it in the lobby or keep it. They brought it back, which I'm still a bit moved by. The other member whose box it was got sorted out, and nobody went without in the end.

What we don't know is how it happened. Nobody knows yet. Rasmus's first guess was the scan, a box scanned to the wrong compartment, but the scan shows what it shows and that doesn't explain it. My guess was something at the hatch: two boxes going in through the back in the dark when they should have gone into two separate compartments. Neither of us can prove anything. The Monday sweep at Mill Gate found nothing odd with the cabinet. I also asked myself whether it was anything for the lobby insurance, and it isn't; nothing was damaged or stolen. So it sits as a mystery for now.

Maija asked what she should do if one happens on her watch. I said write down everything the same day, while people still remember: which compartment, which drop, who opened it. Then tell Rasmus before anybody touches Backroom. That's what we'd have liked to have done ourselves.

## Short boxes: the make-good goes up

This was the first real decision of the day, and it was mostly my push.

**Decided: the make-good for a short box goes up to 5 €.** The swap card stays as it is. This replaces what we decided on 17 March 2026, when we set a short box at a swap card and a make-good of 3 €.

Going back on it took some talking, so here's the reasoning. In March, 3 € seemed fair. We had one grower and very few boxes, and short boxes were rare. A missing item was usually one bunch of something, and 3 € roughly covered that. It was meant as an apology with a number on it, not a refund. Then the standard box went up to 29 €. Now 3 € looks small next to what people pay, and it looks especially small next to the rainy-day rule. A member whose drop is cancelled for weather gets half the box price back. A member who gets a box with a real hole in it gets 3 €, and from the member's side that doesn't feel like the same company being fair. Maija said the same thing from the outside, before I'd made my case. She said that if she were a member and the box was missing the thing she'd planned dinner around, 3 € would annoy her more than nothing.

Rasmus's worry was money, and it's a fair one. With the house cut at 12 %, every make-good comes straight out of a thin slice, and the floor price in each GSA means the grower's 18 € doesn't move. He ran a few what-ifs on the whiteboard and wiped them, and in the end he agreed that 5 € is the price of not losing people. We talked a little about whether a grower who comes up short should carry some of the make-good. We didn't go anywhere with it. It would mean reopening every GSA, and none of us wants that conversation this summer. It's on the parking lot, not decided.

There's also a half-idea that came out of this. When one grower is short, another often has surplus of something similar that week. If we could see that on the Wednesday, some short boxes might not be short at all. Rasmus said it's a Field question and a big one. On the parking lot.

The new make-good applies from the next box week. Rasmus changes the figure in Backroom and on the swap card template. I'll let the building champions know so nobody is surprised by the bigger credit.

## The grower desk

**Decided: Maija takes over grower onboarding from me.** This replaces the decision of 27 January 2026, which was that I onboard new growers.

That January decision made sense at the time. There was one grower, Aino at Ridgeback Farm. Onboarding meant me driving out, sitting at her kitchen table and working out the GSA together, line by line. It was half sales and half friendship, and it had to be me because there was nobody else. Then Pekka, Liisa, Juho and Kaisa came in. Each one took longer than I'd planned, not because they were difficult but because there's always a call after the call. Meanwhile I'm running the pack line every week, the Garage is a registered food premises with its own paperwork, and I still do lobby walks. Onboarding has turned into the thing I'm always half doing, and growers notice when they're half attended to.

What we noticed while talking it through is that it isn't only onboarding. New growers need the onboarding pack and someone to walk them through Field. But all five current growers also need someone who answers about box sheets, a late drop scan, a question about the commission, or Pekka asking for the fourth time why the cabinet beeps when the hatch is open. It's one area of work, and nobody owns it. Rasmus said, "So it's a desk, like a help desk, but for growers." Maija said "the grower desk" back to him, sort of trying it on, and it stuck straight away, because it says both halves: bringing growers in and looking after them once they're in. That's what Maija is taking. The grower desk is hers.

I'm not disappearing from the growers. I'll still go to the growers' circle, and Aino will still ring me about everything, which is fine. But the first answer comes from Maija from now on. Each grower gets a call from me to introduce her, so it doesn't feel like they're being handed off. Rasmus will give her a coordinator login to Field's grower view so she sees what they see. Maija asked whether she'd be signing GSAs. No: GSAs are still signed by me for Larder, and Maija does everything up to that point. She seemed relieved, honestly.

## Pay: by the hour or by the month

The last thing was the one we'd been circling all morning without saying it. How do we pay her? Maija raised it herself, which I was grateful for.

Rasmus's first instinct was a monthly fee, because it's simpler to plan around and simpler in the books. My instinct was by the hour. Nobody, Maija included, knows yet how many hours the grower desk takes in a week. Some weeks a new grower will eat two days. Other weeks it'll be a handful of calls. A monthly fee would mean one of us guessing, and whoever guessed wrong would either be paying for hours that didn't happen or getting hours for free. Maija said she'd rather be paid for what she actually does than be quietly resented for a fixed amount in a slow week, or quietly resent us in a busy one. She also said she keeps a log of her time anyway, out of habit from her last job.

**Decided: Maija is paid by the hour, 22 € an hour.** That settles the question she raised this morning, by the hour or monthly. It's by the hour, and the monthly idea is off the table.

Rasmus still wants to see how the hours land against the cash we have, the grant included. That isn't to reopen the decision but so we're not surprised. He'll set up a simple way for her to log hours, preferably in Backroom rather than yet another spreadsheet. Maija said a spreadsheet is fine for the first few weeks and we shouldn't build anything for her yet. Rasmus looked visibly torn between relief and wanting to build it anyway.
