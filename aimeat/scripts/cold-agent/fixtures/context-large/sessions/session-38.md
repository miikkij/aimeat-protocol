> ## Using a 40 000 € investment in Season 2: options
>
> *Prepared for Rasmus. It uses the figures in force at the end of the extended pilot and the 40 000 € Hanna mentioned. It assumes no outcome for the grant application.*
>
> ### 1. Where Larder stands
>
> | Item | Figure |
> |---|---|
> | Pods | 9 × K-16 (16 compartments, 4 chill cells each) |
> | Pod lease + lobby insurance | 85 € + 6 € per pod per month |
> | Box prices | Sprout 16 €, Larder 26 €, Harvest 39 € |
> | House cut | 14 % |
> | Floor price | 18 € per standard box |
> | Sales share to HAs | 2 % of each pod's box sales |
> | Boxes picked up last week (NN) | 131 (target 160) |
> | Monthly churn | 9 % |
> | Orphan rate | 3 % |
> | Coordinator | 22 € per hour |
>
> Two caveats. First, the churn figure is from August. If the cut is working, September may be lower, and several conclusions below depend on that. Second, I don't know the mix of Sprout, Larder and Harvest boxes, so I model every box as a standard box. That is roughly neutral: a Harvest box earns more house cut and a Sprout box earns less.
>
> ### 2. What the house cut covers today
>
> On a standard box at 26 €, 14 % is 3.64 €. That leaves a grower share of 22.36 €, comfortably above the 18 € floor price. The floor only starts to matter if prices fall again or a box is discounted.
>
> At 131 boxes a week, the house cut comes to about 477 € a week, or about 2 065 € a month.
>
> Modelled monthly running costs (my own assumptions are marked):
>
> | Line | Monthly | Basis |
> |---|---|---|
> | Pod lease | 765 € | 9 × 85 € |
> | Lobby insurance | 54 € | 9 × 6 € |
> | Sales share | ~295 € | 2 % of 131 × 26 € a week |
> | Coordinator | ~1 430 € | *assumed* 15 h a week |
> | Van days (twin run) | ~1 040 € | *assumed* 120 € per hire, 2 a week |
> | Cargo bike leg | ~400 € | *assumed* |
> | Champion perk and caretaker boxes | ~380 € | retail value, 9 buildings |
> | Make-goods (short boxes, rainy-day rule) | ~150 € | *assumed* |
> | **Total** | **~4 510 €** | |
>
> That leaves a monthly gap of roughly 2 450 €, and it does not pay either founder anything. At that gap, 40 000 € on its own lasts about 16 months. If the NN reaches 160, the gap narrows to roughly 2 050 € and the money lasts about 19 months. These are the two ends of the "do nothing new" case.
>
> Growth helps less than you might expect. Each extra box brings in 3.64 € of house cut minus 0.52 € of sales share, about 3.12 € in all, while most costs are fixed.
>
> One useful consequence: 160 boxes a week does not need a single new pod. Nine K-16s on a twin run offer 288 compartment-slots a week, 72 of them in chill cells. The constraint is members, not hardware.
>
> ### 3. Options
>
> **1. Hold most of it as a reserve.**
> Spend little and keep the money as cover against bad weeks: a churn rebound, a wet autumn full of rainy-day rule make-goods, or a change to the lease. This is the lowest-risk option and the easiest to reverse. It does nothing to close the gap; it only buys time.
>
> **2. More coordinator hours.**
> Every extra 10 hours a week of coordinator time costs about 950 € a month. It mainly buys back Ilona's time and makes the grower desk sturdier (chasing box sheets, onboarding growers). Its effect on the NN is indirect. It is easy to scale up or down.
>
> **3. The Android build and Picker.**
> Moving Android members onto the QR stub cuts wake code SMS spend, which grows with every member. Most of the cost is one-off: contractor time, or Rasmus's time taken from other work. The monthly saving is modest but permanent, and it improves the pickup experience. Build overruns are common, so a fixed budget would help.
>
> **4. Reliability work.**
> This means hardening the chain from scan to compartment: ghost drop detection, door pulse retries, and a check that each back-load puts the box into the compartment Field recorded. Every failure costs a make-good and some trust. This option is cheap compared with options 2 and 3 and is mostly Rasmus's time. It protects churn, which is arguably the number that matters most.
>
> **5. Member growth in the existing buildings.**
> Since hardware is not the bottleneck, the money could go into finding members in the nine current buildings: printed material in lobbies, support for lobby hosts, and short pass promotions. A short pass at the Larder tier brings in 208 € up front, which also helps cash flow. Of all the options, this has the most direct effect on the NN, and it is the hardest to predict.
>
> **6. Buying pods instead of leasing.**
> Lease and insurance together cost about 9 800 € a year for nine pods. Buying would only make sense if the purchase price per pod were low enough to pay back within Season 2, and it would tie up much of the 40 000 €. I would treat this as a contingency rather than a plan, with the Brightlatch quote as a reference point.
>
> **7. A grower cushion.**
> This would be a small fund to keep growers whole in bad weeks, for example weeks of weather skips. It builds loyalty among the eight growers. It does not move any metric directly, and it could turn into an expectation that is hard to withdraw.
>
> ### 4. Comparison
>
> | Option | Rough cost | Effect on NN | Effect on churn | Reversible? |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 1 Reserve | none | none | none | fully |
> | 2 Coordinator hours | ~950 €/month per 10 h | indirect | small | yes |
> | 3 Android build | one-off | small | small | no (sunk) |
> | 4 Reliability | low | small | positive | mostly sunk |
> | 5 Member growth | variable | direct | unclear | yes |
> | 6 Buy pods | large | none | none | hard |
> | 7 Grower cushion | variable | none | none | hard to withdraw |
>
> ### 5. Packages to discuss
>
> The options can be combined, and I don't think the figures point to a single one:
>
> - **Patient:** mostly option 1, with option 4 on top. This buys the most time but makes slow progress on the NN.
> - **Operational:** options 2, 3 and 4. This lowers day-to-day friction and the cost per member, and leaves growth to word of mouth.
> - **Growth:** option 5 with some of option 2. This is the fastest route towards 160 and carries the highest risk of spending without a lasting result.
>
> ### 6. What this note can't answer
>
> - What terms come with the money, and whether they affect how fast it should be spent.
> - Whether September churn confirms the effect of the cut. If it does, the reserve case looks less necessary.
> - Several open operating questions, such as the summer setpoint, change costs at the margin but are outside this note.
