# Larder · session 7 · 24 February 2026 · Logistics: the first loop

We started late because Ilona came straight from the Garage with sleet melting off her jacket and a bag of Aino's carrots she wanted me to taste. They were good. The wind was coming hard off the harbour all morning and the windows in my flat rattled for the whole session, which felt right for a meeting about driving around Lakeport in the dark.

Logistics has been sitting on the red list for two weeks under the vague heading "how do boxes get into pods". We've spent most of January and February on the front end of the bet: lobby walks, the GSA with Ridgeback, Picker, the Ledger. Nobody has actually worked out how a box gets from Aino's field into a compartment. Today we did the first pass at that, and we got three decisions out of it.

## Naming the thing

We had no word for the delivery itself, so the first twenty minutes went in circles. Ilona kept calling it "the morning loop", because in her head it's a round you do in the morning that comes back to where it started. I kept saying "the delivery" and then "the run", and at some point I said "the dawn run", because in February in Lakeport it will be done in the dark or close to it. That one stuck. Ilona said "fine, dawn run, but it's still a loop", and she's right about that too.

So for the record: the **dawn run** is the morning delivery round that fills the pods with that day's drops. I've started writing it as **DR** on the whiteboard, and Ilona has already used DR in a message to Aino, so it's in circulation. "Morning loop" is the same thing. Ilona still says it about half the time and I'm not going to police it.

**Loop** has become its own word, separate from the dawn run. A loop is a fixed route that visits a set of pods in a set order. The dawn run is the event, the loop is the route. Right now there is only one loop, because there are only going to be four pods. But Ilona made the point that once there are more pods we might have more than one loop, and the DR would then be "do loop A, then loop B" or two people doing one each. I liked keeping the words apart for that reason. Every pod on a loop is a **stop**, the way any courier would say it. Pod zero at Alder Court will be one stop; the other three stops depend on where the remaining lobby slots land.

The thing that goes with a loop is the **loop sheet**: a printed list of the loop's stops, in order, with the compartment of each box at each stop. Ilona wanted it on paper. Her argument: phones die in the cold, and phones are awkward when you're carrying a box with both hands. My first instinct was to make it a screen in the Ledger. We settled on both: the Ledger produces it, the van gets a printout. I'll do the layout this week. It needs to be readable at arm's length under a lobby light, so big compartment numbers, member names small or not at all. I'm not sure we even want names on it. I put that on the parking lot rather than deciding it now.

## Delivery day

**Decision: the dawn run is on Tuesdays.**

We spent the longest on this, and I'm writing the reasoning down properly because I suspect we'll be asked about it.

The box week runs Monday to Sunday. A Tuesday drop puts the box early in its own box week, so a member has the box in the fridge for most of the week it belongs to. A Friday or Saturday drop would mean the box mostly gets eaten in the next box week. That isn't fatal, but it's confusing to explain in Picker and confusing in the Ledger.

Monday was the obvious alternative. Ilona was against it for the grower side. From her conversations with Aino, Monday is when a small farm deals with whatever the weekend did to it, and harvesting, packing and loading all by Monday dawn would mean doing it on Sunday. Ilona said, and I quote, "I am not signing the first grower up for Sundays." With a Tuesday dawn run the grower gets Monday to harvest and pack, and the box sheet can come in before that without anyone working the weekend.

Ilona also raised the lobby side. Mondays feel like the heaviest day in a building: people back from weekends, deliveries piling up, caretakers catching up. A Tuesday drop lands on a quieter morning. That's a guess, not something we've measured, and she said so. She's going to ask Raimo what an Alder Court lobby looks like early on a Tuesday compared with a Monday, since he's the only caretaker we can just ask.

Then there's the window. With the pickup window at 24 hours from the drop, a Tuesday DR means everything should be picked up by Wednesday morning, and any orphan box goes to the caretaker midweek rather than on a weekend when the caretaker may not be around. Raimo works weekdays as far as we know. I added that to the list of reasons, and Ilona wondered aloud whether that should have been the main reason all along.

We only half joked about the last point: the Tuesday sit-down is Tuesday. Doing the DR on the same morning means we come to the sit-down with the run fresh in our heads, and whatever went wrong gets written down within hours. It also means some Tuesday sit-downs will start late and be held by two tired people. We accepted that.

We talked briefly about running two drop days a week and dropped it. Four pods, one grower, no data. One DR a week is plenty to learn from.

## Hatches, back-loading and front-picking

This part came out of me reading the K-12 manual again over the weekend. I wanted to know how we physically put a box into a cell without the van driver standing in front of the pod opening members' doors one by one. It turns out the K-12 can be loaded from behind. The rear loading door is called the **hatch** in Kestrel's manual, and Ilona and I just took that word. Opening the hatch gives access to the backs of the compartments, so whoever does the dawn run can fill them without opening the members' doors at all.

Ilona named that **back-load**: filling compartments from the hatch side, without touching the front. As soon as she said it I said the other half has to be **front-pick**, which is a member opening their compartment from the front with Picker. Back-load in the morning, front-pick during the day. It's a tidy pair, and it cleanly separates our side of the pod from the member's side, which I think will matter for how the Ledger logs things: a back-load event and a front-pick event are different things that happen to the same compartment.

The catch is space. The hatch needs room behind the pod to open and for a person to stand there with a box. That depends on the lobby slot. Some of the lobby slots we saw on lobby walks were really just a stretch of wall. Ilona's worry is that we've been telling HAs "it's about the size of a wardrobe", and a wardrobe that needs a person standing behind it is a different conversation. We did not decide anything about this. I'm going to ask Oskar whether the hatch on a K-12 can be on a side, what clearance Kestrel recommends, and whether the plinth changes anything. Ilona will take a tape measure to the Alder Court slot with Raimo. If back-loading doesn't work at some stop, the fallback is loading from the front through the members' doors. Nobody likes it, but it isn't impossible.

I also realised during this part that the dawn run is the last metre. It's the whole of it, every week, done by one of us. Everything we've drawn so far has a little arrow labelled "drop" and nothing behind the arrow. Today was about what's behind the arrow.

## Packing

**Decision: growers pack their own boxes at the farm.**

The alternative was packing at the Garage. Bulk vegetables come in from the grower, and one of us weighs and divides them into boxes. Ilona considered it seriously for about ten minutes, because it would give us control over what's in each box and let us even things out. Then she talked herself out of it.

The reasons:

- **Time.** On a Tuesday dawn run, packing at the Garage means a night shift before a morning shift, done by the same two people.
- **Handling.** Every extra time a carrot is put down and picked up is a chance for it to bruise or get mixed up. The grower already handles everything once, at harvest.
- **The grower knows the box.** The box sheet comes from the grower. The person who wrote the box sheet should be the one making it true.
- **The Garage isn't set up for it.** Ilona was blunt about this. It's a garage. There's no washable surface, no cold room, and nowhere to put a rejected crate.

I raised the food-premises question, since packing anywhere touches it. We agreed that farm packing doesn't settle it either way. The question is still open, and the decision on packing doesn't depend on its answer. I mention it here so nobody later reads this as "we decided packing at the farm avoids registration". We didn't.

What it does mean is that the onboarding pack needs a section on packing. Ilona owns that, since she does grower onboarding. It should cover what a packed box looks like, how it's labelled so whoever does the DR can match it to a compartment without thinking, and when it needs to be ready on a Monday evening or before the run. She'll go through it with Aino first, since Aino is our only grower and will in effect write the first version of it with us. Labelling got as far as "the label should show the stop and the compartment from the loop sheet". The actual format is on my list.

Ilona slipped into "customer" again while we were talking about labels ("the customer shouldn't see anything weird on the box"). I pointed it out, she rolled her eyes and said "member, member". It's nearly gone from her vocabulary.

## The van

**Decision: the dawn run uses a hired van on van day.**

"Van day" was Ilona's phrase. She said it while thinking out loud about the weekly rhythm ("so Monday is box sheet day, Tuesday is van day"), and it immediately sounded like a thing. Van day is the day a van is hired for the dawn run. With the DR on Tuesdays, van day is Tuesday for now, but we kept the word separate on purpose. If the DR ever moves, van day moves with it, and if we ever need a van for something other than the DR (a pod install, a crate pickup), that isn't van day.

Reasons for hiring rather than buying or leasing:

- We don't want a vehicle sitting idle six days a week while we're testing the bet with four pods.
- A hire van is someone else's problem when it breaks.
- Neither of us has a vehicle that would fit a week's worth of boxes from Aino plus the loop.
- If the pilot fails, a hired van just means we stop hiring. There's nothing to sell.

The open questions about the van, none of which we answered: who drives (probably whoever isn't writing code that week, which in practice means Ilona), whether we collect the van the evening before or on the morning, and whether the van goes to Ridgeback first or Aino drives to the Garage. We left that last one for Ilona to talk through with Aino before we decide it. We also talked about the chill cells. In February, nobody worries about vegetables getting warm in a van for a morning. In June they will. Ilona wondered about coolboxes in the van, and that went on the parking lot along with a half-idea I had about whether the DR could ever be done without a van at all. It's too early for either.

Ilona will phone around for hire options and what a weekly hire looks like. No prices yet, so nothing goes into the box economics until she has real numbers. I'm noting that the 24 € standard box price was set before we knew what the dawn run costs. Neither of us thinks that means changing it; it just means we'll find out how much room the house cut has once van day has a price.

## Where this leaves the first loop

On paper, the whole thing now looks like this:

1. Grower sends the box sheet.
2. Grower harvests and packs at the farm on Monday.
3. Van day (Tuesday): someone picks up the hired van, collects the boxes, and drives the loop with the loop sheet.
4. At each stop: open the hatch, back-load each box into its compartment per the loop sheet, close the hatch.
5. Members front-pick through the day with Picker.
6. Twenty-four hours after each drop the window closes, and whatever is left goes to the caretaker.

Ilona drew it on the whiteboard as a circle and wrote "first loop" in the middle. I took a photo of it before the sleet came back and she left to meet a grower prospect on the phone. It's the first time the whole thing has looked like a service rather than a pod with an app attached.

It also tells us something about the north number. Every box picked up starts as a box that was back-loaded on a Tuesday. If the DR doesn't happen, NN for that week is zero, however good Picker is. Ilona said, "so the van is the product", and I'd like that on a wall somewhere.
