# Larder · session 12 · 31 March 2026 · Prices and box sizes

We did the dawn run first, so the sit-down started late, past ten, at the bench in the Garage with the heater on and sleet blowing in under the door every time someone came or went. Rasmus had a cold, and I had my lists. It was a long session but a good one: we finally sat down with the box price instead of circling it.

## Going back on 24 €

On 27 January we set the standard box at 24 €. At the time it felt right. It was a clean number, it sounded like a weekly treat rather than a bill, and we only had Aino's figures to go on. Then we found out what 24 € actually does. The first thing to give was the house cut. At 20 % the grower share left Aino less than her market stall pays, so we dropped it to 15 % within minutes of hearing that. That fixed Aino's side, but it left us very little.

Since then two things have piled on top. Pekka and Liisa both came back with what packing at the farm really costs them, and it's more than Aino's numbers suggested. The bags, the labour on drop-day eve and the extra handling are all real, and none of it was in our January sums. Then there's the lobby fee: 40 € a month per pod, four pods, every month whether a pod is full or half empty. At 24 € we can pay the growers fairly or cover the lobby fee. We can't do both without the house cut going to nothing.

Rasmus argued the other side for a while, honestly, which I was glad of. His worry is that we've told 38 members a price and now we're changing it a few weeks after first drop. People remember the first price. I said the bet was never that people want cheap vegetables. It's that they'll collect from their own lobby if it's open whenever they get home. If 27 € kills it, we've learned something real. If 24 € kills us, we've learned nothing except that we can't do sums.

**Decision: the standard box goes up to 27 €.** This replaces the 24 € we set on 27 January. The reason is to cover the growers' costs and the lobby fee. The house cut stays at 15 %, so the grower share rises with the price, and that's the point.

We both said, more or less at the same time, that we don't want to do this again in a month. So whatever comes next on price, it has to be argued harder than 24 € was.

## The half

The small box has been hovering for weeks. Oskar from Harbour Row (our champion there, not Kestrel's Oskar) has passed on the same thing three times now. A lot of his neighbours live alone, and a standard box is simply too much food for one person. Some of them would rather not sign up than throw half of it away. I've seen the same at Alder Court. The honesty shelf mostly sells two or three things at a time, a bunch of something and a few onions, which says to me that people want smaller amounts more often than they want a big haul.

Rasmus has been calling it "the half" since he sketched it on the whiteboard a fortnight ago, because the first drawing was literally a standard box with a line down the middle. I kept saying small box. By the end of today we were using both without noticing. I'll write "small box" when it's for anyone outside the two of us, but in these notes it'll be "the half" half the time.

The price was the harder part. My first instinct was half of 27 €, but almost nothing about a box halves with it. It still takes one compartment and one wake code. It's still one stop's worth of carrying on the morning loop, and one more line on the grower's packing table. Rasmus put it well: "the cost is in the box existing, not in what's in it." So it can't be half price, and it can't be so close to the standard box that nobody sees the point.

We talked through a couple of numbers and kept coming back to the same one.

**Decision: we add a small box (the half) at 17 €.**

The reasoning, so I don't forget it:
- It covers the same fixed costs as a standard box: compartment, drop, packing time.
- It's clearly cheaper than 27 € at a glance, which matters for the people Oskar is talking about.
- The grower share follows the same 15 % house cut, so growers aren't asked to do smaller boxes for worse terms.

Things this touches that we noted but didn't settle:
- Box sheets. Each grower now has to say what goes into both sizes. Liisa will be fine; she already thinks in two sizes for her market crates. Pekka will grumble, because he likes one list. I'll talk him through it rather than send something.
- Compartments. Rasmus wondered aloud whether two halves could share a cell. I said no, one member, one compartment, and he agreed straight away. He was mostly thinking out loud about chill cell space.
- PUR. Rasmus wants pickup rate split by box size in the Ledger from the first week the half exists. If people who live alone are the ones who forget to collect, we'll see it in orphan box counts before we see it anywhere else, and I'd rather know early.

## Box tiers, drip plan, skip week

While Rasmus was mocking up the Picker screen with two sizes on it, he started calling the choice "box tiers", as in "the box tiers sit above the payment bit". It stuck because we needed one word for "the sizes on offer" that doesn't sound like a supermarket. Right now there are two box tiers: standard and the half. We haven't touched what they'll be called in Picker; "standard box" and "small box" are just our working words for the moment.

Below the box tiers, on the same sketch, he had the season pass on one side and plain weekly payment on the other. He wrote "drip plan" over the weekly one because he needed a label that fit the box on the sketch, and "pay as you go" didn't. I laughed, but it's actually a good name. It's paying week by week, a drip rather than a lump, with nothing up front. That's how most of our members pay today; we just never had a word for it next to the season pass. It's a name, not a change.

Then the word "skip week" came out of nowhere, from me this time. A few members have asked how they pause their box when they're away. So far we've handled it by hand, which won't last at 38 members, let alone 100. A skip week is a week a member pauses their box. What happens to the grower's numbers on a skip week, how much notice a member has to give, and whether season pass members can skip at all were all far too much for today. **Skip week goes on the parking lot**, with a note that it needs to come back before it becomes a habit we handle by text message.

## Cabinets

Small thing, but I want it written down. Pekka rang during the run this morning about his box sheet. Twice in that call he said "the cabinet", as in "how many fit in the cabinet". Liisa said "cabinet" on Friday as well, and I'd let it go. So the growers have their own word for the pods. Rasmus said we now have pod, cabinet, compartment and cell, "more words than pods". It doesn't bother me. When I'm talking to growers I'll say cabinet if that's what they say. It's their mental picture of the thing, and correcting them would just be odd.

## Members

Rasmus read the number out of the Ledger before we started: **38 members**. That's honest progress from first drop, but the north number is boxes picked up per week, and the pilot's target is 100. We're nowhere near that yet. The half is partly a bet on that gap. Not "the bet", a smaller one: that there are single households in every one of our buildings who looked at a standard box and said no. We'll find out.

We spent a while on how the price change lands with the 38. Rasmus will write a member note per building rather than one message to everyone, because the champions will get the questions anyway, and they should see it coming. Oskar at Harbour Row in particular will want to hear about the half before his neighbours do. I'll ring him myself.

## The grant

I brought the Lakeport food-innovation grant. I'd seen it mentioned and finally read the page on Sunday. I haven't gone through the whole call properly yet, but we fit the shape of it: local growers, less food going to waste, something new in how people get vegetables.

Rasmus's first question was whether this breaks the no-investors rule. It doesn't. A grant isn't someone owning a piece of Larder, and that was the whole reason for the rule. Hanna's angel group is still not something we want during the pilot, grant or no grant. We'll tell Hanna we're applying, though, because she'll have opinions on how to write it, and they'll be good ones.

**Decision: Larder applies for the Lakeport food-innovation grant.**

I'll write it. Rasmus will pull whatever numbers it needs from the Ledger: members, boxes, drops, PUR so far. I suspect the food premises registration for the Garage will come up somewhere in the application. That's another reason to get it all tidied in one folder rather than in four email threads.

## Lease or buy

The grant set off a longer conversation that we didn't finish. If money ever came in, would we keep leasing the pods from Kestrel, or buy them?

Rasmus leans towards leasing, at least while the pilot runs. If the kill criteria trigger, a leased pod goes back on a truck and that's the end of it. Kestrel also deals with spine faults, and he's had one heartbeat scare already that he didn't want to fix himself. I lean the other way, mostly on feel. A lease payment never ends, and every month it does we've paid for a pod we don't own. If the bet holds and we're still doing this in a year, buying looks very different. I also pointed out that owning the pods makes the insurance question even more ours. That one is still open, and we didn't solve it today either.

Neither of us moved the other. **Lease or buy stays open.** Rasmus will write up what we'd actually need to know to decide, and we'll come back to it. I don't want to ring Oskar at Kestrel about it until we know exactly what we're asking, because a half-formed question to a supplier tends to come back as a quote.
