# Larder · session 26 · 7 July 2026 · Nine pods

It was hot from the start today. The Garage was already warm at half past five, so we sat with the door rolled up and the fan pointed at the add-on shelf, which Maija said was the most valuable thing in the room. Rasmus brought cold coffee in a jam jar, and it was gone in twenty minutes. All three of us were here: Rasmus, Maija and me.

## Nine pods, not six

We started with the question we have been circling since the midsummer review: do we bring the rest of wave 2 in now or keep waiting?

On 12 May we decided the pilot would grow to 6 pods, with Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard joining the four wave 1 buildings. Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 stayed on the "later" side of the line. I still think that was the right call for May. We had five growers, and I wasn't sure they could carry a Thursday for six pods, let alone nine. Packing had only just moved to the Garage. The cargo bike leg was still something Rasmus drew on napkins. I didn't want to promise compartments to three more HAs before I knew we could fill them and still make the seven o'clock line. Six pods was the most I thought we could run without one of us doing the outer loop twice on a Thursday morning.

So we went back on it today, on purpose, and I want the reasons written down so we don't later pretend we drifted into it.

- **Supply.** We now have seven growers (more below). In May the worry was mostly whether there would be enough vegetables. That worry is mostly gone.
- **The extension.** The pilot now runs to 30 September and the north number target is 160. Rasmus put it bluntly: on six pods, 160 needs nearly every compartment filled every week. Then every skip week and every empty cell becomes a crisis. Nine pods gives us room.
- **The pack line holds.** Wednesday evenings in the Garage have settled into a rhythm. Maija's caution, and she's right, is that more pods on one pack line evening is exactly where double drops come from. We agreed that's an argument for being careful, not for waiting.
- **The HAs are waiting.** Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 have been "later" since the lobby walks. I don't want to lose a lobby slot because we made a keen HA wait a whole summer. The sales share makes it easy for them to say yes, but easy yeses go stale.
- **Cost.** Three more leases at 85 € a month each, plus Kestrel's lobby insurance at 6 € a month per pod. That isn't nothing. With the grant money in the account, it doesn't keep me awake.

Rasmus's arguments against were practical, not fundamental. Pod health gets busier. Three more spines send heartbeats he has to babysit. Backroom has a few places where "six" is more or less hard-coded in his head, if not in the code. He also doesn't want the order to Kestrel held up while he waits for Brightlatch, who still haven't sent a quote. He was clear that the Brightlatch question is a separate track and doesn't change this decision.

**Decision: the pilot grows to 9 pods. Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 join, which completes wave 2. This replaces the 12 May decision of 6 pods.** All three get K-16s with the green Cropbox shroud, like the others.

Maija asked straight out whether a K-16 is too much pod for some of these buildings. That's still the open question it was, and we didn't settle it today. We noted it again and moved on, because we'll learn more once the three pods are standing than from arguing about it in a garage.

Each of the new buildings needs a building champion before its first drop. Oskar from Harbour Row, our champion there, not Oskar at Kestrel, texted last week offering to have a coffee with whoever we find. I'd like to take him up on that, because he explains Cropbox to neighbours better than I do.

### A third wave?

As soon as nine was agreed, Maija asked what comes after: a third wave of buildings this year? The waitlist keeps growing in places we haven't walked yet, and she's the one answering those emails. I would like a third wave. Rasmus would like to survive the next nine weeks first. We didn't decide anything, and that was deliberate. It's an open question, and I've written it at the top of the red list's sister page so it doesn't vanish.

## Pod clusters

This came out of Maija and Rasmus bent over the draft loop sheets for Thursday with three new stops added. Rasmus's guess is that the new buildings will end up on the outer loop rather than the cargo bike leg, though that's a guess until Maija drives it. While he traced the route with a pencil, he pointed out that at least one pair of buildings looked close enough that the van could park once and someone could trolley boxes to both pods. He called it "a cluster", server-room language as usual. Maija wrote **pod cluster** in the margin of the loop sheet, because "the two-buildings-one-stop thing" wouldn't fit. By the end of the session all three of us were saying it.

So a pod cluster is pods in neighbouring buildings that one stop on a loop can serve. Park once, walk the crates, load both hatches, drive on. The appeal is obvious. Every stop we save on a Thursday morning is time we get back against the seven o'clock line.

We didn't decide which pods form clusters. On the map, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 look like the best candidate, but maps lie about kerbs, steps and fob doors. Maija will walk it with a trolley before we put it on a loop sheet. Rasmus wondered whether Backroom should know about clusters, so a loop sheet could print one stop with two pods under it. I asked him not to build anything until Maija has actually pushed a trolley between two buildings.

## The swap market

Helmi's question was roughly this: "If I've got more of something than the box sheets need, and one of the others is short, can't we just trade?" She told Maija the growers already do this informally. Pekka rang Liisa a few weeks ago to fill a gap on his box sheet, and apparently that's not the first time. Helmi called what she wanted "a little swap market between us". Maija wrote it down, brought it today, and by the time I'd said "the swap market" three times it had become the name.

The swap market, as we talked about it, is a page where growers offer their surplus to each other. Kettle Hill has too much of one thing, Saltmeadow is short of it, they find each other on the page, and a member gets a full box instead of a swap card. If that happens even occasionally, we pay out fewer 5 € make-goods for short boxes and have fewer awkward member notes. That alone was enough to interest me.

Then Rasmus and I disagreed, pleasantly, for about forty minutes.

**My view:** this is Larder's next product. Seven growers swapping among themselves is the small version. The big version is every small grower around Lakeport, including ones who will never supply us, using a place we run to move surplus. Growers trust us now. They see Field every week. Nobody else is building this for them. I got carried away enough to mention restaurants, and Rasmus said "no" before I finished the sentence.

**Rasmus's view:** it's a feature of Backroom. A table with a grower, an item, an amount and a "taken" button. He can build it in an evening or two, because the grower accounts, the logins and the Field connection already exist. He doesn't want to call it a product, because a product needs a name, a page, support and a price, and then it's another thing to look after during a pilot that already has nine pods. "The Ledger already does half of it," he said, then "Backroom, sorry, Backroom". Maija laughed at him for that.

Maija sided with Rasmus on how to build it and with me on why it matters. I think that's the honest position, and it's roughly where we ended up.

**Decision: the swap market is built as a page in Backroom for growers, with no fee for now.**

Why no fee:
- The growers' circle only recently pushed the house cut down to 12 %. Putting a commission on the first new thing we hand them would undo that goodwill straight away.
- We don't know yet whether anyone will use it. A fee would make sure nobody does.
- It helps our own boxes, which is reason enough to run it at cost for a while.

How the produce physically moves between growers is still loose. They have been sorting that out themselves by van and phone, and for now they can keep doing so. Rasmus wants to know whether an offer should show up in Field as well as Backroom, since growers live in Field. That's a design question for him, not a decision for today.

**Open: should the swap market become a product of its own?** I still think yes. Rasmus still thinks "let's see if anyone presses the button". We left it there, and I've stopped arguing until there's data.
