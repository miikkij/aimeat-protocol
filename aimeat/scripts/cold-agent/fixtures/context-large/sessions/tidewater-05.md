# Tidewater (board game) · playtest 5 · 23 August 2026 · One pilot

Sunday, 23 August 2026, at mine again. Pauli, Venla and me. Hot and still, windows open, the neighbour's lawnmower going for most of the first hour, which Venla said was the perfect soundtrack for a game about weather. Coffee, then more coffee, then Pauli went out for ice cream halfway through the second game.

I came into this one with two things I wanted to look at properly: the pilots and the reef. Both have been bothering me since the last session, and both turned out to be connected, which I had half suspected but hadn't said out loud.

## The pilot problem

Since 19 April every player has had one pilot. At the time that felt obviously right: it was symmetrical, nobody felt cheated, and it meant every captain sheet started from the same place. We didn't really argue about it then. It just seemed fair.

What we've seen since, and what was very clear in the first game today, is that with a pilot each, the fog banks stop meaning anything. Everyone has a way through, so everyone goes through. The board might as well not have fog on it. Pauli put it bluntly after the first game: "I didn't plan a single route. I just pointed the pilot at the fog and went." Venla agreed. She'd been trying to build a beacon on the far harbour tile behind the northern fog bank, which is supposed to be the hard, lonely, high-reward corner, and she got there with no trouble at all because the pilot simply walked her in.

So the fog was decorative, and worse, the pilots were boring. Nobody wanted them or feared anyone else having them, because everyone had one.

We talked through a few options:

- Pilots cost a cargo card to use. Pauli liked this, but it adds another payment to a game that already has too many small payments (see the reef, below).
- Pilots only usable every other loop. Felt fiddly. Nobody wanted another thing to track next to the tide track.
- One pilot total, shared somehow.

The third one is where we spent the time. The question was who gets it. Venla suggested it goes to whoever holds the champion token, and it moves with the token. My first reaction was that this is rich-get-richer: the leader gets the best piece on the board, so the leader stays the leader. Pauli had the same worry.

But we played the second game that way and it didn't go like that. What happened is that the pilot became the thing everyone was fighting over. The moment Pauli took the lead, Venla and I both started looking at how to knock him off it, partly for the points but mostly to get the pilot back. The leader having the pilot also makes the leader predictable. You know they can cut through the fog, so you can guess where they're heading and get there first or clog the harbour tile. Being champion stopped being a comfortable place to sit.

Also, and this is the part I like most, the fog banks matter again. If you aren't champion, the fog is a wall, and the corners behind it are genuinely hard to reach. The board has shape again.

**Decision: there is only one pilot on the board, and it goes to whoever holds the champion token.** This replaces the 19 April decision that each player has one pilot. The reason we're going back on it: a pilot each made the fog banks pointless and made the pilot itself uninteresting. One pilot tied to the champion token gives the fog its teeth back and gives everyone a reason to go after the leader.

Small worry I'm keeping an eye on: in a two-player game, the champion token might swing back and forth so often that the pilot just ping-pongs. We didn't test that today. Parking lot.

## The reef

Since 19 April, passing the reef costs two cargo cards. On the same day we raised the question of whether the reef is too punishing, and we've been carrying that question around ever since without answering it. Back then two cards seemed right because we wanted the reef to feel dangerous; the ring around the centre was meant to be something you think hard about before crossing.

It's too much. Today made that plain. In the first game, across the whole thing, nobody crossed the reef on purpose. Pauli crossed it once because a wave pushed him into a position where there was no other route, and he was furious about it, and he was right to be. The middle of the board went dead. Everyone routed around the outside, which, combined with the pilots, meant we all basically sailed in a circle with the occasional dart through a fog bank. The board felt like a doughnut.

Two cards is also a lot relative to what a hold carries. Paying two to cross means arriving with very little, so there was no point going.

Venla raised the obvious thing: we also changed the pilot rule this afternoon, and with only one pilot around, non-champions will need some other way to get across the board quickly. If the fog is a wall again and the reef is a wall too, the non-leaders are boxed in. So the two changes really have to go together.

We played the second game at one cargo card and the difference was immediate. People crossed. Not every time, but it became a real choice: is getting through the middle worth one card? Sometimes yes, sometimes no. That's what it was meant to be all along.

**Decision: passing the reef costs one cargo card instead of two.** This replaces the 19 April decision that it costs two. Reason: at two cards nobody chose to cross, the centre of the board died, and with the single pilot now in place the non-champions need a usable route across. At one card the reef is still a cost you feel, but it's a decision rather than a punishment.

That also settles the question from 19 April, "Is the reef too punishing?" Yes, at two cards it was, and the answer is to drop the cost to one cargo card. Crossing that off the open list after four months.

## Salvage

New word today. In the second game, Venla had dropped a cargo card on a harbour tile early and then got pulled away by the storm deck (two nasty waves in a row on her side) and never came back for it. It was sitting there as an orphan, about to cost her at the end. Pauli sailed in, looked at it, and asked if he could just take it. Venla said, "So you're salvaging my wreck?" and Pauli said yes, exactly, salvage.

We tried it for the rest of that game: salvage means taking an orphan into your hold, and you get one point for it. The word stuck immediately because it fits the theme so well. Nobody had to think about what it meant.

What I liked about it in play:

- It gives a reason to visit harbour tiles that would otherwise be ignored.
- It softens the orphan penalty a little for the table, without letting the person who dropped it off the hook. I need to think about that more carefully, though. If Pauli salvages Venla's orphan, it's no longer an orphan, so does Venla still lose the point? Today we played it as: once salvaged, it's in Pauli's hold, it's his cargo now, and it's not an orphan any more. That felt right but I want to write it out properly before the next session.
- It made Pauli stop and think about hold space, which is good. He had to leave something else behind once.

Pauli joked that salvage plus the cheaper reef means he'll spend his whole game as a scrap dealer circling the hub. Possibly true. We'll see.
