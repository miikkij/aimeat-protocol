# Larder · session 15 · 21 April 2026 · Where orphan boxes go

Ilona came back from the dawn run soaked. The rain off the bay had been coming in sideways since six, and the van's wipers were losing. We started late, a bit after ten, in the Garage with the heater on and too much coffee. Hilda from the Harbour Pantry joined us for the first hour. Two real subjects today. The first has sat on the red list for three weeks running: where orphan boxes go.

## Orphan boxes: going back on 3 February

On 3 February we decided that an OB is left with the building's caretaker. At the time it looked like the obvious answer. The caretaker already holds the lobby keys and is in the building most weekdays. The idea was that a member who missed the window could knock on a door and still get their vegetables instead of losing them. We had one pod in our heads back then, and Raimo at Alder Court had been easy to work with on everything to do with pod zero. Asking him to mind the odd box felt like a small favour. In hindsight, nobody actually asked a caretaker whether they wanted a box of leeks on their desk every Wednesday.

Since first drop we've found out. Raimo has been polite about it but clear. He has no fridge and no shelf that isn't already full. He doesn't want to be the person a member argues with about a box that's gone limp overnight. He also pointed out, fairly, that once a box is in his room he's responsible for it, and he never agreed to that. The other wave 1 caretakers have said much the same, some less politely. On two lobby walks this month Ilona was asked "and who takes the leftovers?" before she'd even got round to the lobby slot. The caretaker rule was costing us goodwill in exactly the place where we can least afford to lose it. Ilona put it as: "We built the whole thing on caretakers liking us, and then handed them our leftovers to deal with."

Then there's the waste. A few of the boxes Raimo did take in ended up in the bio bin because nobody came back for them. That bothered both of us more than the logistics did. Good vegetables from Aino and Pekka going to compost is a bad look and a worse feeling.

We talked for a while about the alternatives before Hilda arrived:

- **Leave the box in the cell for another day.** That blocks a compartment for the next box week and doesn't solve anything if the member never shows. It also quietly makes the window longer than 24 hours, which we're not reopening today.
- **Send it back to the grower.** Nobody wants a day-old box back at the farm, and it's a pointless extra trip.
- **Give it to a neighbour.** It's tempting, but it gets messy fast: who decides, and who gets told. We left it alone.
- **A food bank.** This was Ilona's suggestion from a couple of weeks ago, and why Hilda was here.

So the decision, and it **replaces the one from 3 February**:

- **Orphan boxes go to the Harbour Pantry food bank, on the orphan run. They are no longer left with the caretaker.**
- Why: the caretakers did not want to store them, and we would rather the food got eaten than thrown away.

Nothing changes on the member's side of the payment today. The box was charged on drop day as always. We didn't get into whether a member who misses a box should hear about where it went, and we didn't get into make-goods for it either.

One thing we were careful about: this does not answer the other caretaker question that's still open, whether caretakers should get something for helping with the pods at all. Taking the OBs off their hands makes that question smaller, if anything, but we didn't touch it today. Ilona will tell Raimo herself this week. She thinks he'll be relieved.

## Hilda and the Harbour Pantry

Ilona rang the Harbour Pantry cold last week, and Hilda, who looks after what comes in through their door, said she'd rather come and see us than talk on the phone. Fair enough. She'd walked past the Alder Court pod on her way and recognised the green shroud, which pleased Ilona more than it probably should have.

Hilda's questions were practical, and better than ours:

- What's actually in a box? Fresh vegetables from named local growers, packed at the farm. She liked that we can say which farm.
- How long has it been sitting? A box becomes an orphan when its window closes, so it's a day past the drop, give or take the loop order.
- Was it kept cold? I pulled up last Tuesday's cold chain log for Alder Court in the Ledger. She was more interested than I expected. She said most of what comes in to them arrives with no history at all, and a temperature record is a luxury.
- Does a box from a dry cell come out different from one in a chill cell after a day? Honest answer: it depends what's in it. We agreed the Pantry judges our boxes on sight, like anything else they take in, and they're free to refuse one.

She asked how many to expect. I gave her the orphan rate rather than a weekly count, because the count moves with membership. The honest answer is a handful a week, not a vanload. She said a handful is easy, and that fresh vegetables are what they're always short of. Tins they have.

The name for the round itself came out of Ilona working out the timing aloud: "So the dawn run fills the pods, the window closes the next morning, and then someone does the... orphan run?" Hilda laughed, I wrote it on the whiteboard, and that was that. It sits next to "dawn run" well enough that nobody argued. **The orphan run is the weekday round that collects the orphan boxes after the window closes and takes them to the Harbour Pantry.** With the dawn run on Tuesdays, it falls on Wednesday mornings for now. The Pantry's intake is weekday mornings, so that fits.

Ilona will drive the first orphan run herself tomorrow, partly so Hilda has a face to match the boxes. We didn't settle who does it after that. It's a short round, but it's another morning out of someone's week, and we both know how that goes.

Then I asked the awkward question: what if an orphan turns up at the weekend? A drop could slip, a door pulse could fail and a member couldn't get in until we'd sorted it, or there could be something we haven't thought of yet. Hilda couldn't say. Their weekends are run by a different group of volunteers, and she didn't want to promise anything on their behalf. **Open: can the food bank take orphan boxes at weekends?** She'll ask. Until we know, a weekend OB is a gap, and we said so out loud rather than pretend otherwise.

Small practical things before she left. I said I'd check exactly what's printed on a box when it leaves the farm, so nothing with a member's name goes out the door to the Pantry. Hilda will send over whatever paperwork they normally ask donors to fill in. And she wants a number to ring if a run is late, so she isn't holding a shelf for nothing.

Hilda left around eleven, back into the rain with an umbrella that had clearly lost an argument with the wind earlier.

## Orphan rate

Last week I added a column to the Ledger's weekly view: the share of boxes that end their window still sitting in a cell. I typed "orphan rate" as the label. The column was too narrow, so the header became "OBR", for orphan box rate. I'd tried "OR" first, but it looked like a logic operator and Ilona read it as "operating room". So OBR it is, on screen and in these notes. Same thing as the orphan rate.

**Orphan rate so far: 6 %.** That's across all drops since first drop, all pods.

We spent longer on this number than I'd planned. It's close to the mirror image of the pickup rate, but not quite. The OBR counts boxes that physically stayed in the cell, and that's what decides how much goes on the orphan run, so it's the number Hilda cares about. The PUR is what the kill criteria watch. At 6 % we're comfortably clear of that line, which is nice to say out loud once.

Ilona wanted to set a target for it. I didn't, at least not yet, because the sample is small and a single bad week at one building swings it. We left it without a target. What we did agree is that I'll show it on the weekly view where we both see it every Tuesday, next to the north number.

A few half-ideas from the discussion, all onto the parking lot:

- An SMS nudge from Picker a few hours before the window closes, for members whose box is still sitting there. It's cheap to build, but we don't want to annoy the members who simply collect late.
- Looking at whether the same few members account for most of the OBs. My hunch from scrolling the Ledger is yes, but I haven't counted, so it's only a hunch.
- Letting a member flag in advance that they'll miss a week. That's close to things we already have, so maybe just better wording in Picker.

## Neighbour credit

The second subject started from the north number. Last week was 41 boxes picked up against the pilot target of 100, with 38 members. We're not going to close that gap with more lobby walks alone. The cheapest new member we can get is one in a building that already has a pod: same stop on the loop, same caretaker, no new lobby slot, and usually a free cell or two. The building champions are doing good work talking Larder up, but there's only one of them per building, and they have lives.

Ilona's line: "The best salesperson is whoever lives in the next flat." Members already talk to each other in the lift. We just don't give them any reason to bring it up.

Ilona first called it a referral bonus. I pulled a face. "Referral" sounds like an app store, and half our members would rather not be marketed at by their own building. I wrote "neighbour credit" on the whiteboard instead, because that's literally what it is: credit for bringing in a neighbour, and the neighbour gets it too. Ilona kept saying it for the rest of the session, so it's stuck.

The decision:

- **A member who brings in a neighbour gets a 5 € neighbour credit, and so does the neighbour.**

Reasons we talked through:

- Both sides get something, so it doesn't feel like selling your neighbour out for a discount.
- It's credit, not cash, so it stays inside boxes.
- 5 € is enough to notice on a standard box without giving the box away. It's also much cheaper than anything else we could do to find a member: no flyers, no extra lobby walk, no extra stop.
- It keeps the growth in buildings where we already have a pod, which is where every new member is easiest to serve.

What we didn't decide, on purpose: whether it stacks with the champion perk, whether a household on a waitlist in a building without a pod counts as a "neighbour", and exactly when the credit lands. I said I'd sketch how the Ledger would track it before we argue about the edges. All three are on the parking lot. Ilona's worry is that someone games it with their own second email address. Mine is that nobody uses it at all. We'll see which of us is right.

Last bit of colour: the rain stopped exactly as we finished, about half twelve, which Ilona took as a personal insult after the morning she'd had. The red list is one item shorter. Orphan box storage is off it, and the weekend question goes on the open list instead.
