# Larder · session 18 · 12 May 2026 · Six pods and the Garage pack line

We started late because the dawn run ran long and I needed a shower and two coffees before I could think. Rasmus had the whiteboard already covered in boxes and arrows when I got to the Garage. Rain on and off all afternoon, the door half open for the air, the smell of wet cardboard. The red list was short this week, with nothing actually blocking, so we spent the whole sit-down on three things that turned out to be one thing: how many pods, who packs, and what comes after wave 1.

## Six pods, not four

Back on 17 February we decided the pilot would start with 4 pods and stay there. We had good reasons at the time. We wanted to test the bet in a handful of lobbies before spending more on leases, a single loop was all one person could drive on van day, and we didn't know yet whether anyone would actually come down to the lobby for a box. Four felt honest: enough buildings that one strange HA or one keen building champion couldn't skew the picture, and few enough that if it all went wrong we could pull out without much damage.

Rasmus opened by saying that the bet isn't really the question any more. The pickup rate has never come close to the kill criteria line, and the orphan rate has stayed low. The waitlist keeps growing from buildings we have no pod in. The bigger point, though, was the north number. Four K-12s don't have enough compartments to reach the target of 100 boxes picked up a week even if every compartment were full every week. We knew that in February, but back then 100 was a distant thing to aim at. Now we are near enough that the four pods are what holds us back, not the members.

I made the case for waiting. Every extra pod is another 85 € lease, another 6 € of lobby insurance and another 40 € lobby fee every month. It's also another HA to keep happy, another lobby on the sweep, and another stop on a loop that already took me longer than I liked this morning. Rasmus didn't disagree with any of that. His answer was that if we wait until the end of the pilot to grow, all we will have shown is that four buildings like us, and that is a weaker story for Season 2 than having shown we can add buildings without the wheels coming off. I think he's right. Adding pods is the part we haven't tested at all.

**Decision: the pilot grows to 6 pods. Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard join.** This replaces the 17 February decision that the pilot starts with 4 pods. We picked these two because both have households on the waitlist and both fit onto the existing loop as two more stops without a detour across town. Rasmus will talk to Oskar at Kestrel about two more K-12 units on the same lease terms. I asked him to make it clear that this is two units and not the start of a big order. Rasmus laughed and said Oskar always assumes the big order anyway.

Neither of us said it out loud until near the end, but six pods won't reach 100 either. That's what the last part of the session was about.

## The Mill Gate double drop

Before we got to packing I told Rasmus properly about last Tuesday, because I'd only mentioned it in passing on the phone.

On last Tuesday's dawn run two boxes went into compartment 7 of the Mill Gate pod. I still can't say exactly how. My best guess is that at that stop the boxes were sitting on the van floor in no useful order, I was reading the loop sheet in bad light, and two crates that looked the same went through the same hatch door one after the other. The member opened their compartment, found two boxes, and took both. I don't blame them at all; I would have too, and I'd probably have thought it was a nice surprise. We wrote one box off.

I called it a double drop when I told the story, because that's what it was: one drop, done twice into the same compartment. Rasmus wrote it on the whiteboard straight away, under a heading "things the Ledger should notice". So the name stuck before I'd finished my coffee. His half-idea is that Field could object if a second drop scan comes in for a compartment that already has one that morning. It isn't decided, but it's on his list to look at.

What the double drop actually showed us is that the mistake wasn't at Mill Gate. It happened earlier, when the boxes got into the van in whatever order they arrived from the farms.

## Packing moves to the Garage

On 24 February we decided that growers pack their own boxes at the farm. That was right for where we were then. We had one grower, then three. The Garage was a storeroom full of crates and my bicycle. The growers know their own produce better than we ever will. And having them pack meant one less job for the two of us, when the two of us were already doing everything. Aino in particular liked packing at Ridgeback, and the boxes from there have always looked lovely.

But we now have five growers, and that means boxes come in packed five different ways, labelled five different ways, and arriving at different times. Every stop on the dawn run starts with me in the back of the van hunting for the right boxes for that pod. The loop sheet tells me which compartment each box goes into, but it can't tell me where the box is in the van. With four pods that was annoying. With six it will cause more double drops, and probably worse ones. Rasmus pointed out that the problem grows faster than the number of pods, because each new stop adds more boxes to dig through at every stop that comes after it.

We talked through the halfway options first. One was asking every grower to pack to the same labels and the same order. That still leaves five farms each trying to guess the loop order. Another was sorting the boxes at the Garage before the run without repacking them, which is basically doing the work twice. Neither of us liked either option for long.

**Decision: all packing moves to the Garage. I run a pack line there on the evening before the dawn run.** This replaces the 24 February decision that growers pack their own boxes at the farm. Every box is packed at the Garage, in loop order, so when the van stops at a pod the boxes for that pod are together and in compartment order, and the loop sheet matches what's in front of me.

On the words: I called it the pack line almost as a joke, because I pictured the folding tables along the garage wall with crates moving from one end to the other like a little assembly line. Rasmus took it seriously and now it's just what we call it. He also started calling the Garage "the hub" while drawing the flow on the whiteboard, with arrows from five farms into one circle and out to six pods. I told him that sounds like a parcel company. He said that's the point: when he says hub he means the Garage as the packing room. "The Garage" still also means the store, the place with the crates and the spare shrouds. I can live with that. It's useful to have a word that means "the packing part" without having to say it every time.

Rasmus will make the Ledger produce a pack sheet for each pack line evening. It lists every box to pack that evening, grouped by stop in loop order, with the member and the compartment beside each one. It's the loop sheet seen from the other end: the loop sheet tells me where a box goes, and the pack sheet tells me what to put in it and in what order. The box sheets from the growers still decide what goes into the boxes. The pack sheet only decides which box is which.

Things we talked about and didn't settle:

- How each grower gets their produce to the hub before the pack line. It will be different for each of the five, and I'd rather talk to them one by one than invent a rule for them over coffee.
- What the GSAs say about packing. I need to reread them, because packing at the farm was one of the duties we wrote in, and I don't want any grower to find out their agreement has changed from a message.
- Monday is getting heavy. The sweep is on Mondays and now the pack line is Monday evening too. Rasmus asked if I was sure I could run the pack line every week. Honestly, I'm not, but I'd rather find out over a few weeks than guess. If it turns out to be too much for one person, we'll raise it then.
- Crates coming back. If every box leaves from the hub, the crates should come back to the hub. That probably makes things easier, but neither of us thought it through properly.

I also said, mostly for myself, that this makes the Garage much more central than we ever planned. That's exactly why we registered it as food premises, and I'm glad we did it before we needed it.

## Wave 2

With six pods decided, Rasmus asked what the rule is for the next one, and the one after that. We don't want to keep reopening the question of how many pods every few weeks, but we also don't want to promise a big number we can't serve.

What worked with wave 1 was bringing the buildings in together: lobby walks in the same few weeks, building champions found in the same weeks, member notes sent at the same time, and one round of explaining to growers that the boxes were going up. So we will do it in waves again. Rasmus said "wave 2" before I'd finished the sentence, and it's obviously the right name.

**Decision: wave 2 is five more buildings over the summer: Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard now, then Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 later.** Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard are the two new pods decided above. For the other three, "later" means once the first two have settled and the pack line is running smoothly, not on a date we've picked. We were careful not to put a date on it. If the hub can't cope with six pods, there's no point in planning for nine.

What wave 2 means in practice, in rough order:

- Lobby slots confirmed with the HAs at Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard, on the same lobby fee as everyone else.
- One building champion in each of the two buildings. I'd like to find them among the waitlist households if possible, because those are people who already asked for us.
- Both buildings set up in the Ledger and in Picker so waitlist households can sign up the same way current members do.
- Two more green shrouds with the Cropbox mark.
- Two more stops on the loop sheet, and Rasmus wants to reorder the whole loop instead of just tacking them onto the end.
- Lobby walks at Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3, but only to meet the HAs and look at the floor. No promises on timing.

At some point Rasmus asked whether Oskar at Harbour Row would be willing to tell the new champions what the job is really like. It's a nice idea, but I don't want to hand our Harbour Row champion work they never asked for. I'll ask them, and if they say no, that's fine.

We finished around seven. The rain had stopped. Rasmus stayed to measure the wall for the tables.
