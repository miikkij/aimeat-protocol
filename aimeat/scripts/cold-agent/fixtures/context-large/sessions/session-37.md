> ## Bringing back an advance payment option: analysis
>
> ### Summary
>
> An advance payment option can be brought back without recreating the June problem, as long as a skip week never triggers a cash refund. On current prices, a **6-week pass with one free box** looks like the best balance of uptake, cost and simplicity. The case rests mainly on lower churn and fewer drop-day card charges, not on margin: on house-cut terms the pass roughly breaks even.
>
> ### 1. Why the season pass was dropped
>
> 1. **Skip-week refunds.** When a pass holder skipped a week, part of the prepaid amount had to go back to their card. That meant partial refunds after the grower had already planned the box, card fees paid twice, and refunds that took days to show up for members.
> 2. **The drip plan as the fix.** Moving every member to the drip plan, with the card on file charged on the drop day, removed refunds entirely. It is simple, but it gives a member a fresh decision point every single week.
>
> ### 2. What has changed since June
>
> 1. **Churn** is 9 % a month, after the rise to 29 €. The cut to 26 € is recent and its effect is not yet visible.
> 2. **Prices:** Sprout 16 €, Larder 26 €, Harvest 39 €.
> 3. **House cut** is 14 %, and every GSA now carries a floor price of 18 € per standard box.
> 4. **Scale:** 9 pods with wave 2 complete; 131 boxes picked up last week against an NN target of 160 by the end of the extended pilot.
> 5. **Pickup:** orphan rate 3 %, pickup window 36 hours.
>
> ### 3. Unit economics at current prices
>
> | Tier | Price | House cut (14 %) | Grower share |
> |---|---|---|---|
> | Sprout | 16.00 € | 2.24 € | 13.76 € |
> | Larder | 26.00 € | 3.64 € | 22.36 € |
> | Harvest | 39.00 € | 5.46 € | 33.54 € |
>
> For the standard box, the grower share (22.36 €) sits well above the 18 € floor price.
>
> **Six standard boxes on the drip plan:** 156 € revenue, 21.84 € house cut.
>
> **Six-week pass, pay for five, get six:** 130 € revenue.
> - If the grower is paid the full share on all six boxes (134.16 €), Larder loses 4.16 € on the pass.
> - If the free box is paid to the grower at the floor price of 18 €, the grower receives 129.80 € and Larder keeps 0.20 €.
>
> So the free box costs Larder about 21.64 € in house cut per pass, compared with the same six weeks on the drip plan.
>
> ### 4. Retention value (modelled)
>
> These figures are estimates, not measurements.
>
> 1. At 9 % monthly churn, a member's expected remaining membership is about 11 months (1 ÷ 0.09).
> 2. At roughly 4.3 boxes a month and 3.64 € house cut per standard box, that is about 172 € of house cut per member.
> 3. The chance of a drip-plan member leaving within any six-week stretch is about 13 %.
> 4. If pass holders almost never leave mid-pass, the expected saving per pass is about 0.13 × 172 € ≈ 22 €.
>
> That roughly equals the 21.64 € cost of the free box. One caution: members who choose to prepay are probably less likely to leave anyway (a selection effect), so the true saving is likely smaller than modelled.
>
> ### 5. Options compared
>
> | Option | Length | Incentive | Skip handling | Assessment |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | A. Drip plan only | – | – | n/a | Simplest; does nothing for churn |
> | B. Short pass with bonus | 6 weeks | 1 free box | Up to 2 skips, credited | Recommended |
> | C. Longer pass, no bonus | 8 weeks | None | End date moves | Uptake risk |
> | D. Season-length pass | 12+ weeks | Discount | Refunds | Repeats the June problem |
>
> **Length.** Six weeks is long enough to cover a typical "should I keep this?" moment. It is short enough that few members will need a skip week inside one pass, which keeps exceptions rare.
>
> **Incentive.** Without one, the only reason to prepay is convenience. Some members value that, but uptake is likely to be modest. A visible free box is easy to explain at the pod and in Picker.
>
> **Skip handling.** Moving the end date is the simplest mechanism, but each member's end date then drifts. Growers' box sheets and Backroom's forecasts would have to cope with passes ending in different weeks. A capped credit (at most two skip weeks per pass, each worth one box, applied to the member's first drip-plan charge after the pass) keeps every end date fixed and still involves no cash refund.
>
> ### 6. Recommendation
>
> 1. Offer a **6-week pass at the price of five boxes**, with the sixth box free.
> 2. Make the **last week the free one.** That rewards finishing the pass and pushes the cost to the end.
> 3. Pay the grower for the free box at the **floor price of 18 €**, so no grower is paid less than the GSA guarantees.
> 4. Start with the **Larder tier only**; extend to Sprout and Harvest once the mechanics work.
> 5. Run the pass **alongside the drip plan**, not instead of it. The pass is a single charge to the card on file at purchase.
> 6. Handle skips with the **capped credit** described above.
>
> ### 7. Risks and open points
>
> 1. **Pilot end date.** A pass sold now runs past the current end of the extended pilot, 30 September. Selling it assumes continuity, which is not yet settled.
> 2. **Grower acceptance.** Paying the free box at the floor price means growers get less than their usual share for that box. Check this against each GSA and raise it at the growers' circle.
> 3. **Orphan boxes.** A prepaid box left uncollected may feel worse to the member than a drip-plan one. Expect more requests for make-goods.
> 4. **Selection effect.** Measure churn among pass holders against comparable drip-plan members, not against the whole base.
> 5. **Kill criteria.** The pass does not change how pickup rate is measured. The 85 % threshold should be watched exactly as now.
> 6. **Add-ons.** Decide whether add-ons can be included in a pass or stay on weekly charges.
