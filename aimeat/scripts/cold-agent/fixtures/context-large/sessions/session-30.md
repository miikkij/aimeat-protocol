> ## House cut vs. Larder's costs: is 12 % enough?
>
> You asked me to check whether the current 12 % house cut covers what Larder spends getting a box from a grower into a compartment, and if not, what level would. Below is what I worked from, what the numbers show, and where I would set it.
>
> ### 1. What I worked from
>
> **Figures you gave me:**
> - Standard box 29 €, small box 17 €, family box 39 €
> - House cut 12 %; floor price of 18 € per standard box in every GSA
> - Lobby fee as a sales share: 2 % of each pod's box sales
> - 9 pods leased from Kestrel at 85 € per pod per month, plus lobby insurance at 6 € per pod per month
> - One drop day a week (Thursday); a hired van on van day for the outer loop; a cargo bike leg in the centre
> - Maija on the grower desk at 22 € an hour
> - Most recent weekly count: 104 boxes picked up
>
> **Things I had to estimate (flagged throughout):**
> - Van hire per van day, including fuel: 90 €
> - Cargo bike leg per drop day, including rider time: 75 €
> - Grower desk hours: 6 a week
> - Box mix: I treat every box as a standard box. Small boxes earn less, family boxes more. At your current mix the error is small, but if Sprout grows, these numbers will look better than reality.
>
> Crate deposits are left out: they are held and refunded, not income.
>
> ### 2. What 12 % earns per box
>
> | Box | Price | House cut (12 %) | Sales share (2 %) | Left for Larder |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | Small | 17.00 € | 2.04 € | 0.34 € | 1.70 € |
> | Standard | 29.00 € | 3.48 € | 0.58 € | 2.90 € |
> | Family | 39.00 € | 4.68 € | 0.78 € | 3.90 € |
>
> The structural point: the sales share is charged on the full box price, but Larder only keeps 12 % of that price. Two points of sales share are therefore one sixth of Larder's entire take (2 ÷ 12). Before any other cost is paid, about 17 % of the house cut goes straight to the HA. And unlike a flat fee, this never gets cheaper per box as you grow. At a higher house cut the proportion shrinks: at 16 % it is one eighth.
>
> ### 3. Weekly costs the house cut has to cover
>
> | Cost | Basis | Per week |
> |---|---|---|
> | Pod leases | 9 × 85 € × 12 ÷ 52 | 176.54 € |
> | Lobby insurance | 9 × 6 € × 12 ÷ 52 | 12.46 € |
> | Van on van day | estimate | 90.00 € |
> | Cargo bike leg | estimate | 75.00 € |
> | Grower desk | 6 h × 22 € (hours estimated) | 132.00 € |
> | **Total** | | **486.00 €** |
>
> Some costs are not in this model: your own time, hosting, and the evenings on the pack line. The real gap is wider than shown.
>
> ### 4. Where 12 % leaves you
>
> At 104 boxes, Larder keeps 104 × 2.90 € = 301.60 € a week against 486.00 € of costs, a shortfall of about 184 € a week.
>
> Break-even at 12 % is 486 ÷ 2.90 ≈ **168 boxes a week**. That is above the NN target of 160 for the end of the extended pilot. Put plainly: at 12 %, Larder would not cover its running costs even if the pilot hit its own target.
>
> ### 5. Other levels
>
> | House cut | Larder per standard box | Grower per standard box | Left after sales share | Weekly at 104 boxes | Break-even (boxes/week) |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | 12 % | 3.48 € | 25.52 € | 2.90 € | 301.60 € | 168 |
> | 14 % | 4.06 € | 24.94 € | 3.48 € | 361.92 € | 140 |
> | 16 % | 4.64 € | 24.36 € | 4.06 € | 422.24 € | 120 |
> | 18 % | 5.22 € | 23.78 € | 4.64 € | 482.56 € | 105 |
>
> What this shows:
>
> 1. **The floor price does not come into play.** The grower share on a standard box stays above 18 € for any house cut up to about 37.9 % (11 ÷ 29). The floor protects growers against moves much larger than any of these options, so it gives no argument either way.
> 2. **14 % gets break-even under the target, but only just.** The 20-box margin below 160 depends on my 75 € cargo bike estimate. If the cargo bike leg actually costs 25 € a week more, break-even at 14 % rises to about 147 (511 ÷ 3.48).
> 3. **16 % puts break-even at about 120.** That is within reach of modest growth from 104, and it leaves room for estimates that turn out low.
> 4. **18 % almost breaks even today**, but it takes 1.74 € per standard box back from growers compared with now. That is the kind of move that damages the relationship the growers' circle built.
>
> ### 6. Grower relations
>
> The house cut went down to 12 % in May because the growers' circle asked for a bigger grower share. Any increase reverses part of that, so how you frame it matters as much as the number.
>
> 1. At 16 %, a grower earns 24.36 € on a standard box, 1.16 € less than today. For an illustrative grower supplying 20 standard boxes a week, that is about 23 € a week.
> 2. Growers call it the commission. Tie the change to named costs (the sales share and the cargo bike leg), not to a general "Larder needs more".
> 3. One move, explained once, is easier than two small ones. If you go part of the way now and find in the autumn that it wasn't enough, you will have the same conversation twice in one season.
> 4. Every GSA will need the new figure. Since Ilona signs them, time the amendments to arrive with the explanation, not before it.
>
> ### 7. Recommendation
>
> I would set the house cut at **16 %**, for these reasons:
>
> 1. Break-even falls to about 120 boxes a week, reachable from current volume.
> 2. The margin absorbs errors in my delivery estimates, which are the weakest part of this model.
> 3. The sales share drops from a sixth to an eighth of Larder's take.
> 4. Growers stay far above the 18 € floor.
> 5. It is the level least likely to need a second increase.
>
> **What would change my mind:** if weekly volume is clearly above 104, or your real delivery costs are lower than my estimates, 14 % may be enough. Rerun the table with actual figures before deciding.
