> ## Autumn revenue if the Alder Court pod moves
>
> ### 1. What this looks at
> You asked what happens to Larder's revenue from October to December if the Alder Court lobby slot ends on 30 September and the pod has to go somewhere else. Below are the starting figures, the assumptions, three scenarios, a comparison and a recommendation. Everything here is modelled. Please check the per-pod numbers in Backroom before relying on them.
>
> ### 2. Starting point (figures you gave me)
> - 9 pods live. Wave 1 and wave 2 are complete.
> - 131 boxes picked up last week across all pods. That is about 14.6 per pod on average.
> - North number target: 160 by 30 September.
> - Monthly churn: 9 %. Orphan rate: 3 %.
> - Standard box: 29 €.
> - House cut: 14 %, so 4.06 € per standard box. The sales share to the HA is 2 %, or 0.58 € per box. That leaves **3.48 € net to Larder per box**, before delivery costs.
> - The floor price of 18 € does not bind at this price. The grower share is 24.94 €.
> - Fixed cost per pod: lease 85 € plus lobby insurance 6 €, so **91 € a month**.
>
> ### 3. Assumptions specific to Alder Court
> 1. **Volume.** Pod zero has had the longest time to build up, so I assume it runs above the average, at 18 boxes picked up a week. If Backroom shows a different figure, the results scale in a straight line.
> 2. **Box mix.** Every box is treated as a standard box. Small and family boxes would change the totals but not the order of the scenarios.
> 3. **Perks.** The caretaker box and the lobby host box are left out. They follow the pod to whichever building it goes to, so they are roughly the same in every scenario.
> 4. **Delivery.** Cost per drop is left out. A new building may sit better or worse on the loop. That is noted as a risk, not modelled.
> 5. **Months.** Each month is counted as 4 weeks, to keep the arithmetic readable.
> 6. **Moving and storage charges.** Kestrel's charges for moving or storing a pod are not known, so they are excluded. Add them once you have a quote.
> 7. **Existing members.** I assume few Alder Court members would follow the pod to another building. They are treated as lost unless another pod is within easy walking distance.
>
> ### 4. Scenarios
> **A. Move at once** to a building that is already asking for a pod, such as Linden Yard, which you mentioned. The pod is dark for two weeks in early October while it is moved. It then opens at 8 boxes a week and grows by 2 a week until it reaches 18.
>
> **A-low. Move at once, but the building disappoints.** Same as A, except the new building levels off at 12 boxes a week. That is closer to the weaker pods than to pod zero.
>
> **B. Store for a month, then choose.** The pod is kept in storage through October, either at Kestrel's depot or in the hub if there is room. The month is used for lobby walks, waitlist checks and HA terms at two or three candidate buildings. It opens in early November with a better-prepared launch: 10 boxes in the first week, then 2 more a week, reaching 18 in December.
>
> **C. Hand the pod back.** Larder runs 8 pods. This depends on the lease terms, which I don't have, so it is not modelled in detail. It removes the 91 € a month and all of the pod's revenue.
>
> ### 5. Results
> All figures are net house cut after the sales share, minus lease and insurance.
>
> | Scenario | Boxes Oct / Nov / Dec | October | November | December | Q4 total |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | Alder Court as is (reference) | 72 / 72 / 72 | 159.56 € | 159.56 € | 159.56 € | 478.68 € |
> | A: move at once | 18 / 60 / 72 | −28.36 € | 117.80 € | 159.56 € | 249.00 € |
> | A-low: weaker building | 18 / 45 / 48 | −28.36 € | 65.60 € | 76.04 € | 113.28 € |
> | B: store, then choose | 0 / 52 / 72 | −91.00 € | 89.96 € | 159.56 € | 158.52 € |
>
> Gross box sales at 29 € over the quarter are 4,350 € in A, 3,219 € in A-low and 3,596 € in B.
>
> ### 6. Reading the numbers
> 1. **Over the quarter the gap is small.** If the quick move lands well, B costs about 90 € more than A. That is about one month of lease and insurance. Neither path is a large sum compared with the pilot's running costs.
> 2. **The real risk sits beyond December.** A building that levels off at 12 rather than 18 costs 6 boxes a week, every week. Over half a year that is 6 × 26 × 3.48 € = **542.88 €**, several times what a storage month costs. A pod is also hard to move twice. A second move means a second HA conversation and a second moving charge.
> 3. **North number.** The slot runs until 30 September, so the move itself does not touch the 160 target date. What it changes is the base carried into autumn. Without Alder Court, last week's 131 becomes roughly 113, and the new pod has to rebuild from zero.
> 4. **Pickup rate.** A rushed launch usually brings more first-time members who miss the window. With the pickup window at 36 hours, the first weeks at a new building may show a higher orphan rate than the 3 % you have now. While the kill criteria use pickup rate as the yardstick, a weak new pod pulls the whole average down.
> 5. **Existing members.** At 9 % monthly churn the member base already loses people every month. A dark spell at Alder Court will push some members out for good, whichever scenario you pick. Talking to them early matters more than the choice of scenario.
> 6. **Operational exposure.** A move into a building that is not fully prepared is where the small failures cluster: a plinth that needs re-levelling, a slow heartbeat, a missed fob handover. Each one costs time on the dawn run.
>
> ### 7. Recommendation
> **Keep the Alder Court pod in storage for one month before choosing a new building.** Use October to compare two or three candidates on waitlist size, lobby access, power and HA terms, then open in early November.
>
> The reasoning is that the cost is bounded: about 91 € of lease and insurance, any storage fee, and roughly four weeks of lost house cut. In return Larder gets a better chance of a building that settles at pod zero's level rather than below it. Given the half-year exposure in point 6.2, that trade looks worth making.
>
> ### 8. What would change this
> - If a candidate building's waitlist already comes close to 18 households, the quick move (A) is the better choice. The ramp would be shorter than modelled.
> - If Kestrel charges a lot for storage, or the only free space is your own premises, the storage month gets more expensive.
> - If many Alder Court members can walk to another pod, the loss of existing members is smaller and timing matters less.
> - If the moving charge is high, it weighs on every move scenario equally. Doing the move once and doing it right then matters even more.
