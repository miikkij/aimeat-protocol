# Tidewater (board game) · playtest 4 · 12 July 2026 · Five players

Sunday, and hot enough that we played with the balcony door open and the cargo cards weighed down with coffee mugs. Pauli and Venla came over as usual, and this time we had two guests as well: a couple Venla knows from her drawing class who had heard her complain about "Rasmus's boat game" and wanted to see it. That gave us five at the table for the first time, which was the whole point of today.

## Five at the table

The problem with five was obvious before we started: there are only four captain sheets. Venla sat on the floor and drew a fifth one from memory while I set up the board. It came out better than the printed ones, which is a bit embarrassing for the printed ones. Its hold boxes are slightly wider, and one of the guests said it was easier to read. I'm not changing the layout because of that, but I'm noting it.

On 8 March we decided Tidewater was for 2 to 4 players. The reasoning then was mostly fear. I thought five ships would jam the lanes around the hub, that the wait between turns would get long enough for people to drift off to their phones, and that the storm deck would hit so many ships per wave that it would feel random rather than tense. We never tested any of that. We just drew the line at four because it felt safe, and the box mock-up already said 2–4.

Today the game mostly proved me wrong. The lanes did get crowded near the hub, but crowded in an interesting way: people started routing around each other on purpose, and a couple of really nice blocking moves came out of it that I've never seen at four. The wait between turns was noticeable, but nobody checked out. People were watching other players' drops because they cared where the cargo was going. The storm deck felt about as mean as it does at four, maybe slightly meaner because more ships means more targets, but nobody called it unfair.

The game ran long. I didn't time it properly, so I don't have a number. We were chatting a lot, two people were learning the rules, and we restarted once (see below), so I don't trust any feeling I have about length from today. Game length stays where it is for now. I want a cleaner run before I say anything about it.

So, after a fair bit of back and forth, mostly between me and Pauli: **Tidewater is now for 2 to 5 players.** This replaces the 8 March decision of 2 to 4. Pauli's argument, which in the end I agreed with, was that the reasons for capping at four were guesses, and today we had actual evidence against most of them. Venla's argument was simpler: "If people bring a friend, they shouldn't have to leave the friend out." The guests, unprompted, said they'd buy a game more readily if it went to five, because their group is five. That's one data point, but it's a real one.

What still worries me at five, for the parking lot rather than the rulebook:

- The heartbeat moves at the end of every loop, and a loop at five takes longer in real time. The tide track might feel slow even if the turn count is fine. I don't know yet.
- More ships around the hub means more ships near the reef, so more people paying for it. That leans into the question we still haven't answered, whether the reef is too punishing. I didn't want to settle that today just because five players made it louder. It stays open.
- Five pilots on the board at once. Nothing broke, but I want to watch it.

## The ledger

Pauli kept score today, as he usually does, on the ruled sheet I printed with columns for beacons, orphans and so on. Halfway through, one of the guests asked who was winning, and Pauli flipped the sheet round and said, "Hang on, let me check the ledger." He said it in a sort of old-harbourmaster voice, and everyone laughed. Venla had already doodled a little anchor in the corner of the sheet weeks ago, so it really does look like something out of a shipping office. By the end of the game all of us, guests included, were calling the score sheet the ledger, so it's the ledger now. I'm going to rename the file.

Small thing that came up while Pauli was "auditing the ledger" (he wouldn't drop the voice): the orphan column is the one people forget to fill in until the very end. Then there's a scramble to work out who dropped what. Not a rules problem, a sheet layout problem. I might move the column or make it bigger. That's a to-do, not a decision.

## Swap cards

The best new idea of the day came from the guests. Mid-game, one of them had a cargo card that was useless to her and perfect for the player next to her. She wanted to trade right then rather than wait for the window. Under the current rules you can't, and she was annoyed about it in a good way. She asked if there could be a card that let you do that just once.

Venla grabbed a blank card and wrote SWAP on it in big letters, and we started calling it a swap card straight away. The idea is a card that lets one player trade one cargo card with another player outside the window. We didn't play with it, we just passed the blank around and argued.

Points made, none of them settled:

- Pauli likes it because it gives the long stretches between windows something to think about, which could matter more at five.
- I'm worried it makes the window less special. If you can trade any time, why have a trading phase at all?
- Venla wondered whether swap cards should come out of the storm deck, so you never know when one turns up. One of the guests thought that would make it feel like luck rather than a choice.
- Nobody knew how many there should be, and I deliberately didn't pick a number.

Nothing decided. It's on the parking lot with a star next to it. I'd like to try it in some form, but I don't want to bolt it onto the same game where we're getting used to five players.

## Dawn run

Before the real game, I asked to try the opening without the dawn run. The thought behind it: at five players, the double move at the start means a lot of ships hitting the board at once. I wondered whether a calmer opening would ease the crowding around the hub.

We played the first couple of loops that way and it was dull. Everyone crept one step out of harbour and then stared at each other. Nobody could reach a harbour tile worth dropping on, so there were no drops. The guests, who didn't know the game, said they couldn't tell what they were supposed to be doing yet. Pauli said it felt like "waiting for the game to start." We reset the board and played the full game with the dawn run. The difference was immediate: by the second loop people already had cargo moving and were making real choices.

So: **the dawn run stays.** The version without it made the opening too slow. Crowding near the hub at five is a separate thing, and I'd rather look at it on its own than solve it by gutting the opening.
