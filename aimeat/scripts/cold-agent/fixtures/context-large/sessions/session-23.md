# Larder · session 23 · 16 June 2026 · Sales share and the cargo bike

Sirpa came over from Alder Court for the first hour, mostly for the lobby fee, and then stayed for a second coffee and the cargo bike argument, which I think she enjoyed more than the money part. Grey and warm, the Garage door open the whole time because the pack line tables still smelled of last Wednesday's spring onions. Ilona had last Thursday's loop sheets spread out on the end table with times pencilled in next to every stop, and those set the tone for the second half of the sit-down.

## Lobby fee: going back on the 40 €

This is a reversal of something we decided ourselves, so I want the reasoning written down properly.

On 24 March we agreed that we pay each HA a lobby fee of 40 € a month per pod. It was the right call at the time. We had pod zero in Alder Court and wave 1 mostly on paper, with no sales history at all. Sirpa's board wanted a plain number they could put in the minutes, approve once and forget about. A flat fee was easy to explain on a lobby walk and easy for us to budget next to the lease and the lobby insurance. Nobody on an HA board had to trust our figures, because there were no figures to trust. Sirpa reminded us that her board liked it precisely because it was boring.

What has changed is that the pods are no longer alike. Some lobbies are busy and some are still finding their feet. A flat fee treats them all the same, and that is starting to feel wrong in both directions.

For us, the problem is wave 2. Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard start from close to nothing. Every new building costs us the same 40 € a month from the day the pod goes in, before it has sold a single box. That was fine with one pod. It is less fine when we think about Kiln Lane, Saltworks and Ropewalk 3 coming later, each of them a fixed cost until its members turn up.

For the HAs, the problem is the busy pods. Oskar at Harbour Row, the building champion (not Oskar at Kestrel), asked in the BC chat a couple of weeks ago whether the HA "gets anything if the pod does well". The honest answer was no. Sirpa said her board members have started asking the same thing in different words. They see the pod in the lobby every day, they see neighbours using it, and they see a payment that never moves.

Ilona put it in one line: the pod is a shared bet with the building, so the building should share in how it goes. She suggested tying the fee to what the pod actually sells. Sirpa said, "So the HA gets a share of the sales." Ilona wrote **sales share** at the top of the whiteboard as a heading, and it stuck. By the end of the hour Sirpa was using it herself ("my board will want the sales share explained on one page").

We talked through two shapes before settling:

- **A smaller flat fee plus a percentage on top.** Left alone. That means two numbers to explain on every lobby walk, which is one too many. It also keeps the fixed cost for new pods, which is half of what we're trying to fix.
- **A percentage only.** Simple, and it scales with the pod in both directions.

**Decision: the lobby fee becomes a sales share, 2 % of the box sales of that pod. This replaces the 24 March decision of a flat 40 € a month per pod.**

On the 2 %, Ilona wanted something an HA notices on a busy pod without it squeezing us on a slow one. We didn't model it to the cent in the room. The point today was the shape, not squeezing the last decimal.

Sirpa's worry was the obvious one: certainty. A slow month or a run of weather skips means less money for the HA, and her board liked knowing what was coming. Ilona said that's the point. If the pod has a bad month, we all have a bad month. Sirpa didn't fully disagree, but she said she would rather present it to her board than be seen to have agreed to it over coffee in a garage. Fair. She thinks they'll go for it, mainly because of how the Alder Court pod has been doing. Whether they do is their call.

Sirpa also asked, kindly but directly, whether the growers end up paying for this one way or another. I said the grower share isn't part of this conversation, and that the floor price in every GSA holds whatever happens to the lobby fee. She seemed satisfied with that. I suspect someone at the growers' circle will ask the same thing, so I'm glad we've said it out loud once.

On the practical side, Backroom already knows every pod's box sales, because payments are tied to the drop and the drop is tied to the pod. I said "the Ledger can just sum it" and Ilona gave me a look; it's been a week. A monthly statement per pod is a small job. One thing came up while sketching it: orphan boxes are paid for, so they count as sales. The statement goes to an HA, though, so it leaves the building, and it must not say anything about where orphans end up. The statement will show sales per pod and stop there. No orphan line, no mention of the orphan run or the Harbour Pantry.

Ilona also thinks the sales share makes the waitlist easier to work. The pitch to an HA in a building on the waitlist becomes: no cost to anyone until boxes sell, then the building earns from every box. That's a much shorter conversation than explaining why we'd pay a fixed fee for a pod that might start with a handful of members.

## Dawn run timing and the cargo bike

Last Tuesday (9 June) we left an open question on the red list: can the dawn run finish before 7:00 with six pods? Ilona's pencilled loop sheets were the evidence. With Ferry Close and Weaver's Yard on the loop, last Thursday's van made the seven o'clock line with almost nothing to spare, and that was on a good morning with the box sheets in early and the pack line finished on time.

The driving between pods isn't the slow part. The van loses its time in the centre: one-way streets, nowhere to stop near some of the lobbies, circling while the clock runs, then walking crates from wherever the van ended up to the hatch. Ilona's times showed the centre stops eating far more than their share of the morning, compared with the stops further out where the van pulls up at the door.

Ilona said, half as a joke, "this bit would be quicker on a bike". Sirpa said she sees delivery cargo bikes go past her window every morning faster than the vans, and then we stopped joking and drew it out properly.

The idea is to split the dawn run in two from the Garage:

- The centre stops go on a cargo bike. Our loop sheets already talk in stops, so I called that part the **cargo bike leg**: one leg of the loop, ridden instead of driven. Ilona said it sounded like a relay race, and that's roughly what it is.
- The rest stays in the van. Ilona started calling the van's part the **outer loop**, since it's the loop round the pods outside the centre. Within ten minutes all three of us were saying "outer loop" without thinking about it.

The two leave the Garage together once the crates are sorted and run in parallel. Neither has to do the whole morning, and the van no longer spends its time on the centre streets it was worst at.

Things we worried about, roughly in the order they came up:

- **Two people at dawn instead of one.** The real cost is ours, in sleep. Ilona and I need a Thursday rota that works for both of us. We didn't settle it today.
- **Chill items on a bike.** The van at least keeps things out of the sun. The cargo bike leg needs an insulated crate for anything headed into a chill cell, with an ice brick in it on hot mornings. I'll test that before it goes near a real drop.
- **Weight and bulk.** Harvest boxes are heavy and some weeks the box sheets are root-vegetable weeks. The bike might need two trips from the Garage on a heavy week. That's still faster than the van circling, but we should know the bike's limits before the first real Thursday.
- **Rain.** Coastal city, so yes. Nobody had a clever answer. Ilona said "waterproofs", and that's where we left it.
- **Rushing.** I raised this one. Rushed stops are where double drops come from, and a cargo bike leg run against the clock could make that worse, not better. The drop scan in Field still happens at every compartment, on whichever phone the rider has. I need to check how Field behaves on a weak signal in a stairwell, because the bike rider won't have the van to sit in while the app catches up.
- **Fobs and keys.** Sirpa's practical point: whoever rides needs the same fobs and access as the van driver, and caretakers will want to know there's a new face at the door at dawn.

Sirpa's other point was the noise. The van reversing in the yard before seven isn't popular, and she'd take a bike over a van outside her building any morning. She said she wasn't trying to lobby for her own building, then laughed because she obviously was.

Ilona's pack line has to change too. Crates now go in two loop orders, one for the bike and one for the van, so the pack sheet splits in two and the crates get sorted into two piles before anyone leaves. She thinks that's mostly a labelling job, not more packing time. We'll see on the first Wednesday.

Van day doesn't change: we still hire the van, it just does a shorter morning.

**Decision: the city-centre part of the loop moves to a cargo bike leg, and the van keeps the outer loop.**

That also **closes the 9 June question**. Can the dawn run finish before 7:00 with six pods? Yes, with the cargo bike leg in the centre. The seven o'clock line stays as it is. We fix it by splitting the loop, not by starting earlier or moving the line.

How we get the bike is not decided. Buying, leasing and hiring week by week all came up and none won. Ilona will find out what's actually available in Lakeport before we talk money. The exact split is also open. One stop sits right on the edge of the centre, and we went back and forth on whether it belongs to the bike or the van without settling it. Ilona will try it both ways on the loop sheets.

I keep coming back to why this matters more than it looks. A pod filled late is a pod where someone on an early shift walks past an empty compartment. That's a missed pickup, and missed pickups show up directly in the north number. Getting every pod full before seven is the cheapest growth we've got.
