# Larder · session 14 · 14 April 2026 · A new name and wave 1

Grey, wet morning, rain coming in sideways off the harbour, and the Garage smelled of damp cardboard when Rasmus arrived. He'd stopped at the bakery on the corner for the good coffee, which usually means he has something he's been chewing on. He did: the name. So we skipped the red list for the first half hour and started there.

## The name

Rasmus put it bluntly. On almost every lobby walk this month, somebody has heard "Larder" and assumed we are a pantry-storage app, the kind that tracks what's in your cupboards and nags you when the lentils run out. I've had the same thing. On the Mill Gate walk a man from the HA asked, quite seriously, whether it would tell him when his tins go out of date. At Harbour Row, Oskar (our champion there, not Oskar at Kestrel) told me last week that two neighbours had asked him whether "the Larder thing" was a shopping list. He'd spent five minutes explaining that no, it's vegetables, from actual farms, in the cabinet downstairs, and then he said, laughing, that he was tired of starting every chat by correcting the name.

So we had to talk about going back on a decision. On 20 January we decided the product is called Larder. I still like the word. In January it felt warm and a bit old-fashioned, a larder is where food lives, it's one word, and it sat nicely next to Picker and the Ledger. What we never did was try it on strangers. We tried it on each other, on Aino, on a couple of friends who already knew what we were building, and of course it made sense to them. Now we have three months of real conversations, and in real conversations the name is doing the opposite of its job: it sends people to the wrong picture, and we spend the first minute undoing it.

We went through the cost of changing honestly, because it isn't nothing:

- every screen in Picker that says Larder, and the SMS that carries the wake code
- the grant application, which went in with the old name on every page
- the Garage paperwork, since the food premises registration was done under Larder
- the signs and stickers already in the lobbies
- the growers, who have just about got used to the name (Liisa still calls the whole thing "the cabinet scheme", so maybe not)

The cost of not changing is that every new building starts with a misunderstanding, and that gets worse the more buildings there are. Rasmus's point that settled it for me: this is the cheapest it will ever be. Four pods, a few dozen members, a handful of growers. Every month we wait, the name gets printed somewhere else.

Then we needed something to change it to. We spent a while scribbling on the back of an old loop sheet. Rasmus had a column of names that all sounded like fintech. I wrote "Cropbox" half as a joke, because that is literally what it is, a crop in a box, and Rasmus circled it twice and said "nobody will think that's a cupboard app". That was more or less it. It stuck because it says what's inside, it can't be mistaken for anything to do with storing food at home, and it reads well large on a pod front. We tested it the cheap way: Rasmus rang his sister, said "Cropbox" with no context and asked what she thought it was. She said "a vegetable box?" Good enough for a Tuesday.

One small grumble from me: "box" is already our own word for the box itself, so we're going to end up saying things like "the Cropbox box". Rasmus says nobody outside this garage will ever say that, and inside the garage we'll survive.

**Decision: the product is renamed Cropbox**, because people met on lobby walks took "Larder" for a pantry-storage app. This replaces the decision of 20 January 2026 that the product is called Larder. Picker keeps its name; nothing about the app's name was on the table. I'm keeping these notes in the old Larder file for now, simply because moving everything is a job for a quieter week.

### Trademark

Before the coffee was cold, Rasmus raised the obvious worry: does the name Cropbox clash with an existing trademark? Neither of us knows. It's a plain enough word that someone could well have registered it, here or elsewhere, for something food-shaped. We didn't settle it today. We agreed the rename stands as the decision, but that we should find out before we print anything expensive with it. The question is open. I said I'd ask Hanna whether anyone she knows has dealt with a trademark search, purely for a pointer; she's helpful with that kind of thing, and asking her is not the same as anything to do with money.

## Wave 1

I've been saying "the first batch of buildings" for weeks, and today Rasmus said batch sounds like bread rolls. I said what I actually mean is that buildings come in together, in waves: same round of lobby walks, same conversations with the HAs, same weeks of getting the pods settled, and then a pause before the next lot. Rasmus liked "wave" straight away, mostly because it gives us a way to talk about the buildings after these without having to name them or promise them anything yet. So: a wave is a group of buildings brought into the project together.

**Decision: wave 1 is the four buildings of the first pods: Alder Court, Quarry Street 12, Harbour Row and Mill Gate.**

The reasons were practical. These are the four we've actually done the work with: the walks, the HAs, the champions, the caretakers. Drawing a line around them stops us treating every enthusiastic email as a fifth pod. And it makes the pilot easier to read: when we look at the numbers at the end of June, we'll be looking at one wave, not a smear of buildings at different stages. We did not talk about what comes after wave 1, and I want to note that we deliberately didn't. That's for another day.

### The waitlist

Linked to that: Rasmus has started marking, in the Ledger, households who have asked for us from buildings where there is no pod. I've been keeping the same people in the back of my notebook, with phone numbers and the odd "very keen!!" next to a name. He called it the waitlist, which is the boring, obvious word and therefore the right one. From now on it lives in the Ledger, not in my notebook, and I'll move my scribbles across.

We spent a while on what to do with these people in the meantime, and ended up doing nothing, which I think is correct. A half-idea, that someone on the waitlist could walk to the nearest wave 1 pod and collect from there, went onto the parking lot. It raises too many questions about fobs and front doors to deal with this week. You can't just stroll into someone else's lobby, and I don't want any of our champions to become the person who buzzes strangers in.

## The number

Rasmus pulled up the Ledger: **41 boxes picked up last week.** The target is still 100. Some members took a skip week over the Easter holidays, so the week before Easter flattered us and last week didn't, but 41 is roughly where we are.

What struck us both, now that wave 1 has a name: if the waitlist can't be served until we decide anything about the next wave, then the road from 41 to 100 runs through these four lobbies. That's reasoning, not a plan, but it changed how we talked about the champions and the lobby walks for the rest of the morning. More doors in the buildings we already have, rather than more buildings.

## The shroud

Rasmus had photos on his laptop of the K-12 at Alder Court, taken last week from the lobby entrance. Plain grey metal front, the supplier's small sticker, a strip of our old Larder sticker that's already peeling at one corner, and nothing at all to tell someone walking past what it is. Raimo told me he's been asked twice whether it's a parcel locker, and once whether it's "something for the electricity". It doesn't help that from the front the compartment doors all look the same, chill cells and dry cell doors alike.

Rasmus had been calling the idea "the front skin", which I said sounded like a medical condition. I said "shroud": something that covers the whole front of the thing and gives it a face, like a cover over a machine. He made a face, then used it himself twice in the next five minutes, and that's how these things stick. We also didn't want to say "panel", because it's too close to the doors of the compartments and we'd end up talking past each other. So the shroud is the branded cover panel on the front of a pod.

We went back and forth on colour longer than we should have. Rasmus wanted something dark and serious; I wanted something people would notice from the stairwell. Green won for both reasons in the end: it reads as vegetables without any words, it stands out in lobbies that are mostly beige and grey, and a darker green doesn't look like a toy.

**Decision: the shroud is green with the Cropbox mark.** The mark itself doesn't exist yet. Rasmus is going to sketch something simple. The exact shade of green we left to the sketch; I don't want to choose a colour from a description.

Things we noted while talking, none of them decided:

- the shroud mustn't cover the compartment doors, the screen on the spine, or the hatch, or we've made the pod worse to make it prettier
- the dry cell doors could use small labels so members aren't peering at numbers, but that's a separate job
- we don't know yet what Kestrel lets us attach to the front of a leased unit; that's a question for Oskar at Kestrel, not something to guess

Small funny thing to end on: Raimo calls every OB he takes in "a Larder bag". He'll have to learn a new word, and I suspect he won't.
