> # Standard box pricing after moving packing to the Garage
>
> ## Summary
>
> The current standard box price of 27 € was set before packing moved to the Garage. Your costs now include a weekly pack line that the price was never built to carry. On the figures you gave me, the margin left to Larder per box is already thin once pod costs are counted, and the pack line pushes it below zero. I recommend a modest rise to **28 €**. The reasoning, the alternatives and the open points are below.
>
> ## 1. What the current price has to cover
>
> Figures I'm working from, as you gave them to me:
>
> - Standard box: 27 €; small box: 17 €
> - House cut: 12 % (grower share therefore 88 %)
> - Floor price: 18 € per standard box
> - Per pod, per month: lease 85 €, lobby insurance 6 €, lobby fee 40 €
> - Pods in the pilot: 6
> - Last week: 88 boxes picked up, pickup rate 91 %
> - North number target: 100 boxes picked up per week
>
> At 27 €, the house cut is 3.24 € per standard box and the grower share is 23.76 €. That is comfortably above the 18 € floor, so the floor is not what binds at this price.
>
> **Pod costs.** 85 + 6 + 40 = 131 € per pod per month. Across 6 pods that is 786 € a month, or roughly 181 € a week (using 4.33 weeks per month).
>
> **Boxes charged, not boxes picked up.** Because cards on file are charged on the drop day, income follows boxes dropped, not boxes collected. At a 91 % pickup rate, 88 boxes picked up implies about 97 boxes dropped (88 ÷ 0.91 ≈ 96.7).
>
> **Pod cost per box.** 181 € ÷ 97 ≈ 1.87 € per box. So of the 3.24 € house cut, about 1.87 € goes on pods alone, which leaves about 1.37 € for everything else: van hire on van day, fuel, make-goods, crates that don't come back, card fees and hosting. I don't have your van hire figure, so I have not put a number on it. Even a modest daily hire spread over about 97 boxes will take most of what remains.
>
> ## 2. Estimating the pack line
>
> These are modelled estimates, not measured costs. Please replace them with real figures when you have them.
>
> | Item | Weekly estimate | Basis |
> |---|---|---|
> | Labour | 60 € | 4 hours at a placeholder 15 €/hour |
> | Consumables (tape, labels, liners) | ~24 € | ~0.25 € per box × 97 |
> | Power for cold storage in the Garage | ~10 € | rough allowance |
> | Crate washing (water, detergent) | ~6 € | rough allowance |
> | **Total** | **~100 €** | ≈ 1.03 € per box at 97 boxes |
>
> Most of this cost scales with volume. At around 110 boxes dropped (roughly what a pickup target of 100 implies at the current rate), the per-box cost stays close to 1 €. The labour line is the weakest assumption. It treats the pack line as one person for four hours at a low rate. If it is really longer, or needs a second pair of hands, the cost per box rises quickly.
>
> ## 3. A structural point: the house cut is a percentage
>
> This matters as much as the price itself. If a price rise simply passes through the existing 12 % house cut, Larder keeps only 0.12 € of every extra euro and the growers receive 0.88 €. To raise about 1.03 € per box for packing entirely inside the percentage, the standard box would need to go up by around 8.60 €. That is not realistic.
>
> So a rise meant to pay for packing only does that job if the extra amount is kept apart from the share calculation. Two consequences follow:
>
> 1. **Check the GSA wording.** Whether the grower share is defined on the whole box price or on a base price decides whether this is possible without amending the agreements.
> 2. **Expect the growers to notice.** If the grower share stays at 23.76 € while the price rises, the share is no longer 88 % of what the member pays. The growers' circle recently pushed for a bigger share. Some growers may read a held share as a cut, even though their euros per box are unchanged. That conversation is better held before any announcement than after.
>
> The figures below assume the increase is kept for packing. If it is not, none of the options covers the pack line.
>
> ## 4. Options
>
> | Standard price | Change vs 27 € | Extra per box (kept for packing) | Extra per week at ~97 boxes | Covers modelled pack line (~100 €/week)? |
> |---|---|---|---|---|
> | 27 € | — | 0 € | 0 € | No |
> | 28 € | +3.7 % | 1.00 € | ~97 € | Roughly |
> | 29 € | +7.4 % | 2.00 € | ~194 € | Yes, with margin |
> | 30 € | +11.1 % | 3.00 € | ~291 € | Yes, with a large margin |
>
> ## 5. Member-side considerations
>
> 1. **This would be a second rise in about two months.** The move to 27 € was recent. Members tend to remember the last rise when judging the next one, and the step is more visible than its size.
> 2. **Pickup rate headroom.** You are at 91 % against a kill threshold of 85 % held for four weeks. A price rise acts mostly on sign-ups and skip weeks rather than on pickups. Still, a fall in the north number would make it harder to read whether the pilot is working. Six points of headroom is real, but it is not a cushion to spend lightly.
> 3. **Season pass holders.** A pass is 10 boxes for the price of 9: 243 € at 27 €, 252 € at 28 €, 261 € at 29 €. Members who have already paid in advance need a clear answer on what a change means for them before it is announced.
> 4. **Champion perk.** At 10 % off, a building champion pays 24.30 € today and would pay 25.20 € at 28 €. This is small, but champions are the people most likely to be asked about the rise, so they should hear about it first.
> 5. **Small box.** I have not modelled the small box. Packing labour per box is probably similar for both sizes, and the house cut on a 17 € box is only 2.04 €. The small box may carry its packing cost less well than the standard box does. The gap between the two sizes also widens from 10 € to 11 € at 28 €.
>
> ## 6. Recommendation: 28 €
>
> 1. **It roughly covers the modelled pack line at current volumes**, assuming the extra is kept for packing.
> 2. **It is the smallest visible rise that does the job.** At 3.7 % it is easier to explain right after the last rise than 7.4 % would be.
> 3. **It protects the north number.** It does the least to tempt members into skip weeks while the pilot is still being measured.
> 4. **It is easy to revisit.** Once you have measured several pack line evenings and are closer to 100 boxes picked up a week, you will know whether 28 € is enough. The weakness is that if the labour estimate turns out low, 28 € is too little and you would be raising the price again.
>
> 29 € is defensible if you believe the labour line is understated. It buys margin, but it asks more of members at a sensitive moment. 30 € looks hard to justify on these numbers.
>
> ## 7. What I would check before deciding
>
> - Actual pack line hours over the next few evenings, including crate handling
> - The GSA wording on how the grower share is calculated
> - Your real van day cost per dawn run, which I could not include
> - How season pass holders will be treated
> - Whether the small box needs looking at separately
>
> Happy to rerun this with measured figures once you have them.
