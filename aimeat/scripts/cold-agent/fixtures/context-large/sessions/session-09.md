# Larder · session 9 · 10 March 2026 · Payments and the honesty shelf

The first drop is in. At this morning's dawn run Ilona and I put 11 boxes into the Alder Court pod. Then we sat down at her kitchen table with wet coats over the radiator to work through the rest of the list. Some things got decided quickly because the morning had made them feel urgent. That is probably the most useful thing the first drop did for us besides proving the pod opens.

## The morning

"First drop" was Ilona's phrase. She wrote it in marker across the top of the loop sheet on Sunday, underlined twice, and we've both been saying it since as if it were a holiday. It has stuck as the name for today. There will be other drops, but there is only one first drop.

The van was booked for van day as planned, and we loaded it at the Garage. That is the other new name this week. Up to now it has been "Ilona's garage on Pier Road" or "your place". This morning, while we were stacking crates, she said "lock the Garage behind you", and the capital G was audible. It's where the crates, the spare parts box from Kestrel and now the paperwork live. I've started typing it that way in the Ledger notes, so it's official as far as I'm concerned.

Alder Court was the only stop. The sleet was coming sideways off the harbour and the van's wipers squeaked the whole way. Raimo met us at the door with his keys, which he didn't have to do. He also held the lobby door while we wheeled the crates in, which he also didn't have to do. That puts the caretaker question back on the table. We said so to each other in the van and then agreed not to reopen it today. It is still open.

The last metre went the way we'd rehearsed it. Hatch open, back-load into the cells by the numbers on the loop sheet, hatch shut. Then I watched the Ledger on my phone until each of the 11 compartments flipped to "loaded". All three chill cells were in use. The heartbeat came through on time. The spine screen showed the right box count. Nobody's door was opened from the front by us, which was the point.

Eleven is not much against an NN target of 100, and Ilona was the first to say so. I'm the one who has to keep saying it's fine: the north number is a weekly number for the whole pilot, and today was about whether the chain works at all. It did. The pickup window closes tomorrow morning. I don't want to report anything about pickups until it has, so the PUR for the first drop goes in next week's note.

Small thing for the record: Aino's box sheet came in on Sunday evening as a photo of a handwritten list. I typed it into the Ledger by hand. That's fine for now, and it goes on the parking lot as "box sheet as a form, someday".

## Payments

What pushed payments to the top of the list was the Ledger itself. After the drop it showed eleven boxes loaded and an empty payments table. We have been circling this since the first weeks without settling it. Having actual vegetables in actual cells made the gap embarrassing.

We went through the options we'd been half-discussing:

- Pay per box at pickup, as part of getting the wake code. It's tidy in theory, but it means a member can leave a box sitting there unpaid, and then we're chasing money for an orphan box.
- A monthly invoice. Ilona's view is that we'd become a billing office, and I agree.
- Prepaid credit in Picker. This came up and nobody argued for it very hard.
- Card on file.

**Decision: members pay in Picker with a card on file, charged on the drop day.**

The reasoning, roughly as we said it:

- A card on file is the normal thing people already know from every other subscription. Members save a card in Picker once and then don't think about it.
- Charging on the drop day ties the money to a box that actually went into a compartment. Not the day the order is placed, not the start of the box week. If there's no drop for someone, there's no charge. Ilona felt strongly about this. She doesn't want a member paying for a box that's still at the farm.
- It keeps payment out of the pod entirely. The pod opens a door when the Ledger sends a door pulse, and that's all it has to do.
- On my side it's one charge per member per drop day. The Ledger can record the grower share and the house cut against each box at the same moment, which makes the grower statements simple.

I'll build the card form in Picker and the drop-day charge run in the Ledger. It needs to be live before the next drop day. That's going on the red list, because without it there's nothing for the next drop's members to pay with.

Then Ilona raised a question I hadn't considered. Some of the people she talked to at Alder Court are older. She thinks a few of them won't want a card stored in an app at all, but would happily tap a card at the pod when they collect. So: should members be able to pay by card at the pod itself? I don't know. I don't know whether a K-12 spine can take a card reader. If it can, I don't know what that does to the lease, the power draw, or my weekends. And it would mean a second way of paying alongside the one we just chose, which is exactly the kind of thing that doubles the work. Ilona's point stands, though: card on file assumes a certain kind of member, and the bet is about everyone in the building. **Left open.** I'll ask Oskar what the spine supports so we're at least arguing from facts next time.

## The honesty shelf

This came from Aino, indirectly. When we picked up the boxes on Monday she had a few crates of leeks and some knobbly beetroot that weren't on anyone's box sheet. "Surplus," she called it. That's the growers' word, and Pekka uses it the same way: whatever the farm has beyond what the box sheets need. She asked, not entirely joking, whether we could "do something with it" instead of her taking it to the stall or the compost.

Ilona grew up near farms with roadside tables, the kind with a jar or a sign and no one watching. She said: "Put a shelf on top of the pod. An honesty shelf. People take a leek, pay with their phone." I laughed. Then neither of us could think of a good reason not to. The name was settled before the idea was, and that's usually a sign.

What we mean by it, as of today:

- An unlocked shelf on top of the pod. No compartment and no wake code; anyone in the lobby can take from it.
- Surplus from our growers goes on it.
- People pay by phone. My guess is a QR sticker that opens a page in Picker, with no account needed. I'll sketch it before we commit to anything.

**Decision: an honesty shelf is tried on the Alder Court pod.**

Why Alder Court: it's pod zero, it's the one we can actually visit, and Raimo is there and paying attention. Why try it at all: it gives growers somewhere to put surplus, it puts something in the lobby that non-members see every day, and it's cheap to stop if it doesn't work.

The worries, so they're on paper:

- Theft. Ilona's view is that a leek walking off is not a disaster, and we'll find out what kind of building it is.
- What the HA thinks. The lobby slot was agreed for a pod, not a market stall on top of one. Ilona will ask before anything goes up.
- What's safe on a dry shelf in a heated lobby. Root veg yes, salad no.
- Whether the shelf makes the spine screen harder to read. I haven't measured anything.

Also to the parking lot: should a member be able to add something from the shelf to their own box order? Probably not, and certainly not now.

## Investors

This was shorter than I expected. The question was whether we should be looking for outside money now, while there's a first drop to point to, to get more pods sooner. People have been asking Ilona at the markets whether we're "raising". I'll admit the word had been rattling around my head too.

**Decision: no outside investors during the pilot.**

We agreed on why without much argument:

- The pilot exists to test the bet. Outside money would come with someone else's opinion about how fast it should be proven, and we haven't proven it yet. Today we had eleven boxes in one pod.
- We want the north number to be the thing steering us, not someone else's milestones.
- It's cheaper in time. Every hour spent on a pitch deck is an hour not spent on Picker or on lobby walks.
- If the pilot works, we'll be in a much better position to have this conversation. If it doesn't, we won't owe anyone an explanation except each other and the growers.

Ilona said, "Let's not borrow our own enthusiasm back at interest." I'm writing it down because it's the best line of the day.

## Food premises

We first raised this on 10 February: does Larder need a food-premises registration? It has been sitting open since. Today the honesty shelf decided it for us. Boxes packed and sealed at the farm and moved straight to a cell is one thing. Loose surplus going onto a shelf, plus crates sitting in the Garage overnight between farm pickup and the dawn run, is food handling by any reasonable reading.

Ilona went through the city food authority's pages over coffee and read the definition out loud. "Food premises" is their term for any place registered with them for handling food. The Garage is that place for us, whatever else it is.

**Decision: Ilona registers the Garage as a food premises.** She filled in the city's online form before we finished and sent it. **The open question from 10 February is now settled: yes, Larder needs one, and the Garage is registered.**

Neither of us knows yet what, if anything, the authority will want to come and look at. Ilona will keep whatever comes back in the Garage folder. I said the registration makes the honesty shelf feel more legitimate. She said it makes the Garage feel less like a garage, which I think she meant as a compliment to it.
