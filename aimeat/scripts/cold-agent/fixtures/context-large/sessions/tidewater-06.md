# Tidewater (board game) · playtest 6 · 4 October 2026 · Towards a print run

We met at my place again, the last one of this round of six. Grey Sunday, rain on and off, Pauli brought cinnamon buns and Venla brought the sketchbook, which turned out to matter. I'd planned to play one more full game and then talk about what comes next, and that's roughly how it went. The game part was short on surprises, which is what I wanted. The talk part ran long.

## The game

We played a three-player game with the rules as they stand now: no changes since last time. The dawn run did its job again. Everyone was out of the hub and heading somewhere useful before the first loop was over, and nobody sat staring at the board. Pauli went hard for beacons early and held the champion for most of the middle game. That meant Pauli also had the pilot through that stretch. Venla grumbled about that, but it's the point of tying the two together. Venla took the champion back late, mostly by grinding cargo cards through the long lanes while Pauli was busy building.

A few things I noticed, none of them big:

- The heartbeat felt right. Nobody looked up at the end and said "already?" or "finally". We came in around the hour, which is where I want it.
- The reef at one cargo card is still fine. People pass it when it's worth it and go around when it isn't, which is the choice I want them making.
- The swap card got played once. Venla used it right before the heartbeat filled to grab the one card they needed for a beacon. Pauli called it "legal theft". I'm keeping the phrase.
- The ledger is still a mess to read at the end. That's a layout problem, not a rules problem. I've written it on the parking lot for the print version below.

Nobody had a rules complaint I'd act on. Pauli asked again whether there should be a way to see the whole storm deck's contents before the game. I said no, same as before, and we left it there. After six sessions I think the rules are as done as they'll get without strangers playing it. That's what pushed the rest of the afternoon.

## Towards a print run

The idea of getting real copies made has been floating around since about the fourth session. This time we actually talked it through. The thing that made it concrete is the Lakeport game fair on 21 November. Small designers can show things there, and people actually sit down and play rather than just walking past tables. Pauli has been before and says the crowd is the right one for something like Tidewater: people who will play a game for an hour with strangers and tell you honestly what they think.

**Decision: we're doing a print run of 50 copies for the Lakeport game fair on 21 November.**

How we got to that:

- Strangers. Everything so far has been the three of us, plus my sister's family that one time. We know the game too well. I'd rather hear that the dawn run is confusing from someone who's never seen it than keep guessing.
- Fifty felt like the honest middle. Fewer than that and it's basically a few nicer prototypes, and I'd be paying setup costs for very little. Much more and I'd end up with boxes in the hallway until next summer. Venla's line was "fifty is enough to be embarrassing if nobody wants them, and few enough to survive it", which is about right.
- A fixed date stops us fiddling. If there's no deadline I'll keep tweaking the reef forever. With the fair there, the rules freeze, and anything new goes on the parking lot for later.
- Something to hand people. Pauli made the point that at a fair, people who liked the game will want to take one home. Having nothing to give them would waste the best moment we'll get.

We did not talk money in any real way. I haven't asked anyone for prices yet, and I'm not going to guess in these notes. If the quotes come back silly, we talk again. But the plan is fifty for the fair.

The timing worries me a bit. Seven weeks sounds like a lot until you count back from the fair: proofs, corrections, delivery. Pauli thinks it's fine if we stop changing things now. I think Pauli is more optimistic about printers than printers deserve. We agreed I'll find out what lead time actually looks like before anyone panics.

What has to be print-ready, roughly:

- Board, harbour tiles, fog banks, the reef ring, the hub
- Cargo cards, the storm deck, the swap cards
- Captain sheets
- Pod pieces, ship pieces, the pilot, gull tokens, beacons, the champion token
- A ledger pad, laid out so you can actually read the final scores
- The rulebook, which right now is my notes plus arrows

The rulebook is the bit I'm most nervous about. It's one thing to teach the game across the table. It's another for someone to open a box at a fair and get through the dawn run without me standing behind them. I'll write a clean draft. Then Pauli, who is the best of us at finding holes, reads it pretending never to have seen the game.

## Box art

This is where it got interesting, and where we didn't land anywhere.

Venla opened the sketchbook halfway through the second coffee. There were a few drawings in it: a ship nosing through a fog bank, some whales, a lighthouse that's obviously a beacon. They're good. They have the right feel, a bit cold and salty, not cartoonish. Pauli said straight away that's the box. Venla said "maybe", which I've learned means "I haven't decided and don't push me".

**Open question: will Venla draw the box art, or do we hire someone?**

What came up on each side:

- For Venla doing it: the drawings already look like Tidewater, and Venla knows the game better than any outside artist would. It would also feel like ours, which matters to me more than I expected.
- Against: it's a lot of work on a short clock, on top of Venla's actual life. A box front isn't the same job as a sketch. There's lettering, the back of the box, the spine. Venla said, fairly, that drawing for fun and drawing to a deadline are different things, and they don't want to hate the game by November.
- For hiring someone: it takes the pressure off Venla, and someone who does boxes for a living knows things none of us do.
- Against: we don't know anyone, we don't know what it costs, and a stranger has to learn the game from scratch on a short timeline.

There was also a half-idea of splitting the job: Venla's drawing with someone else doing the layout and lettering. We didn't go anywhere with it. It's on the parking lot along with everything else.

We left it open. Venla wants a few days to think about whether they actually want to take it on. I said there's no wrong answer, and I meant it. I'll quietly find out what hiring someone would involve, so that if Venla says no we're not starting from zero.
