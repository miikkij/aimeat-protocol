Final Boss 1 — The Aberration Problem

LIÉNARD-WIECHERT POTENTIALS AND THE 8-MINUTE LAG

The Problem, Precisely

Earth orbits the Sun at v ≈ 30 km/s. Light takes τ ≈ 499 seconds to travel from Sun to Earth. In that time, the Sun has "moved" (from Earth's perspective) by:

Δx = v × τ ≈ 30 × 499 ≈ 15,000 km

If the push-wave tunnel pointed at the Sun's retarded position (where it was 499 seconds ago), the force would be misaligned by an angle:

θ_aberration = v/c = 30/300,000 = 10⁻⁴ radians ≈ 20 arcseconds

This would create a tangential force component that adds energy to Earth's orbit. The orbit would expand and the Earth would spiral outward in roughly 10,000 years. This is what Laplace calculated in 1805, concluding gravity must propagate at least 10⁷ times faster than light.

The Solution: How Moving Sources Project Forward

In electromagnetism, a charge moving at constant velocity v produces an electric field given by the Liénard-Wiechert potential. The field does NOT point toward the retarded position. It points toward the extrapolated current position.

The exact field of a uniformly moving charge:

E⃗ = (q / 4πε₀) × (1 - β²) × (r⃗ - r⃗_ret) / [(r⃗ - r⃗_ret)·(r⃗ - r⃗_ret) - (v⃗×(r⃗ - r⃗_ret))²/c²]^(3/2)

This looks complicated, but the key result is simple: for a uniformly moving source, this expression exactly equals the Coulomb field pointing toward where the charge is right now. The velocity-dependent terms encode information about the source's motion, and by the time the field reaches the observer, it has "projected forward" to the correct current position.

How Does This Work Mechanically?

Consider the Sun emitting a push-wave at time t₀ from position x₀. The wave expands spherically at speed c. But the Sun is moving at velocity v, so the wavefront is not centered on x₀ — it's compressed in the forward direction (like a Doppler shift) and expanded in the backward direction.

When this asymmetric wavefront arrives at Earth, the direction of maximum intensity is not from the retarded position — it's shifted forward by exactly the right amount. The wave carries the Sun's velocity encoded in its asymmetry.

The critical requirement: the push-waves must obey the relativistic wave equation:

□ψ = (1/c²)(∂²ψ/∂t²) - ∇²ψ = 0

This is the d'Alembertian. Any wave obeying this equation from a moving source automatically produces the Liénard-Wiechert field structure. The aberration cancellation is not a special trick — it's a mathematical consequence of the wave equation being Lorentz-covariant.

Final Boss 2 — Galactic Rotation Curves

CAN THE WAVE MEDIUM REPLACE DARK MATTER?

The Problem

Stars in the outer regions of galaxies orbit too fast. Newtonian gravity with visible mass predicts velocity should drop as 1/√r beyond the main stellar disk. Instead, rotation curves are flat — velocity stays roughly constant out to the farthest observable distances.

The Push-Wave Prediction

Every star in the galaxy radiates push-waves. These waves extend far beyond the visible disk. The total wave intensity at any point is the superposition of all stellar contributions. This creates a wave medium density profile.

The Calculation

For an exponential disk galaxy with surface stellar density:

Σ(R) = Σ₀ × exp(-R / R_d)

where R_d is the disk scale length (~3 kpc for the Milky Way).

Each star at position R' radiates push-waves with intensity falling as 1/r² from the star. The total wave medium density at radius r from the galactic center:

ρ_wave(r) = ∫∫ Σ(R') × A² / |r⃗ - r⃗'|² d²r'

For r much larger than the disk (r >> R_d), all N_total stars appear as a central point source:

ρ_wave(r) ≈ N_total × A² / r²    (for r >> R_d)

This is an isothermal density profile — exactly what dark matter halos look like!

The enclosed "gravitational mass" from the wave medium within radius r:

M_wave(r) = ∫₀ʳ 4πr'² × ρ_wave(r') dr' = 4π × N × A² × ∫₀ʳ dr' = 4πNA² × r

M_wave grows linearly with r. This is the critical result.

The orbital velocity from this linear mass profile:

v(r) = √(G × M_wave(r) / r) = √(G × 4πNA² × r / r) = √(4πGNA²) = constant

Flat rotation curve. No dark matter particles. Just the accumulated push-wave medium of visible stars creating an effective mass that grows linearly with distance.

The Quantitative Test

The flat velocity depends on only one thing: the total number (and amplitude) of stars in the galaxy. This gives us the Baryonic Tully-Fisher Relation:

v⁴_flat ∝ M_baryonic

This is one of the tightest empirical correlations in astronomy. In dark matter models, it's a coincidence — there's no reason the dark matter halo mass should correlate so perfectly with visible mass. In the push-wave model, it's inevitable — the wave medium IS produced by the visible stars. More stars → more waves → more medium → higher flat velocity. The correlation is built in.
