THE PHYSICS

In General Relativity, Mercury's orbit precesses because gravity near the Sun deviates slightly from pure 1/r². The GR correction to Newtonian gravity is:

F = GM/r² × (1 + 3GM/r c²)

The extra term 3GM/rc² is tiny — about 2.5×10⁻⁸ at Mercury's orbit — but over millions of orbits it rotates the perihelion by 43 arcseconds per century.

THE PUSH-WAVE INTERPRETATION

In the push-wave framework, what does this correction term mean physically?

Near the Sun, the push-wave medium is denser. A denser medium means:

1. Push-waves propagate differently through the dense region

2. The cancellation tunnel between Sun and Mercury operates in a denser medium

3. Denser medium → slightly stronger cancellation → slightly stronger force than pure 1/r²

The wave medium density at distance r from the Sun scales as:

ρ_wave(r) = ρ₀ + k × M_sun / r

The extra force from operating in this denser medium gives a correction:

F_correction = α × 3GM²/(r³c²)

where α is the medium coupling constant. If α = 1, we get exactly the GR prediction.

THE TEST

The slider controls α. At α = 0: pure Newton, no precession. At α = 1: GR-equivalent precession = 43 arcsec/century. The question is: does the push-wave framework naturally require α = 1?

The argument: if the tunnel operates through a medium of density ρ ∝ M/r, and the force enhancement is proportional to the medium density, then the coupling is exactly:

ΔF/F = 3GM/rc² → α = 1 naturally

The factor 3 comes from the three spatial dimensions through which the wave medium acts. This is identical to GR's result but with a physical mechanism: the tunnel is slightly stronger in denser wave-space.
