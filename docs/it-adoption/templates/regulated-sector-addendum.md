Answer each `[ORGANIZATION]` field in your own process; a row that does not apply is marked "n/a" with the reason. ## 2. GAMP 5 software category

GAMP 5 is the ISPE guide for a risk-based approach to computerised systems, and it sorts software into categories, among them infrastructure software (1), non-configured products (3), configured products (4) and custom applications (5) ([ISPE, GAMP 5 Guide 2nd Edition](https://ispe.org/publications/guidance-documents/gamp-5-guide-2nd-edition)). The category is your decision. These are the facts that bear on it:

| The AIMEAT node as installed | Behaviour is set through environment variables and runtime settings, without code changes (`aimeat/.env.example`, `aimeat/src/services/config/schema.ts`) | Configured product (4) | [ORGANIZATION] *Category, and the configuration you put under change control.* |
| Apps, extensions, workflows and agents built on the node | Written by people or their AI and published to the node; extensions run in a sandbox (`aimeat/src/services/extension-runtime.ts`) | Custom application (5) | [ORGANIZATION] *Category per app or extension used in the regulated process, or "not used in the regulated process".* |
| AI model behaviour | Answers come from an outside or local model the operator chose; the node records which model answered ([AI transparency](../../ai-transparency.md)) | Not covered by the categories alone | [ORGANIZATION] *How your process treats model output that a person relies on.* |

## 3. Electronic records and electronic signatures

The requirements below are quoted or summarised from the official texts: US FDA [21 CFR Part 11](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-21/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-11) and [EU GMP Annex 11, Computerised Systems](https://health.ec.europa.eu/system/files/2016-11/annex11_01-2011_en_0.pdf). Part 11 applies to records that an FDA predicate rule requires; Annex 11 to computerised systems used in GMP-regulated activities. Read the gap column as "what you must add or decide", never as a claim of compliance.

| Requirement (official source) | What AIMEAT has (source) | What AIMEAT lacks or leaves to you | Your assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Validation.** Part 11 §11.10(a): validation of systems to ensure accuracy, reliability, consistent intended performance, and the ability to discern invalid or altered records. Annex 11 §4: validation documentation covering the life cycle, user requirements, test evidence | Automated test suites in the repository on both database backends ([testing requirements](../../coding-guidelines/testing-requirements.md)); a guard tier of security tests run after every push ([verification matrix](../../security/verification-matrix.md)) | No validation package: no user requirements specification, no IQ, OQ or PQ protocols, no traceability matrix. Project tests are evidence about the code, not validation of your installation for your intended use | [ORGANIZATION] *Validation plan reference, or n/a.* |
| **Audit trail.** Part 11 §11.10(e): secure, computer-generated, time-stamped audit trails that record operator entries and actions that create, modify or delete records; changes shall not obscure earlier information; retained at least as long as the records. Annex 11 §9: a record of all GMP-relevant changes and deletions, with the reason for change or deletion documented; §12.4: record the identity of operators entering, changing, confirming or deleting data, with date and time | Partial trails: a workspace row keeps its first author and its latest writer, and keeps up to 20 earlier versions for the space's retention or 30 days when a writer of another owner replaces it; a change by the same owner or that owner's agents and apps keeps none, and a removed row cannot be restored (`aimeat_workspace_rows_revisions`, `aimeat/src/services/workspace-rows/row-revisions.ts`); a deleted memory record stays restorable for a grace window (`AIMEAT_MEMORY_DELETE_GRACE_DAYS`); the classification audit records shown, used, refused, changed and excepted reads when classification is on ([data classification](../../data-classification.md)); an operator's access to another person's account and a change to a node setting leave a record (`aimeat/src/services/operator-access-audit.ts`); app audit logs (`AIMEAT_APP_AUDIT_KEEP`); AI provenance records for generated content ([AI transparency](../../ai-transparency.md)) | No single audit trail of every create, modify and delete of every record, with the earlier value kept for the record's whole retention period. No field for the reason of a change. No hash chain or other tamper evidence on audit records was found in the node's source code | [ORGANIZATION] *Whether the partial trails are enough for your risk, or which records stay out of the node.* |
| **Authority checks.** Part 11 §11.10(g): only authorised individuals can use the system, sign a record, alter a record or perform the operation. Annex 11 §12.1 to 12.3: access restricted to authorised persons; creation, change and cancellation of access authorisations recorded | Identity per person, agent and app; permissions (scopes) checked on REST, MCP and CLI ([threat model](../../security/threat-model.md)); single sign-on and SCIM ([organisation node sign-in](../../organisation-node-sign-in.md)); an agent's added permissions are shown on the owner's account feed (`aimeat/src/services/agent/scope-add.ts`) | A complete, reviewable record of every grant and removal of the operator role and of every permission change was not verified for this addendum | [ORGANIZATION] *Your access-control procedure and review interval.* |
| **Signature manifestation.** Part 11 §11.50(a): a signed record shows the printed name of the signer, the date and time of signing, and the meaning of the signature (such as review, approval, responsibility or authorship); §11.50(b): these are part of any human-readable form | Each AIMEAT signature seals the document's SHA-256, the signer's identity and display name, the time, the method (passkey, key, session or EU wallet) and an assurance level, signed with the node key (`aimeat/src/services/docsign/statement.ts`); anyone holding the document can look up its signatures by hash ([document signing](../../document-signing.md)) | The sealed statement has no field for the meaning of the signature; the request's title and message are free text. Whether the display name meets "printed name" is your call | [ORGANIZATION] *How the meaning is recorded, e.g. a fixed wording in the request title, and whether that is acceptable.* |
| **Signature and record linking.** Part 11 §11.70: signatures linked to their records so they cannot be excised, copied or transferred to falsify a record. Annex 11 §14: signatures permanently linked to their record, with time and date | The signature covers the document's SHA-256 and the node's Ed25519 seal covers the statement; the seal is checkable with the node's published key (`aimeat/src/services/docsign/statement.ts`) | The link is to a document's bytes. A change to the document gives a different hash, so the earlier signature no longer matches it; keeping the signed version is your procedure | [ORGANIZATION] *Your assessment.* |
| **Signature controls.** Part 11 §§11.100 to 11.300: unique signatures, identity verification before assignment, two distinct identification components for signatures not based on biometrics, controls on identification codes and passwords, and a certification to the FDA | Passkey signing uses a WebAuthn assertion bound to the request; key signing uses the person's registered key; session signing rests only on the authenticated session and the node's seal, and the code calls it the weakest method (`aimeat/src/services/docsign/statement.ts`). An AIMEAT signature is described in the code as an electronic signature, not a qualified one | Identity verification before an account is created, the FDA certification, and the rules for which method is allowed in a regulated process are yours | [ORGANIZATION] *Allowed methods, e.g. passkey only.* |
| **Data storage and backup.** Annex 11 §7: data secured against damage, accessible through the retention period; regular backups, with integrity and restore checked | Backup and restore procedures for both databases, exercised by `aimeat/test/e2e-backup-restore.ts` ([backup and restore](../../operations/backup-and-restore.md)) | Retention periods, archive format and periodic restore checks are yours | [ORGANIZATION] *Procedure reference.* |
| **Change and configuration management.** Annex 11 §10: changes to the system and its configuration made in a controlled manner under a defined procedure | Updates are stop, install, start, with migrations applied automatically and rollback by restoring a backup ([upgrading](../../operations/upgrading.md)); many settings can also be changed at runtime from the admin Config page (`aimeat/src/services/config/schema.ts`) | Runtime setting changes bypass your change process unless you restrict who holds the operator role or pin settings read-only (`AIMEAT_SEALED_CONFIG_KEYS`) | [ORGANIZATION] *Change procedure and which settings you pin.* |
